\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

The model is now applied to the cathode and anode regions of a high-intensity arc in argon at atmospheric pressure, with an arc current of 100--200~A. Experiments and LTE models of such arcs \cite{Lowke2006,Hsu1983,LiangTrelles,lago2004numerical,freton2000comparison,korsipati2026fast,haddad1984temperature} give the state of the plasma near the electrodes. Together with the scale estimates of Section~\ref{sec:scales involved} and previous studies \cite{Almeida2008,DinulescuPfender1980,benilov1995model,hsu1983analysis,tashiro2010numerical,semenov2016numerical}, a distance of \(5\times10^{-4}\)~m from the electrodes was considered to be a reasonable choice for the existence of LTE conditions. At this distance, \(x=L\), we take \(T_{\rm bulk}=21\,000\)~K and \(J_{\rm app}=10^{8}\ \mathrm{A\,m^{-2}}\) for the cathode, and \(T_{\rm bulk}=14\,000\)~K and \(|J_{\rm app}|=10^{7}\ \mathrm{A\,m^{-2}}\) for the anode (table~\ref{tab:base}). Unlike in the validation cases of Section~\ref{sec:validation}, the radial loss~(\ref{eq:radial-loss}) is included, with the reactive conductivity in the electron channel. The calibration~(\ref{eq:radial-calibration}) gives a column radius \(R=3.4\)~mm for the anode, which is typical of a diffuse anode attachment \cite{Hsu1983,Freton2012,korsipati2026fast}, and \(0.51\)~mm for the cathode, which is typical of the radius of a cathode spot \cite{Hsu1983,LiangTrelles}. At 14\,000~K, \(\kappa_R=0.93\ \mathrm{W\,m^{-1}\,K^{-1}}\) is comparable to \(\kappa_e=1.07\ \mathrm{W\,m^{-1}\,K^{-1}}\), whereas at 21\,000~K the plasma is almost fully ionized and \(\kappa_R=0.03\ \mathrm{W\,m^{-1}\,K^{-1}}\) is negligible. At \(x=L\), all densities and temperatures are Dirichlet values (Section~\ref{sec:model-bc}). The electrode materials used in the LTE results are copper for the anode and thoriated tungsten for the cathode; these materials are therefore adopted here. The anode has a work function of \(4.65~\mathrm{eV}\) and is maintained at a uniform prescribed surface temperature of \(T_{\rm wall}=1000~\mathrm{K}\). The cathode surface temperature is determined by the heat balance in Eq.~(\ref{eq:bc-heat-balance}). The cathode is assumed to have uniform properties \(k_w=100~\mathrm{W\,m^{-1}\,K^{-1}}\), \(L_w=1~\mathrm{cm}\), \(T_0=1000~\mathrm{K}\), \(A_R=3\times10^4~\mathrm{A\,m^{-2}\,K^{-2}}\), and \(W=2.63~\mathrm{eV}\). The computational mesh is identical to that described in Section~\ref{sec:numerics}. For both steady-state solutions, the residual of the electron energy equation, Eq.~(\ref{eq:eenergy}), quantified by \(\partial\mathcal{E}/\partial t\), remains below \(1.2\times10^{-3}\) of the largest-magnitude term in the equation throughout the domain. The total current density is uniform to within a relative variation of \(10^{-8}\).

\subsection{Structure of the near-electrode layers}
\label{sec:res-structure}


\emph{Cathode.} A space-charge sheath \(0.58\ \mu\)m thick forms at the surface. In it, \(n_i\) exceeds \(n_e\), by up to a factor of 3.1 at the surface (figure~\ref{fig:base-profiles}(a)), and across it the potential rises by 3.6~V. The field at the surface is \(1.8\times10^7\ \mathrm{V\,m^{-1}}\). Beyond the sheath, the potential keeps rising through the quasi-neutral ionization layer, to 8.3~V at \(x=10^{-4}\)~m (figure~\ref{fig:base-profiles}(e)). Further out, the plasma approaches the bulk state, and the remaining rise to 12.5~V at \(x=L\) is mostly the ohmic drop of the column, whose field is \(9.5\times10^3\ \mathrm{V\,m^{-1}}\). The electron temperature is nearly uniform across the whole layer, 18.2--21~kK, while the heavy-particle temperature rises from the surface value of 3.7~kK and approaches \(T_e\) only at \(x\approx10^{-4}\)~m (figure~\ref{fig:base-profiles}(c)). Over the same distance, the plasma becomes almost fully ionized. Close to the cathode, the charged-particle density is far above the equilibrium density \(n_{\rm eq}(T_h)\) that the reactions would give at the local heavy-particle temperature (figure~\ref{fig:base-profiles}(a)), by a factor of 400 at \(10\ \mu\)m. The two densities come within a factor of 2 of each other beyond \(33\ \mu\)m, and within 1\,\% only beyond \(0.2\)~mm, where \(T_h\) has approached \(T_e\). The emitted electron flux, \(1.03\times10^{27}\ \mathrm{m^{-2}\,s^{-1}}\), corresponds to a current density of \(1.65\times10^8\ \mathrm{A\,m^{-2}}\), 1.65 times the arc current density. It is partly offset by the plasma electrons that reach the surface against the sheath field, \(4.4\times10^{26}\ \mathrm{m^{-2}\,s^{-1}}\) (table~\ref{tab:base}), and the ions carry 5.4\,\% of the current at the surface (figure~\ref{fig:base-profiles}(g)).

\emph{Anode.} The space-charge sheath is practically absent. The charge separation exceeds 1\,\% only within \(0.80\ \mu\)m of the surface, across which the potential changes by only 0.60~V, and at the surface \(n_i\) exceeds \(n_e\) by 18\,\% (figure~\ref{fig:base-profiles}(b)). The anode lies 3.43~V below the bulk-plasma boundary, and this drop is distributed over the whole quasi-neutral layer (figure~\ref{fig:base-profiles}(f)): 1.43~V within \(10\ \mu\)m and 2.27~V within \(100\ \mu\)m. The field is directed towards the anode and retards the electrons, which reach the surface by diffusion down the steep density gradient: across the layer, the charged-particle density falls by a factor of 270, from \(1.5\times10^{23}\) to \(5.7\times10^{20}\ \mathrm{m^{-3}}\). These densities exceed the equilibrium density \(n_{\rm eq}(T_h)\) everywhere (figure~\ref{fig:base-profiles}(b)): by a factor of 90 at \(100\ \mu\)m, 3.2 at \(200\ \mu\)m and 1.4 at \(300\ \mu\)m, while closer to the anode \(n_{\rm eq}(T_h)\) is negligible. The anode layer is over-populated with charged particles, which recombine, and \(n_e\) is within 10\,\% of \(n_{\rm eq}(T_h)\) only within \(0.1\)~mm of \(x=L\). The electron and heavy-particle temperatures decrease together towards the anode, but \(T_h\) falls faster, to the prescribed surface temperature of 1~kK, so that the two separate progressively: by 1.6~kK at \(x=10^{-4}\)~m, 3.5~kK at \(x=10^{-5}\)~m and 4.1~kK at the surface, where \(T_e=5.1\)~kK (figure~\ref{fig:base-profiles}(d)). The ions flow into the anode, \(j_i/j<0\), and carry only 0.7\,\% of the current at the surface (figure~\ref{fig:base-profiles}(h)).

\subsection{Electron energy balance}
\label{sec:res-energy}

Figure~\ref{fig:base-budget} shows every term of the steady electron energy equation~(\ref{eq:eenergy}). The divergence of the electron energy flux~(\ref{eq:heatflux-e}), \(-\nabla\cdot\mathbf{h}_e\), is plotted as one term: it combines the enthalpy flux, conduction and the thermal-diffusion heat flux. The volume terms are Joule heating \(-e\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_e\cdot\mathbf{E}\), elastic exchange with the heavy particles, the ionization term \(-\varepsilon_{\rm ion}S\), radiation \(-w_{\rm rad}\) and the radial loss \(-Q_e\)~(\ref{eq:radial-loss}). Positive terms heat the electrons, and at steady state the terms sum to zero. Integrated over \(x\), each term gives a power per unit area, quoted below in \(\mathrm{MW\,m^{-2}}\). The balance is very different at the two electrodes. At the cathode, Joule heating is the source, and the electron energy flux carries most of it away. At the anode, the electron energy flux is the source, and the Joule term is a sink.

\emph{Cathode.} Joule heating is the only source of electron energy, and it is the largest term everywhere (figure~\ref{fig:base-budget}(a)). It peaks at the surface, \(1.7\times10^{15}\ \mathrm{W\,m^{-3}}\), where the sheath field accelerates the emitted electrons, and falls by more than two orders of magnitude within \(10\ \mu\)m. Over this distance it is balanced almost entirely by \(-\nabla\cdot\mathbf{h}_e\), which takes up \(590\) of the \(615\ \mathrm{MW\,m^{-2}}\) released; in the sheath alone, the two are \(340\) and \(-339\ \mathrm{MW\,m^{-2}}\). The volume losses there are three to four orders of magnitude smaller. The Joule heat released near the surface is therefore not lost locally: the electron energy flux carries it to the cathode and towards the bulk. Beyond \(10\ \mu\)m, \(-\nabla\cdot\mathbf{h}_e\) is small, and the Joule heating, now \(10^{12}\)--\(10^{13}\ \mathrm{W\,m^{-3}}\), is balanced by the volume terms. Ionization is the largest of them out to \(100\ \mu\)m, with a peak of \(2.0\times10^{12}\ \mathrm{W\,m^{-3}}\) at \(6\ \mu\)m. The elastic exchange peaks at \(6.6\times10^{11}\ \mathrm{W\,m^{-3}}\) at \(20\ \mu\)m, where it heats the heavy particles (figure~\ref{fig:base-profiles}(c)). Both terms become negligible beyond \(200\ \mu\)m, where \(T_h\) has approached \(T_e\) and the plasma is close to ionization equilibrium. Between 28 and \(58\ \mu\)m, \(-\nabla\cdot\mathbf{h}_e\) is positive, up to \(1.6\times10^{11}\ \mathrm{W\,m^{-3}}\). There, \(T_e\) has a shallow minimum, 18.60~kK at \(30\ \mu\)m, between a maximum of 18.63~kK at \(11\ \mu\)m and the rise towards the bulk, and the electron energy flux converges into it. In the column part of the domain, \(x>100\ \mu\)m, the radial loss and, to a lesser extent, radiation take up the Joule heating. At \(x=L\), they balance it to within 0.4\,\% (\(8.4\times10^{11}\) and \(1.1\times10^{11}\) against \(9.5\times10^{11}\ \mathrm{W\,m^{-3}}\)), as the calibration~(\ref{eq:radial-calibration}) requires, and \(-\nabla\cdot\mathbf{h}_e\) falls almost to zero. Over the whole domain, Joule heating supplies \(1210\ \mathrm{MW\,m^{-2}}\), of which \(686\ \mathrm{MW\,m^{-2}}\) leave the domain as electron energy flux: \(569\ \mathrm{MW\,m^{-2}}\) into the column at \(x=L\), and \(117\ \mathrm{MW\,m^{-2}}\) into the cathode with the absorbed plasma electrons, net of the energy of the emitted electrons. The radial loss takes \(362\ \mathrm{MW\,m^{-2}}\), most of it at \(x>100\ \mu\)m, and ionization \(85\ \mathrm{MW\,m^{-2}}\), two thirds of it between 10 and \(100\ \mu\)m. Radiation and elastic exchange take \(41\) and \(40\ \mathrm{MW\,m^{-2}}\).

\emph{Anode.} The Joule term is a loss throughout the domain (figure~\ref{fig:base-budget}(b)), because the electrons flow towards the anode against the retarding field. Its magnitude is largest at the surface, \(1.5\times10^{13}\ \mathrm{W\,m^{-3}}\). Even at \(x=L\) it is still negative, \(-1.3\times10^{10}\ \mathrm{W\,m^{-3}}\), whereas the ohmic heating of the column at the imposed bulk state is \(J^2/\sigma=+1.6\times10^{10}\ \mathrm{W\,m^{-3}}\): the retarding field of the anode layer extends over the whole domain. The only source of electron energy is \(-\nabla\cdot\mathbf{h}_e\), which is positive everywhere, so the electron energy flux from the column converges over the whole domain. Of the \(48.2\ \mathrm{MW\,m^{-2}}\) that enter at \(x=L\), only \(8.8\ \mathrm{MW\,m^{-2}}\) reach the surface. Within \(10\ \mu\)m of the anode, \(-\nabla\cdot\mathbf{h}_e\) and the Joule loss balance each other to within 0.1\,\% (\(14.4\ \mathrm{MW\,m^{-2}}\) each), and at the surface the volume terms are five orders of magnitude smaller. Further from the anode, the Joule loss decreases to a few \(10^{10}\ \mathrm{W\,m^{-3}}\), and the volume terms become comparable to it. The elastic loss to the heavy particles peaks at \(2.7\times10^{10}\ \mathrm{W\,m^{-3}}\) at \(79\ \mu\)m, where \(T_e-T_h\) is still 1.6~kK. The ionization term heats the electrons, with a peak of \(1.3\times10^{10}\ \mathrm{W\,m^{-3}}\) at \(243\ \mu\)m: the plasma recombines, \(S<0\), because the charged particles are supplied by diffusion at densities well above the local ionization equilibrium (Section~\ref{sec:val-anode}), and the recombination energy is returned to the electrons. Radiation and the radial loss grow towards the bulk, and at \(x=L\) they reach \(5.4\times10^{9}\) and \(9.4\times10^{9}\ \mathrm{W\,m^{-3}}\). Over the whole domain, the \(39.4\ \mathrm{MW\,m^{-2}}\) supplied by \(-\nabla\cdot\mathbf{h}_e\) and the \(4.9\ \mathrm{MW\,m^{-2}}\) of recombination heating cover the Joule loss (\(34.8\ \mathrm{MW\,m^{-2}}\)), the elastic loss (\(6.7\ \mathrm{MW\,m^{-2}}\)), the radial loss (\(2.2\ \mathrm{MW\,m^{-2}}\)) and radiation (\(0.7\ \mathrm{MW\,m^{-2}}\)). Within \(5\ \mu\)m of \(x=L\), the ionization term changes sign. This is a property of the boundary condition there: the densities are held at \(n_{\rm eq}(T_{\rm bulk})\), while \(T_e\) in the adjacent cells exceeds \(T_{\rm bulk}\) by up to 3~K, so that the plasma is slightly below equilibrium and ionizes. The resulting sink is at most \(4.4\times10^{8}\ \mathrm{W\,m^{-3}}\), 3\,\% of the peak recombination heating.

\subsection{Momentum balance and collisionality of the sheaths}
\label{sec:res-momentum}
% Figures and numbers: figures/results_fig/base_sheath.py (output in base_sheath_report.txt), same plotfiles.

The fluxes~(\ref{eq:flux-e}) and (\ref{eq:flux-i}) follow from the force balances~(\ref{eq:mom-e}) and (\ref{eq:mom-i}), in which the inertia of each species relative to the heavy gas is neglected. Figure~\ref{fig:base-momentum} shows the terms of these balances in the two steady states near the electrode: the electric force, the pressure gradient, the two collisional drags combined and the thermal force. In the steady states the heavy gas is at rest (\(|\mathbf{u}|\le3\times10^{-4}\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\)), so the share \(Y_s\mathbf{F}\) of the net force is negligible (below \(2\times10^{-4}\) of the largest term). For comparison, the figure also shows the inertia \(m_sn_s(\mathbf{u}_s\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}_s\) that the model neglects, evaluated a posteriori from the computed fluxes. The retained terms sum to zero to within \(3\times10^{-3}\) of the largest one.

\emph{Ions.} In both sheaths, the electric force on the ions is balanced by the drag (figure~\ref{fig:base-momentum}(a), (b)). At the cathode, the pressure gradient adds at most 23\,\% to the electric force in the sheath, and 8\,\% at the surface. At the anode, the field is weaker, and the pressure gradient, which also drives the ions towards the surface, is 37\,\% of the electric force there. The thermal force on the ions is negligible, below 1\,\% of the drag in either sheath. The neglected inertia is not small inside the cathode sheath: it exceeds the drag within \(0.33\ \mu\)m of the surface, by a factor of 2.2 at the surface, and stays above 10\,\% of the drag out to \(2.1\ \mu\)m; beyond \(12\ \mu\)m it is below 1\,\%. At the anode, the inertia is at most 52\,\% of the drag (at the surface), and below 10\,\% beyond \(0.17\ \mu\)m.

\emph{Electrons.} In both sheaths the field retards the electrons, and the electric force on them is balanced by their pressure gradient (figure~\ref{fig:base-momentum}(c), (d)). In the sheath the two cancel to within 0.8\,\% (cathode) and 3.1\,\% (anode) of the electric force. The drag (0.6 and 1.5\,\% of the electric force at the surface) and the thermal force (1.1 and 0.3\,\%) are small there. The drag becomes important only in the quasi-neutral layer, where it carries the current: at \(10\ \mu\)m from the surface it is 14\,\% (cathode) and 18\,\% (anode) of the electric force. The inertia of the electrons exceeds their drag within \(0.34\ \mu\)m of the cathode (by up to a factor of 44) and within \(0.45\ \mu\)m of the anode (by up to a factor of 10), but it remains smaller than the electric force: at the surface it is a quarter of the electric force at the cathode and 16\,\% of it at the anode, and at the sheath edge 0.3 and 1.0\,\%. At the cathode, this inertia is that of the emitted electrons, accelerated by the sheath field.

\emph{Mean free paths.} Figure~\ref{fig:base-sheath}(a), (b) shows, evaluated with the local densities and temperatures, the mean free paths \(\lambda_e\) and \(\lambda_i\) of table~\ref{tab:scales}, the length \(\lambda_{T_h}=1/[(n_i+n_n)\overline{Q}_{in}]\) over which ions and atoms exchange energy, and the electron Debye length. The electron mean free path is of order microns: \(3.9\ \mu\)m at the cathode sheath edge and \(3.6\ \mu\)m at the anode one. The sheaths, \(0.58\) and \(0.80\ \mu\)m thick, therefore contain only 0.13 and 0.18 electron mean free paths, and \(\lambda_e\) exceeds the distance from the surface within \(2.3\ \mu\)m of either electrode. The ions are more collisional. At the sheath edge, \(\lambda_i=0.29\ \mu\)m at the cathode and \(0.10\ \mu\)m at the anode, so that the ions cross 1.8 and 7.7 mean free paths in the sheaths (1.1 and 6.7 counting only the collisions with atoms). The anode sheath is thus collision-dominated for the ions, consistent with their small inertia. The cathode sheath is transitional: the ions make one to two collisions across it, and their inertia exceeds the drag over its inner half. There the ions cannot follow the steep rise of the field, and the local relation between field and ion velocity in equation~(\ref{eq:flux-i}) overestimates the ion velocity near the surface. The electrons cross both sheaths practically without collisions. Their balance there is, however, not one of collisional transport: the drag is a few per cent of the electric force, and the field and the pressure gradient balance each other as in the Boltzmann equilibrium of a Maxwellian population retarded by a potential barrier, which does not require collisions. The electron description therefore remains adequate in the sheaths, except within about \(0.1\ \mu\)m of the cathode, where the inertia of the emitted electrons is no longer small. Beyond 2--3~\(\mu\)m from either electrode, the inertia of both species is below 10\,\% of the drag, and the inertialess, collision-dominated description of Section~\ref{sec:model-governing} holds.

\emph{A single heavy-particle temperature.} The model describes the ions and the atoms by one temperature \(T_h\). This requires, first, that the two species exchange energy over a distance short compared with the length \(L_{T_h}=T_h/|\nabla T_h|\) over which \(T_h\) changes. This holds in both layers: \(\lambda_{T_h}/L_{T_h}\) is at most 0.10 at the cathode (at the surface) and 0.021 at the anode. Within about one \(\lambda_{T_h}\) of the surface (\(0.5\ \mu\)m at the cathode and \(0.1\ \mu\)m at the anode), the heavy particles are nevertheless not in local equilibrium with the wall, and the boundary condition \(T_h(0)=T_{\rm wall}\) neglects a temperature jump of order \(\lambda_{T_h}|\nabla T_h|\), about 0.36~kK at the cathode and 21~K at the anode. Second, the ions must not gain energy from the field faster than they share it with the atoms. At the anode, this condition is satisfied except next to the surface: the effective ion temperature \(T_{\rm eff}\) of the two-temperature closure (Appendix~\ref{app:transport}) exceeds \(T_h\) by 35\,\% at the surface, but by more than 10\,\% only within \(0.34\ \mu\)m of it. In the cathode sheath the condition is not satisfied. The energy \(eE\lambda_{T_h}\) that an ion gains from the field between collisions is 8~eV at the surface, against \(k_BT_h=0.3\)~eV, and \(T_{\rm eff}\) exceeds \(T_h\) by a factor of 1.8 at the sheath edge and of 10.6 at the surface; it is more than twice \(T_h\) within \(0.48\ \mu\)m of the surface, and more than 10\,\% above it within \(2.1\ \mu\)m. The random energy of the ions there is not \(k_BT_h\). The model retains this heating in the ion mobility, through \(T_{\rm eff}\), but not in the ion pressure, which is evaluated with \(T_h\). Evaluated with \(T_{\rm eff}\) instead, the ion pressure gradient would reach 46\,\% of the electric force in the cathode sheath, instead of 23\,\%. The ion flux in the inner part of the cathode sheath is therefore uncertain to this extent, in addition to the effect of the neglected inertia. Beyond about \(2\ \mu\)m from the cathode and \(0.3\ \mu\)m from the anode, a single heavy-particle temperature is adequate.

\emph{Boltzmann electrons and the Bohm criterion.} Simplified sheath models describe the electrons in the sheath by the Boltzmann relation and impose the Bohm criterion on the ions entering it, \(u_i\ge u_B\), with \(u_B=\sqrt{k_B(T_e+T_i)/M}\). Figure~\ref{fig:base-sheath}(c)--(f) tests both against the computed sheaths. The electron density follows the Boltzmann relation closely. Started at the sheath edge, the relation reproduces \(n_e\) to within 1.6\,\% across the cathode sheath, where \(n_e\) falls by a factor of 9, and to within 1.5\,\% across the anode sheath, where it falls by a factor of 3.9. Outside the anode sheath, it still holds to within 4.2\,\% up to \(10\ \mu\)m from the surface, but \(n_e\) is 12\,\% below \(n_B\) at \(30\ \mu\)m and 19\,\% below at \(100\ \mu\)m. At the cathode, it fails progressively in the ionization layer, where the electron drag carries the current: \(n_e\) is 9\,\% below \(n_B\) at \(10\ \mu\)m and 53\,\% below at \(100\ \mu\)m. The ions, in contrast, do not satisfy the Bohm criterion at the sheath edge (figure~\ref{fig:base-sheath}(e), (f)). At the cathode they enter the sheath at \(u_i=1690\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\), against \(u_B=2160\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\), i.e.\ at 0.78\,\(u_B\). They reach \(u_B\) only \(0.39\ \mu\)m from the surface, inside the sheath, where the charge separation is already 3.4\,\%, and arrive at the surface at 2.4\,\(u_B\). At the anode, the ions enter the sheath at 0.17\,\(u_B\) and reach the surface at 0.57\,\(u_B\), never attaining \(u_B\). The Bohm flux \(n_iu_B\) at the sheath edge would therefore overestimate the ion flux entering the sheath by 28\,\% at the cathode and by a factor of 6 at the anode. The Bohm criterion is derived for a collisionless sheath. In these sheaths the ions collide with the atoms throughout the presheath and the sheath, and, except in the inner part of the cathode sheath, their velocity is set by the balance of the electric force and the drag rather than by the potential drop through which they have fallen. This agrees with the conclusion of Semenov et al.\ \cite{semenov2016numerical} for the anode layer.

\subsection{Heat flux to the electrode}
\label{sec:res-heat}

The terms of the electrode heat flux~(\ref{eq:bc-qwall}) are shown in figure~\ref{fig:base-qwall}. At the cathode, the heat flux is the small difference of large terms. The absorbed plasma electrons (\(408\ \mathrm{MW\,m^{-2}}\)), gas conduction (\(88\ \mathrm{MW\,m^{-2}}\)) and ion recombination (\(70\ \mathrm{MW\,m^{-2}}\)) heat the surface by \(566\ \mathrm{MW\,m^{-2}}\) in total, and emission cools it by \(540\ \mathrm{MW\,m^{-2}}\), so that only \(q_w=26.7\ \mathrm{MW\,m^{-2}}\) is conducted into the cathode body. The surface temperature, 3670~K, is therefore fixed mainly by the condition that emission cooling nearly balances the heating, and only weakly by the conduction into the cathode body. Emission cooling grows with \(T_{\rm wall}\) as \(T_{\rm wall}^2\exp[-(W-\Delta W)/(k_BT_{\rm wall})]\), i.e.\ by about 0.27\,\% per kelvin at this temperature, so a 5\,\% change in the heating would shift \(T_{\rm wall}\) by only about 20~K, and \(q_w\) by less than 1\,\%. Ion recombination, at \(\varepsilon_{\rm ion}-W=13.1\)~eV per ion, provides 12\,\% of the heating, although the ions carry only 5.4\,\% of the current.

At the anode, \(q_w=65.7\ \mathrm{MW\,m^{-2}}\), and the absorbed electrons deliver 85\,\% of it: 46.8~\(\mathrm{MW\,m^{-2}}\) as the work function released on entering the metal and 8.8~\(\mathrm{MW\,m^{-2}}\) as kinetic energy. Gas conduction contributes \(9.2\ \mathrm{MW\,m^{-2}}\) and ion recombination 1.2\,\%. The anode heat flux is thus set by the electron current, through \(W\), and by the electron temperature at the surface. Since the surface temperature is prescribed, this is the heat that the cooling of the anode has to remove.

\emph{Ion impact energy.} Equation~(\ref{eq:bc-qwall}) charges the ions with their recombination energy only, not with the kinetic energy with which they strike the surface. In the model, the ion energy follows from the local field through the two-temperature closure (Appendix~\ref{app:transport}), which presumes that the ions make many collisions over the distance in which the field changes. The cathode sheath does not satisfy this: the ions cross it with only one to two collisions, and their inertia exceeds the drag over its inner part (Section~\ref{sec:res-momentum}). The local mean ion energy at the cathode surface is therefore not a reliable impact energy, and its distribution would require a kinetic treatment of the sheath. We therefore give only the maximum impact energy at the cathode: that of an ion that enters the sheath with the mean energy at the sheath edge, \(\tfrac32k_BT_{\rm eff}+\tfrac12Mv_d^2=1.3\)~eV (equations~(\ref{eq:wannier}) and (\ref{eq:mu-in})), and crosses it without a collision, gaining \(e\phi_{\rm s}=3.6\)~eV. This maximum is 4.9~eV per ion, and with the ion flux of table~\ref{tab:base} it delivers at most \(26\ \mathrm{MW\,m^{-2}}\), comparable to \(q_w\) itself. Added to the heating, however, this term would raise \(T_{\rm wall}\) by only about 20~K, since most of it would be removed by emission cooling, and \(q_w\) by less than 1\,\%. At the anode, where the ions carry 0.7\,\% of the current and fall through only 0.60~V in a collision-dominated sheath, their kinetic energy is negligible.

\emph{Cathode.} The heat flux to the cathode hardly changes: \(q_w=26.3\)--\(27.3\ \mathrm{MW\,m^{-2}}\) in every case (figures~\ref{fig:sweep-T}(a) and \ref{fig:sweep-J}(a)), while \(T_{\rm wall}\) varies only between 3633 and 3726~K. This follows from the heat balance~(\ref{eq:bc-heat-balance}). With the electrode parameters used here, a change of \(q_w\) by \(1\ \mathrm{MW\,m^{-2}}\) requires a change of \(T_{\rm wall}\) by 100~K, which would change the emission cooling by about 30\,\%. The heating of the surface by the absorbed electrons, the ions and gas conduction does depend on the plasma: it increases from 488 to \(735\ \mathrm{MW\,m^{-2}}\) between \(J_{\rm app}=8\times10^{7}\) and \(1.5\times10^{8}\ \mathrm{A\,m^{-2}}\). The surface temperature then rises by only 93~K, and the additional heating is removed by emission cooling. Within these ranges, the heat flux conducted into the cathode is therefore set by the electrode, through \(W\), \(A_R\), \(k_w\), \(L_w\) and \(T_0\), rather than by the plasma. The plasma adjusts instead. With increasing current density, the sheath voltage rises from 3.1 to 5.0~V and the ion fraction of the current at the surface from 5.0 to 6.2\,\% (figure~\ref{fig:sweep-J}). The electron temperature at the surface rises from 16.7 to 21.4~kK, and the voltage across the domain from 11.4 to 15.4~V. The emitted current exceeds the arc current in every case, but by less as the current grows, from 1.77 to 1.44 times the arc current. Above \(1.5\times10^{8}\ \mathrm{A\,m^{-2}}\), no steady state was reached with this cathode heat balance. Increasing the bulk temperature at fixed current acts on the sheath in the opposite direction (figure~\ref{fig:sweep-T}). Between 19\,000 and 23\,000~K, \(\phi_{\rm s}\) decreases from 4.1 to 3.2~V, \(j_i/j\) from 5.8 to 5.1\,\% and \(T_e\) at the surface from 18.9 to 17.6~kK, while the voltage across the domain, \(\phi(L)\), falls from 13.1 to 12.1~V. The sheath width changes by less than 10\,\% in either sweep (figures~\ref{fig:sweep-T}(g) and \ref{fig:sweep-J}(g)): it decreases from 0.60 to \(0.55\ \mu\)m as the current density increases, and from 0.59 to \(0.57\ \mu\)m as the bulk temperature increases. The larger sheath voltage at higher current is sustained across a nearly unchanged width by a denser ion space charge: the ion density at the surface rises from \(5.5\times10^{21}\) to \(9.2\times10^{21}\ \mathrm{m^{-3}}\).

\emph{Anode.} The anode heat flux increases almost in proportion to the current density, from 48.8 to \(126\ \mathrm{MW\,m^{-2}}\) between \(|J_{\rm app}|=7\times10^{6}\) and \(2\times10^{7}\ \mathrm{A\,m^{-2}}\) (figure~\ref{fig:sweep-J}(b)), i.e.\ from 7.0 to 6.3~W per ampere. Of this, the work function released by the absorbed electrons contributes \(W|J_{\rm app}|/e\) to within 1\,\%, i.e.\ 4.7~W per ampere. Their kinetic energy, \(2k_BT_e\) per electron with \(T_e\) at the surface between 5.1 and 5.7~kK, contributes another 0.9--1.0~W per ampere. Gas conduction (9.0--\(11.1\ \mathrm{MW\,m^{-2}}\)) and ion recombination (below \(1.3\ \mathrm{MW\,m^{-2}}\)) contribute the rest; at the lower current densities, gas conduction makes up a larger share of \(q_w\), which is why the heat flux per ampere falls with the current. At fixed current, the bulk temperature has a much weaker effect: \(q_w\) rises from 60.4 to \(65.7\ \mathrm{MW\,m^{-2}}\) between 11\,000 and 14\,000~K (figure~\ref{fig:sweep-T}(b)). The electron terms are fixed by the current and by \(T_e\) at the surface, which stays between 5.0 and 5.3~kK, and the increase comes mostly from gas conduction, which rises from 4.4 to \(9.2\ \mathrm{MW\,m^{-2}}\). In every anode case, the ions carry less than 1\,\% of the current. The anode lies below the bulk-plasma boundary by \(\phi(L)=1.4\) to 3.4~V as \(T_{\rm bulk}\) increases from 11\,000 to 14\,000~K. With increasing current density this drop decreases, from 4.0~V at \(7\times10^{6}\) to 2.2~V at \(2\times10^{7}\ \mathrm{A\,m^{-2}}\). The potential at the edge of the thin space-charge layer stays between 0.23 and 0.75~V. The width of this layer decreases with the electron density at the surface. It decreases from 0.90 to \(0.63\ \mu\)m as the current density increases from \(7\times10^{6}\) to \(2\times10^{7}\ \mathrm{A\,m^{-2}}\) (figure~\ref{fig:sweep-J}(h)), while \(n_e\) at the surface rises from \(4.0\times10^{20}\) to \(1.1\times10^{21}\ \mathrm{m^{-3}}\): somewhat more slowly than \(n_e^{-1/2}\), the scaling of the Debye length. At fixed current, \(n_e\) at the surface changes by at most 4\,\% with the bulk temperature, and the width stays between 0.72 and \(0.81\ \mu\)m (figure~\ref{fig:sweep-T}(h)).

\label{sec:res-time}

To estimate how quickly the near-electrode layers follow a change of the arc, each steady state of Sections~\ref{sec:res-structure}--\ref{sec:res-sweep} was perturbed by a step of \(+2\,\%\) in \(|J_{\rm app}|\), and the time-dependent equations were integrated to the new steady state. The surface temperature and the radial-loss coefficient \(\Lambda\) were held at their steady values, since the electrode body responds on a time scale of seconds and \(\Lambda\) represents the column outside the domain. The response of the ion current density \(j_i\) and of the heat flux \(q_w\) at the surface is characterized by the time to steady state \(t_{99}\), after which the quantity stays within 1\,\% of its total change.

In a one-dimensional domain, the step also excites an axial acoustic oscillation of the heavy gas between the electrode and the boundary \(x=L\). In a three-dimensional arc, such a pressure disturbance is relieved radially within \(R/c\approx0.1\ \mu\)s. The axial acoustics are therefore removed by multiplying the inertia of the heavy gas, the left-hand side of equation~(\ref{eq:heavy-momentum}), by \(\epsilon=10^{-4}\). This raises the sound speed to \(c/\sqrt{\epsilon}\) and leaves the steady state, with \(\mathbf{u}=0\), unchanged; \(t_{99}\) varies by less than 3\,\% for \(10^{-6}\le\epsilon\le10^{-3}\). At the anode, the acoustics do not affect the response, and the physical sound speed is retained.

\emph{Cathode.} The response has two stages (figure~\ref{fig:timescales}(a)). About 14\,\% of the change of \(j_i\) occurs within 10~ps; the rest follows within about \(1.5\ \mu\)s, \(t_{99}=1.40\ \mu\)s, the final approach being exponential with a relaxation time of \(0.39\ \mu\)s. The heat flux first moves in the opposite direction and settles later, \(t_{99}=1.78\ \mu\)s. The cathode layer responds faster at higher current density: between \(8\times10^{7}\) and \(1.5\times10^{8}\ \mathrm{A\,m^{-2}}\), \(t_{99}\) falls from 2.1 to 0.62~\(\mu\)s for \(j_i\) and from 2.6 to 0.89~\(\mu\)s for \(q_w\) (figure~\ref{fig:timescales}(c)). The bulk temperature changes \(t_{99}\) by less than 15\,\% between 19\,000 and 23\,000~K (figure~\ref{fig:timescales}(e)).

\emph{Anode.} The anode layer relaxes about 24 times more slowly, with a relaxation time of \(9.3\ \mu\)s, and its two quantities behave differently (figure~\ref{fig:timescales}(b)). Because the dominant electron terms of the heat flux~(\ref{eq:bc-qwall}) are carried by the current (Section~\ref{sec:res-heat}), 92\,\% of the change of \(q_w\) occurs within 1~ns, and \(t_{99}=14\ \mu\)s. The ion current density first moves in the opposite direction and needs \(t_{99}=41\ \mu\)s. For \(j_i\), \(t_{99}\) decreases from 77 to 41~\(\mu\)s as \(T_{\rm bulk}\) rises from 11\,000 to 14\,000~K, and only from 46 to 41~\(\mu\)s over the current range; that of \(q_w\) stays between 9 and \(16\ \mu\)s in all cases (figure~\ref{fig:timescales}(d), (f)).

The cathode layer can therefore be treated as quasi-steady for changes of the arc slower than a few microseconds. At the anode, the heat flux follows the current within about \(15\ \mu\)s, but the ion current needs 40--80~\(\mu\)s.
