\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

The model is now applied to the cathode and anode regions of a high-intensity arc in argon at atmospheric pressure, with an arc current of 100--200~A. Experiments and LTE models of such arcs \cite{Lowke2006,Hsu1983,LiangTrelles,lago2004numerical,freton2000comparison,korsipati2026fast,haddad1984temperature} give the state of the plasma near the electrodes. Together with the scale estimates of Section~\ref{sec:scales involved} and previous studies \cite{Almeida2008,DinulescuPfender1980,benilov1995model,hsu1983analysis,tashiro2010numerical,semenov2016numerical}, a distance of \(5\times10^{-4}\)~m from the electrodes was considered to be a reasonable choice for the existence of LTE conditions. At this distance, \(x=L\), we take \(T_{\rm bulk}=21\,000\)~K and \(J_{\rm app}=10^{8}\ \mathrm{A\,m^{-2}}\) for the cathode, and \(T_{\rm bulk}=15\,000\)~K and \(|J_{\rm app}|=4\times10^{6}\ \mathrm{A\,m^{-2}}\) for the anode (table~\ref{tab:base}). Unlike in the validation cases of Section~\ref{sec:validation}, the radial loss~(\ref{eq:radial-loss}) is included, with \(T_{\rm edge}=1000\)~K and the reactive conductivity in the electron channel, for an assumed column radius at each electrode. At the cathode, where the arc attaches in a small spot, \(R=0.5\)~mm, which is typical of the radius of a cathode spot \cite{Hsu1983,LiangTrelles}. At 21\,000~K the plasma is almost fully ionized, and \(\kappa_R=0.03\ \mathrm{W\,m^{-1}\,K^{-1}}\) is negligible against \(\kappa_e=2.6\ \mathrm{W\,m^{-1}\,K^{-1}}\). With this radius the column balance~(\ref{eq:column-balance}) holds at the cathode bulk state to within 0.7\,\%. At the anode, where the attachment is wider, \(R=3.5\)~mm. At 15\,000~K the reactive conductivity is not negligible, \(\kappa_R=0.88\) against \(\kappa_e=1.27\ \mathrm{W\,m^{-1}\,K^{-1}}\), and \(\kappa_h=0.12\ \mathrm{W\,m^{-1}\,K^{-1}}\). At this current density, however, the anode bulk state does not satisfy the column balance for any radius: radiation alone is 4.5 times the Joule heating \(J_{\rm app}^2/\sigma\), and the radial loss adds another 4.6 times. The plasma therefore cools from the imposed bulk state across the domain (Section~\ref{sec:res-structure}). At \(x=L\), all densities and temperatures are Dirichlet values (Section~\ref{sec:model-bc}). The electrode materials used in the LTE results are copper for the anode and thoriated tungsten for the cathode; these materials are therefore adopted here. The anode has a work function of \(4.65~\mathrm{eV}\) and is maintained at a uniform prescribed surface temperature of \(T_{\rm wall}=1000~\mathrm{K}\). The cathode surface temperature is determined by the heat balance in Eq.~(\ref{eq:bc-heat-balance}). The cathode is assumed to have uniform properties \(k_w=100~\mathrm{W\,m^{-1}\,K^{-1}}\), \(L_w=1~\mathrm{cm}\), \(T_0=1000~\mathrm{K}\), \(A_R=3\times10^4~\mathrm{A\,m^{-2}\,K^{-2}}\), and \(W=2.63~\mathrm{eV}\). The computational mesh is identical to that described in Section~\ref{sec:numerics}. For both steady-state solutions, the residual of the electron energy equation, Eq.~(\ref{eq:eenergy}), quantified by \(\partial\mathcal{E}/\partial t\), remains below \(1.5\times10^{-3}\) of the largest-magnitude term in the equation throughout the domain. The total current density is uniform to within a relative variation of \(6.2\times10^{-9}\).

\subsection{Structure of the near-electrode layers}
\label{sec:res-structure}


\emph{Cathode.} A space-charge sheath \(0.58\ \mu\)m thick forms at the surface. In it, \(n_i\) exceeds \(n_e\), by up to a factor of 3.1 at the surface (figure~\ref{fig:base-profiles}(a)), and across it the potential rises by 3.6~V. The field at the surface is \(1.8\times10^7\ \mathrm{V\,m^{-1}}\). Beyond the sheath, the potential keeps rising through the quasi-neutral ionization layer, to 8.3~V at \(x=10^{-4}\)~m (figure~\ref{fig:base-profiles}(e)). Further out, the plasma approaches the bulk state, and the remaining rise to 12.5~V at \(x=L\) is mostly the ohmic drop of the column, whose field is \(9.7\times10^3\ \mathrm{V\,m^{-1}}\). The electron temperature is nearly uniform across the whole layer, 18.2--21~kK, while the heavy-particle temperature rises from the surface value of 3.7~kK and approaches \(T_e\) only at \(x\approx10^{-4}\)~m (figure~\ref{fig:base-profiles}(c)). Over the same distance, the plasma becomes almost fully ionized. Close to the cathode, the charged-particle density is far above the equilibrium density \(n_{\rm eq}(T_h)\) that the reactions would give at the local heavy-particle temperature (figure~\ref{fig:base-profiles}(a)), by a factor of 390 at \(10\ \mu\)m. The two densities come within a factor of 2 of each other beyond \(33\ \mu\)m, and within 1\,\% only beyond \(0.2\)~mm, where \(T_h\) has approached \(T_e\). The emitted electron flux, \(1.03\times10^{27}\ \mathrm{m^{-2}\,s^{-1}}\), corresponds to a current density of \(1.65\times10^8\ \mathrm{A\,m^{-2}}\), 1.65 times the arc current density. It is partly offset by the plasma electrons that reach the surface against the sheath field, \(4.4\times10^{26}\ \mathrm{m^{-2}\,s^{-1}}\) (table~\ref{tab:base}), and the ions carry 5.4\,\% of the current at the surface (figure~\ref{fig:base-profiles}(g)).

\emph{Anode.} The space-charge sheath is thin. The charge separation exceeds 1\,\% only within \(1.0\ \mu\)m of the surface, across which the potential changes by 1.37~V. In this sheath the ions are in excess, and at the surface \(n_i\) exceeds \(n_e\) by a factor of 2.9 (figure~\ref{fig:base-profiles}(b)). The anode lies 5.21~V below the bulk-plasma boundary, and most of this drop is distributed over the quasi-neutral layer (figure~\ref{fig:base-profiles}(f)): 2.36~V within \(10\ \mu\)m and 3.42~V within \(100\ \mu\)m. The field is directed towards the anode and retards the electrons, which reach the surface by diffusion down the steep density gradient: across the layer, the charged-particle density falls by a factor of 860, from \(1.8\times10^{23}\) to \(2.1\times10^{20}\ \mathrm{m^{-3}}\). The charged-particle densities exceed the equilibrium density \(n_{\rm eq}(T_h)\) (figure~\ref{fig:base-profiles}(b)): by a factor of 34 at \(100\ \mu\)m, 2.4 at \(200\ \mu\)m and 1.2 at \(300\ \mu\)m, while closer to the anode \(n_{\rm eq}(T_h)\) is negligible. The anode layer is over-populated with charged particles, which recombine, and \(n_e\) is within 10\,\% of \(n_{\rm eq}(T_h)\) only within \(0.15\)~mm of \(x=L\). The electron and heavy-particle temperatures decrease together towards the anode, but \(T_h\) falls faster, to the prescribed surface temperature of 1~kK, so that the two separate progressively: by 1.3~kK at \(x=10^{-4}\)~m, 4.2~kK at \(x=10^{-5}\)~m and 5.1~kK at the surface, where \(T_e=6.1\)~kK (figure~\ref{fig:base-profiles}(d)). The ions flow into the anode, \(j_i/j<0\), and carry 2.7\,\% of the current at the surface; their share grows towards the bulk plasma, to 13\,\% near \(x=L\) (figure~\ref{fig:base-profiles}(h)).

\subsection{Electron energy balance}
\label{sec:res-energy}

Figure~\ref{fig:base-budget} shows every term of the steady electron energy equation~(\ref{eq:eenergy}). The divergence of the electron energy flux~(\ref{eq:heatflux-e}), \(-\nabla\cdot\mathbf{h}_e\), is plotted as one term: it combines the enthalpy flux, conduction and the thermal-diffusion heat flux. The volume terms are Joule heating \(-e\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_e\cdot\mathbf{E}\), elastic exchange with the heavy particles, the ionization term \(-\varepsilon_{\rm ion}S\), radiation \(-w_{\rm rad}\) and the radial loss \(-Q_e\)~(\ref{eq:radial-loss}). Positive terms heat the electrons, and at steady state the terms sum to zero. Local values are quoted in \(\mathrm{W\,m^{-3}}\), as in the figure. Where a term is integrated over a part of the domain, the integral is given as a fraction of the energy supplied to the electrons over the whole domain. The balance is very different at the two electrodes.

\emph{Cathode.} Joule heating is the only source of electron energy, and it is the largest term everywhere (figure~\ref{fig:base-budget}(a)). It peaks at the surface, \(1.7\times10^{15}\ \mathrm{W\,m^{-3}}\), where the sheath field accelerates the emitted electrons, and falls by more than two orders of magnitude within \(10\ \mu\)m. Over this distance it is balanced almost entirely by \(-\nabla\cdot\mathbf{h}_e\), which takes up 96\,\% of the Joule heat released there; in the sheath alone, the two cancel to within 0.1\,\%. The volume losses there are three to four orders of magnitude smaller. The Joule heat released near the surface is therefore not lost locally: the electron energy flux carries it to the cathode and towards the bulk. Beyond \(10\ \mu\)m, \(-\nabla\cdot\mathbf{h}_e\) is small, and the Joule heating, now \(10^{12}\)--\(10^{13}\ \mathrm{W\,m^{-3}}\), is balanced by the volume terms. Ionization is the largest of them out to \(100\ \mu\)m, with a peak of \(2.0\times10^{12}\ \mathrm{W\,m^{-3}}\) at \(6\ \mu\)m. The elastic exchange peaks at \(6.6\times10^{11}\ \mathrm{W\,m^{-3}}\) at \(20\ \mu\)m, where it heats the heavy particles (figure~\ref{fig:base-profiles}(c)). Both terms become negligible beyond \(200\ \mu\)m, where \(T_h\) has approached \(T_e\) and the plasma is close to ionization equilibrium. In the column part of the domain, \(x>100\ \mu\)m, the radial loss and, to a lesser extent, radiation take up the Joule heating. Of the Joule heat released over the whole domain, 56\,\% leaves the domain as electron energy flux: 47\,\% into the column at \(x=L\), and 9.6\,\% into the cathode with the absorbed plasma electrons, net of the energy of the emitted electrons. The radial loss takes 30\,\%, most of it at \(x>100\ \mu\)m, and ionization 7\,\%, two thirds of it between 10 and \(100\ \mu\)m. Radiation and elastic exchange take 3.3\,\% each.

\emph{Anode.} The Joule term is a loss throughout the domain (figure~\ref{fig:base-budget}(b)), because the electrons flow towards the anode against the retarding field. Its magnitude is largest at the surface, \(1.5\times10^{13}\ \mathrm{W\,m^{-3}}\). Apart from recombination, the only source of electron energy is \(-\nabla\cdot\mathbf{h}_e\), which is positive everywhere, so the electron energy flux from the column converges over the whole domain. Only 14\,\% of the electron energy flux that enters at \(x=L\) reaches the surface. Within \(10\ \mu\)m of the anode, \(-\nabla\cdot\mathbf{h}_e\) and the Joule loss balance each other to within 0.3\,\%, and account for 44\,\% of the Joule loss over the domain; at the surface the volume terms are five orders of magnitude smaller. Further from the anode, the Joule loss decreases to about \(10^{10}\ \mathrm{W\,m^{-3}}\), and the volume terms become comparable to it. The elastic loss to the heavy particles peaks at \(3.6\times10^{10}\ \mathrm{W\,m^{-3}}\) at \(57\ \mu\)m, where \(T_e-T_h\) is still 2.2~kK. The ionization term heats the electrons, with a peak of \(2.0\times10^{10}\ \mathrm{W\,m^{-3}}\) at \(204\ \mu\)m: the plasma recombines, \(S<0\), because the charged particles are supplied by diffusion at densities well above the local ionization equilibrium (Section~\ref{sec:val-anode}), and the recombination energy is returned to the electrons. Only within \(19\ \mu\)m of \(x=L\), where \(n_e\) is within 0.1\,\% of \(n_{\rm eq}(T_h)\), is it a weak sink, at most \(6.4\times10^{8}\ \mathrm{W\,m^{-3}}\). Radiation and the radial loss grow towards the bulk, and at \(x=L\) they reach \(1.0\times10^{10}\) and \(9.8\times10^{9}\ \mathrm{W\,m^{-3}}\), together twice the Joule loss there, \(9.7\times10^{9}\ \mathrm{W\,m^{-3}}\): since the bulk state does not satisfy the column balance~(\ref{eq:column-balance}), these losses of the bulk plasma are supplied by the electron energy flux from \(x=L\). Over the whole domain, \(-\nabla\cdot\mathbf{h}_e\) supplies 80\,\% of the electron energy and recombination heating 20\,\%; of this, the Joule loss takes 66\,\%, the elastic loss 23\,\%, the radial loss 7.4\,\% and radiation 3.9\,\%.

\subsection{Momentum balance and collisionality of the sheaths}
\label{sec:res-momentum}
% Figures and numbers: figures/results_fig/base_sheath.py (output in base_sheath_report.txt), same plotfiles.

The fluxes~(\ref{eq:flux-e}) and (\ref{eq:flux-i}) follow from the force balances~(\ref{eq:mom-e}) and (\ref{eq:mom-i}), in which the inertia of each species relative to the heavy gas is neglected. Figure~\ref{fig:base-momentum} shows the terms of these balances in the two steady states near the electrode: the electric force, the pressure gradient, the two collisional drags combined and the thermal force. In the steady states the heavy gas is at rest (\(|\mathbf{u}|\le3\times10^{-4}\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\)), so the share \(Y_s\mathbf{F}\) of the net force is negligible (below \(2\times10^{-4}\) of the largest term). For comparison, the figure also shows the inertia \(m_sn_s(\mathbf{u}_s\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}_s\) that the model neglects, evaluated a posteriori from the computed fluxes. The retained terms sum to zero to within \(4\times10^{-3}\) of the largest one.

\emph{Ions.} In both sheaths, the electric force on the ions is balanced by the drag (figure~\ref{fig:base-momentum}(a), (b)). At the cathode, the pressure gradient adds at most 23\,\% to the electric force in the sheath, and 8\,\% at the surface. At the anode, the field is weaker, and the pressure gradient, which also drives the ions towards the surface, is 18\,\% of the electric force at the surface and at most 23\,\% in the sheath. The thermal force on the ions is negligible, below 1\,\% of the drag in either sheath. The neglected inertia is not small inside the cathode sheath: it exceeds the drag within \(0.33\ \mu\)m of the surface, by a factor of 2.2 at the surface, and stays above 10\,\% of the drag out to \(2.1\ \mu\)m; beyond \(12\ \mu\)m it is below 1\,\%. At the anode, the inertia is at most 78\,\% of the drag (at the surface), and below 10\,\% beyond \(0.47\ \mu\)m.

\emph{Electrons.} In both sheaths the field retards the electrons, and the electric force on them is balanced by their pressure gradient (figure~\ref{fig:base-momentum}(c), (d)). In the sheath the two cancel to within 0.8\,\% (cathode) and 1.3\,\% (anode) of the electric force. The drag (0.6 and 0.7\,\% of the electric force at the surface) and the thermal force (1.1 and 0.4\,\%) are small there. The drag becomes important only in the quasi-neutral layer, where it carries the current: at \(10\ \mu\)m from the surface it is 14\,\% (cathode) and 5.1\,\% (anode) of the electric force; at the anode the thermal force is as large there. The inertia of the electrons exceeds their drag within \(0.34\ \mu\)m of the cathode (by up to a factor of 44) and within \(0.40\ \mu\)m of the anode (by up to a factor of 22), but it remains smaller than the electric and pressure forces which are the dominating ones.

\emph{Mean free paths.} Figure~\ref{fig:base-sheath}(a), (b) shows, evaluated with the local densities and temperatures, the mean free paths \(\lambda_e\) and \(\lambda_i\) of table~\ref{tab:scales}, the length \(\lambda_{T_h}=1/[(n_i+n_n)\overline{Q}_{in}]\) over which ions and atoms exchange energy, and the electron Debye length. The electron mean free path is of order microns: \(3.9\ \mu\)m at the cathode sheath edge and \(3.5\ \mu\)m at the anode one. The sheaths, \(0.58\) and \(1.0\ \mu\)m thick, therefore contain only 0.13 and 0.24 electron mean free paths. The ions are more collisional. At the sheath edge, \(\lambda_i=0.29\ \mu\)m at the cathode and \(0.11\ \mu\)m at the anode, so that the ions cross 1.8 and 9.9 mean free paths in the sheaths. The anode sheath is thus collision-dominated for the ions, consistent with their inertia, which is below 10\,\% of the drag except within \(0.5\ \mu\)m of the surface. The cathode sheath is transitional: the ions make one to two collisions across it, and their inertia exceeds the drag over its inner half. There the ions cannot follow the steep rise of the field, and the local relation between field and ion velocity in equation~(\ref{eq:flux-i}) overestimates the ion velocity near the surface. The electrons cross both sheaths practically without collisions. Their balance there is, however, not one of collisional transport: the drag is a few per cent of the electric force, and the field and the pressure gradient balance each other as in the Boltzmann equilibrium of a Maxwellian population retarded by a potential barrier, which does not require collisions. The electron description therefore remains adequate in the sheaths. Beyond 2--3~\(\mu\)m from either electrode, the inertia of both species is below 10\,\% of the drag, and the inertialess, collision-dominated description of Section~\ref{sec:model-governing} holds there.

\emph{A single heavy-particle temperature.} The model describes the ions and the atoms by one temperature \(T_h\). This requires, first, that the two species exchange energy over a distance short compared with the length \(L_{T_h}=T_h/|\nabla T_h|\) over which \(T_h\) changes. This holds in both layers: \(\lambda_{T_h}/L_{T_h}\) is at most 0.10 at the cathode (at the surface) and 0.025 at the anode. The ions must not gain energy from the field faster than they share it with the atoms. At the anode, this condition is satisfied except next to the surface: the effective ion temperature \(T_{\rm eff}\) of the two-temperature closure (Appendix~\ref{app:transport}) is 2.3 times \(T_h\) at the surface, but exceeds it by more than 10\,\% only within \(0.69\ \mu\)m of it. In the cathode sheath the condition is not satisfied. The energy \(eE\lambda_{T_h}\) that an ion gains from the field between collisions is 8~eV at the surface, against \(k_BT_h=0.3\)~eV, and \(T_{\rm eff}\) exceeds \(T_h\) by a factor of 1.8 at the sheath edge and of 10.6 at the surface; it is more than twice \(T_h\) within \(0.48\ \mu\)m of the surface, and more than 10\,\% above it within \(2.1\ \mu\)m. The random energy of the ions there is not \(k_BT_h\). The model retains this heating in the ion mobility, through \(T_{\rm eff}\), but not in the ion pressure, which is evaluated with \(T_h\). Evaluated with \(T_{\rm eff}\) instead, the ion pressure gradient would reach 46\,\% of the electric force in the cathode sheath, instead of 23\,\%. The ion flux in the inner part of the cathode sheath is therefore uncertain to this extent, in addition to the effect of the neglected inertia. Beyond about \(2\ \mu\)m from the cathode and \(0.7\ \mu\)m from the anode, a single heavy-particle temperature is adequate.

\emph{Boltzmann electrons and the Bohm criterion.} Simplified sheath models describe the electrons in the sheath by the Boltzmann relation and impose the Bohm criterion on the ions entering it, \(u_i\ge u_B\), with \(u_B=\sqrt{k_B(T_e+T_i)/M}\). Figure~\ref{fig:base-sheath}(c)--(f) tests both against the computed sheaths. The electron density follows the Boltzmann relation closely. Started at the sheath edge, the relation reproduces \(n_e\) to within 1.5\,\% across the cathode sheath, where \(n_e\) falls by a factor of 9.4, and to within 0.6\,\% across the anode sheath, where it falls by a factor of 13. Outside the anode sheath, it still holds to within 6\,\% up to \(10\ \mu\)m from the surface, but \(n_e\) is 16\,\% below \(n_B\) at \(30\ \mu\)m and 36\,\% below at \(100\ \mu\)m. At the cathode, it fails progressively in the ionization layer, where the electron drag carries the current: \(n_e\) is 9\,\% below \(n_B\) at \(10\ \mu\)m and 52\,\% below at \(100\ \mu\)m. The ions, in contrast, do not satisfy the Bohm criterion at the sheath edge (figure~\ref{fig:base-sheath}(e), (f)). At the cathode they enter the sheath at \(u_i=1690\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\), against \(u_B=2160\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\), i.e.\ at 0.78\,\(u_B\). They reach \(u_B\) only \(0.39\ \mu\)m from the surface, inside the sheath, where the charge separation is already 3.4\,\%, and arrive at the surface at 2.4\,\(u_B\). At the anode, the ions enter the sheath at 0.19\,\(u_B\) and reach the surface at 0.86\,\(u_B\), never attaining \(u_B\). The Bohm flux \(n_iu_B\) at the sheath edge would therefore overestimate the ion flux entering the sheath by 28\,\% at the cathode and by a factor of 5 at the anode. The Bohm criterion is derived for a collisionless sheath. In these sheaths the ions collide with the atoms throughout the presheath and the sheath, and, except in the inner part of the cathode sheath, their velocity is set by the balance of the electric force and the drag rather than by the potential drop through which they have fallen. This agrees with the conclusion of Semenov et al.\ \cite{semenov2016numerical} for the anode layer.

\subsection{Heat flux to the electrode}
\label{sec:res-heat}

The terms of the electrode heat flux~(\ref{eq:bc-qwall}) are shown in figure~\ref{fig:base-qwall}. At the cathode, the heat flux is the small difference of large terms. The absorbed plasma electrons (\(407\ \mathrm{MW\,m^{-2}}\)), gas conduction (\(88\ \mathrm{MW\,m^{-2}}\)) and ion recombination (\(71\ \mathrm{MW\,m^{-2}}\)) heat the surface by \(566\ \mathrm{MW\,m^{-2}}\) in total, and emission cools it by \(539\ \mathrm{MW\,m^{-2}}\), so that only \(q_w=26.7\ \mathrm{MW\,m^{-2}}\) is conducted into the cathode body. The surface temperature, 3670~K, is therefore fixed mainly by the condition that emission cooling nearly balances the heating, and only weakly by the conduction into the cathode body. Emission cooling grows with \(T_{\rm wall}\) as \(T_{\rm wall}^2\exp[-(W-\Delta W)/(k_BT_{\rm wall})]\), i.e.\ by about 0.27\,\% per kelvin at this temperature, so a 5\,\% change in the heating would shift \(T_{\rm wall}\) by only about 20~K, and \(q_w\) by less than 1\,\%. Ion recombination, at \(\varepsilon_{\rm ion}-W=13.1\)~eV per ion, provides 12\,\% of the heating, although the ions carry only 5.4\,\% of the current.

At the anode, \(q_w=35.6\ \mathrm{MW\,m^{-2}}\), and the absorbed electrons deliver 66\,\% of it: 19.1~\(\mathrm{MW\,m^{-2}}\) as the work function released on entering the metal and 4.3~\(\mathrm{MW\,m^{-2}}\) as kinetic energy. Gas conduction contributes \(11.0\ \mathrm{MW\,m^{-2}}\), 31\,\%, and ion recombination 3.3\,\%. The anode heat flux is thus set by the electron current, through \(W\), by the electron temperature at the surface and, at this low current density, to a large part by conduction in the heavy gas. Since the surface temperature is prescribed, this is the heat that the cooling of the anode has to remove.

\emph{Ion impact energy.} Equation~(\ref{eq:bc-qwall}) charges the ions with their recombination energy only, not with the kinetic energy with which they strike the surface. In the model, the ion energy follows from the local field through the two-temperature closure (Appendix~\ref{app:transport}), which presumes that the ions make many collisions over the distance in which the field changes. The cathode sheath does not satisfy this: the ions cross it with only one to two collisions, and their inertia exceeds the drag over its inner part (Section~\ref{sec:res-momentum}). The local mean ion energy at the cathode surface is therefore not a reliable impact energy, and its distribution would require a kinetic treatment of the sheath. We therefore give only the maximum impact energy at the cathode: that of an ion that enters the sheath with the mean energy at the sheath edge, \(\tfrac32k_BT_{\rm eff}+\tfrac12Mv_d^2=1.3\)~eV (equations~(\ref{eq:wannier}) and (\ref{eq:mu-in})), and crosses it without a collision, gaining \(e\phi_{\rm s}=3.6\)~eV. This maximum is 4.9~eV per ion, and with the ion flux of table~\ref{tab:base} it delivers at most \(26\ \mathrm{MW\,m^{-2}}\), comparable to \(q_w\) itself. Added to the heating, however, this term would raise \(T_{\rm wall}\) by only about 20~K, since most of it would be removed by emission cooling, and \(q_w\) by less than 1\,\%. At the anode, where the ions carry 2.7\,\% of the current and fall through 1.4~V in a collision-dominated sheath, the same estimate gives at most 1.5~eV per ion and \(0.2\ \mathrm{MW\,m^{-2}}\), which is negligible.

The conditions at the two electrodes are not equally well known. On a thermionic cathode the arc attaches in a spot whose radius, and with it the current density and the temperature of the adjacent plasma, are more or less known \cite{Hsu1983,LiangTrelles}. The anode attachment, however, can be either constricted or diffuse, depending on the arc current, the gas flow and the anode material \cite{heberlein2010anode}, and the current density and the bulk temperature in front of the anode may vary accordingly. The anode case of table~\ref{tab:base} was therefore repeated, with the same column radius \(R=3.5\)~mm, with the bulk temperature varied at fixed current density, \(T_{\rm bulk}=13\,000\)--\(17\,000\)~K at \(|J_{\rm app}|=4\times10^{6}\ \mathrm{A\,m^{-2}}\), and with the current density varied at fixed bulk temperature, \(|J_{\rm app}|=2\times10^{6}\)--\(10^{7}\ \mathrm{A\,m^{-2}}\) at \(T_{\rm bulk}=15\,000\)~K (figure~\ref{fig:anode-sweep}). Everything else is unchanged. At the bulk state, radiation exceeds the Joule heating in all but two of these cases: \(w_{\rm rad}/(J_{\rm app}^2/\sigma)\) rises from 0.91 to 14 between 13\,000 and 17\,000~K, and falls from 18 to 0.72 between \(2\times10^{6}\) and \(10^{7}\ \mathrm{A\,m^{-2}}\). With the radial loss added, the bulk plasma loses more than its Joule heat in every case: the right-hand side of the column balance~(\ref{eq:column-balance}) is 3.5 to 20 times \(J_{\rm app}^2/\sigma\) between 13\,000 and 17\,000~K, and 37 to 1.5 times between \(2\times10^{6}\) and \(10^{7}\ \mathrm{A\,m^{-2}}\). As in the case of table~\ref{tab:base}, the plasma therefore cools from the imposed state across the domain, and the temperature profile is set by \(T_{\rm bulk}\) rather than by the current: at \(300\ \mu\)m from the anode, \(T_e\) is 12.9--13.0~kK at every current density, and it rises from 11.1 to 14.3~kK with the bulk temperature.

The heat flux to the anode increases linearly with the current density, from 25.8 to \(68.5\ \mathrm{MW\,m^{-2}}\) between \(2\times10^{6}\) and \(10^{7}\ \mathrm{A\,m^{-2}}\) (figure~\ref{fig:anode-sweep}(b)). It has two parts of different origin. The electron terms are proportional to the current: the work function released by the absorbed electrons contributes 4.70--4.95~W per ampere, slightly more than \(W/e\), because the ions flow into the anode and the electron current exceeds the arc current by \(|j_i|\), and their kinetic energy adds 0.9--1.3~W per ampere. Gas conduction, in contrast, does not depend on the current: it stays between 10.8 and \(11.8\ \mathrm{MW\,m^{-2}}\), because the heavy-particle temperature profile near the anode is set by the cooling plasma. As a result, the heat flux per ampere falls from 12.9 to 6.9~W as the current density increases, and the share of gas conduction from 46 to 16\,\%. At fixed current density, \(q_w\) rises from 29.5 to \(42.4\ \mathrm{MW\,m^{-2}}\) between 13\,000 and 17\,000~K (figure~\ref{fig:anode-sweep}(a)). Most of this increase comes from gas conduction, which rises from 6.8 to \(15.3\ \mathrm{MW\,m^{-2}}\), and from ion recombination (0.4 to \(2.4\ \mathrm{MW\,m^{-2}}\)); the electron terms rise by 11\,\%, with \(T_e\) at the surface rising from 5.0 to 7.0~kK.

The ions flow into the anode in every case (figure~\ref{fig:anode-sweep}(c), (d)). Their current density, \(|j_i|=1.0\)--\(1.3\times10^{5}\ \mathrm{A\,m^{-2}}\) at 15\,000~K, hardly depends on the arc current, so that their share of the current falls from 6.4 to 1.0\,\% as the current density increases. At fixed current, it grows with the bulk temperature, from 0.9 to 5.3\,\%. The anode lies below the bulk-plasma boundary by \(\phi(L)=4.0\) to 5.9~V as \(T_{\rm bulk}\) increases from 13\,000 to 17\,000~K, and this drop decreases with increasing current density, from 6.2~V at \(2\times10^{6}\) to 3.9~V at \(10^{7}\ \mathrm{A\,m^{-2}}\) (figure~\ref{fig:anode-sweep}(e), (f)). The potential at the sheath edge follows the same trends, between 0.75 and 2.1~V. The sheath width decreases both with the bulk temperature, from 1.13 to \(0.91\ \mu\)m, and with the current density, from 1.16 to \(0.77\ \mu\)m (figure~\ref{fig:anode-sweep}(g), (h)). With increasing current, \(n_e\) at the surface rises from \(1.0\times10^{20}\) to \(5.6\times10^{20}\ \mathrm{m^{-3}}\); with increasing bulk temperature it changes by only 12\,\%, while \(n_i\) at the surface rises from \(3.4\times10^{20}\) to \(9.6\times10^{20}\ \mathrm{m^{-3}}\).

\label{sec:res-time}

% Figure, csv and numbers: figures/results_fig/base_timescales.py (base_timescales_report.txt).
To estimate how quickly the near-electrode layers follow a change of the arc, the cathode case of table~\ref{tab:base} and every anode case of Section~\ref{sec:res-anode-sweep} were perturbed by a step of \(+2\,\%\) in \(|J_{\rm app}|\), and the time-dependent equations were integrated to the new steady state. The surface temperature was held at its steady value, since the electrode body responds on a time scale of seconds; the column radius \(R\) is fixed as in the steady case. The response of the ion current density \(j_i\) and of the heat flux \(q_w\) at the surface is characterized by two times: the settling time \(t_{\rm s}\), after which the quantity stays within 1\,\% of the applied step of its final value, i.e.\ within \(2\times10^{-4}\) of its initial value, and the relaxation time \(\tau\) of the final, exponential approach. The settling time is measured against the applied step rather than against the change of the quantity itself, because at the anode \(j_i\) changes by less than 1\,\%, and in either direction depending on the current.

In a one-dimensional domain, the step also excites an axial acoustic oscillation of the heavy gas between the electrode and the boundary \(x=L\). In a three-dimensional arc, such a pressure disturbance is relieved radially within \(R/c\approx0.1\ \mu\)s. At the cathode, the axial acoustics are therefore removed by multiplying the inertia of the heavy gas, the left-hand side of equation~(\ref{eq:heavy-momentum}), by \(\epsilon=10^{-4}\). This raises the sound speed to \(c/\sqrt{\epsilon}\) and leaves the steady state, with \(\mathbf{u}=0\), unchanged; with \(\epsilon=10^{-3}\), \(t_{\rm s}\) and \(\tau\) differ by less than 0.3\,\%. At the anode, the acoustics do not affect the response (between the physical sound speed and \(\epsilon=10^{-4}\), \(\tau\) agrees to 0.1\,\% and \(t_{\rm s}\) to 1.1\,\%), and the physical sound speed is retained.

\emph{Cathode.} The ion current density rises by 4.9\,\%, 2.5 times the step (figure~\ref{fig:timescales}(a)). About 14\,\% of this change occurs within 10~ps, 62\,\% within \(0.1\ \mu\)s, and the rest follows exponentially with \(\tau=0.36\ \mu\)s, so that \(t_{\rm s}=1.8\ \mu\)s. The heat flux first falls, by up to 32\,\% within 10~ps, and then rises, with the same relaxation time, to 16\,\% above its initial value, \(t_{\rm s}=2.6\ \mu\)s. This large relative change, \(4.3\ \mathrm{MW\,m^{-2}}\), is less than 1\,\% of the heating of the surface (\(566\ \mathrm{MW\,m^{-2}}\), Section~\ref{sec:res-heat}): \(q_w\) is the small difference of large terms, and with \(T_{\rm wall}\) held fixed, emission cooling cannot follow the heating. On the time scale of the electrode, \(T_{\rm wall}\) would rise, and emission cooling would remove most of the additional heating (Section~\ref{sec:res-heat}).

\emph{Anode.} The anode layer relaxes about 25 times more slowly, with \(\tau=8.9\ \mu\)s, and its two quantities behave differently (figure~\ref{fig:timescales}(b)). Because the dominant electron terms of the heat flux~(\ref{eq:bc-qwall}) are carried by the current (Section~\ref{sec:res-heat}), \(q_w\) rises by 1.2\,\% within 0.1~ns and then decreases slowly to its final change, 1.15\,\%, so that \(t_{\rm s}=6.3\ \mu\)s. The ion current density first falls by 0.7\,\% within 0.1~ns, recovers in part by \(1\ \mu\)s, and then decreases with the same relaxation time to its final change, \(-0.48\,\%\); \(t_{\rm s}=20\ \mu\)s. The small, negative change of \(j_i\) agrees with the steady states of Section~\ref{sec:res-anode-sweep}, in which the ion current density hardly depends on the arc current. Over the sweeps, the final change of \(j_i\) varies from \(-0.52\) to \(+0.73\,\%\), changing sign between \(6\times10^{6}\) and \(8\times10^{6}\ \mathrm{A\,m^{-2}}\), while the relaxation time hardly varies: \(\tau=8.7\)--\(9.0\ \mu\)s between \(2\times10^{6}\) and \(10^{7}\ \mathrm{A\,m^{-2}}\), and 9.3 to \(7.9\ \mu\)s between 14\,000 and 17\,000~K (figure~\ref{fig:timescales}(c), (d)). The settling times depend on how much of the change is left to this slow part. They stay below \(32\ \mu\)s for \(j_i\) and \(15\ \mu\)s for \(q_w\) in every case, and at 13\,000~K, where the slow part is negligible, both quantities settle within \(0.8\ \mu\)s.

The cathode layer can therefore be treated as quasi-steady for changes of the arc slower than a few microseconds. The anode layer needs up to about \(30\ \mu\)s, with a relaxation time of 8--9~\(\mu\)s that is almost independent of the current density.
