\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

A unified \rev{one-dimensional} model of the near-electrode layers of an atmospheric-pressure argon arc has been presented. A single set of equations is solved from the electrode surface, through the space-charge sheath and the ionization layer, to a bulk-plasma boundary whose state is supplied by an LTE model of the arc or by measurements. The model includes separate electron and heavy-particle energy equations, thermal diffusion and the collisional drag between species, a heavy-gas momentum equation, the radial heat loss of the column and, at the cathode, a surface heat balance with thermionic emission.

The main predictions of the model for the cathode and anode layers of a high-intensity atmospheric-pressure arc are as follows.
\begin{itemize}
\item The two layers differ in structure. At the cathode, a thin ion-rich sheath with a voltage of \rev{3.1~V} is followed by an ionization layer of about \(10^{-4}\)~m, in which the hot electrons heat the gas and ionize the plasma almost fully. At the anode, the sheath is also ion-rich but weak, 1.4~V, and most of the 5.2~V retarding drop lies in the quasi-neutral layer. There the charged particles, supplied by diffusion, exceed their equilibrium density, and the electron and heavy-particle temperatures separate strongly.
\item The electrons cross both sheaths almost without collisions, yet the drift-diffusion description of them remains adequate, because their momentum balance is dominated by the pressure gradient and the electric force. The ions are collisional and do not reach the Bohm velocity at the sheath edge, so that a Bohm flux would overestimate the ion flux by \rev{37\,\%} at the cathode and by a factor of 5 at the anode.
\item The cathode heat flux, \(27\ \mathrm{MW\,m^{-2}}\), is the small difference between the heating of the surface and emission cooling, and it is therefore set mainly by the electrode rather than by the plasma. The anode heat flux, \(36\ \mathrm{MW\,m^{-2}}\), is two-thirds electronic and one-third gas conduction.
\item At the anode, the bulk temperature sets the heavy-particle temperature, the charged-particle density, the ion current and the gas conduction. The current sets the electron density at the surface and the electron part of the heat flux, so that the heat flux increases linearly with the current. The sheath drop and the sheath width depend on both.
\item Both layers are quasi-steady to within 1\,\% when the current changes over more than about \rev{0.35~ms}. The cathode layer is almost insensitive to the bulk temperature, whereas the anode layer is quasi-steady only when the bulk temperature changes over more than about 2~ms for the heat flux and 11~ms for the ion current.
\end{itemize}

Future work will extend the model to two dimensions and couple it to an LTE model of the arc. An axisymmetric model would resolve the radial structure of the electrode attachment, so that constricted and diffuse anode attachments can be compared. Coupled to an LTE model of the bulk plasma, would enable us to determine the species fluxes, the ion current and the heat transfer to the electrodes self-consistently together with the arc dynamics.
