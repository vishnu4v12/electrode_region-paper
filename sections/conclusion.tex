\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

A unified drift-diffusion--Poisson model of the near-electrode layers of an atmospheric-pressure argon arc has been presented. A single set of equations is solved from the electrode surface, through the space-charge sheath and the ionization layer, to the bulk plasma: the continuity equations with fluxes derived from the species momentum balances, including thermal diffusion and the collisional drag between species, separate electron and heavy-particle energy equations, a heavy-gas momentum equation and Poisson's equation. Adaptive mesh refinement resolves the sheath on a fraction of the Debye length. The surface temperature of the cathode follows from a heat balance that includes thermionic emission with Schottky lowering. The model reproduces the near-cathode layer computed by Almeida et al.\ \cite{Almeida2008} and the near-anode layer computed by Semenov et al.\ \cite{semenov2016numerical}: the temperatures agree within 3--5\,\%, the charged-particle densities within 15\,\% outside the cathode sheath, and the ion fraction of the current at the surface within 5\,\%.

For a cathode at \(T_{\rm bulk}=21\,000\)~K and \(1.2\times10^{8}\ \mathrm{A\,m^{-2}}\), and an anode at \(12\,400\)~K and \(9\times10^{6}\ \mathrm{A\,m^{-2}}\), with the radial heat loss of the column calibrated on the bulk state, the main results are as follows.
\begin{itemize}
\item The two layers have opposite structures. The cathode has an ion-rich sheath \(0.57\ \mu\)m wide with a drop of 4.1~V, followed by an ionization layer of about \(10^{-4}\)~m in which the gas is heated and the plasma becomes almost fully ionized. Joule heating is the dominant electron energy source throughout. The anode has practically no sheath (0.41~V across \(0.84\ \mu\)m). Its 2.6~V retarding drop is spread over the quasi-neutral layer, the charged particles are supplied by diffusion at densities far above ionization equilibrium, and \(T_e-T_h\) reaches 3.9~kK at the surface. The electrons lose energy to the retarding field, and this loss is supplied by the electron energy flux from the column, mainly through conduction and the thermal-diffusion heat flux close to the anode.
\item The electrons cross both sheaths with fewer than 0.2 collisions, yet their density follows the Boltzmann relation within about 2\,\%, so that the electron force balance remains adequate there. The ions cross 1.8 (cathode) and 8.2 (anode) mean free paths in the sheaths and do not satisfy the Bohm criterion at the sheath edge, where they move at 0.82 and 0.13 times the Bohm velocity. A Bohm flux at the sheath edge would overestimate the ion flux by 22\,\% at the cathode and by a factor of 7.5 at the anode.
\item The cathode heat flux, \(27\ \mathrm{MW\,m^{-2}}\), is the small difference between the heating of the surface (\(638\ \mathrm{MW\,m^{-2}}\)) and emission cooling (\(611\ \mathrm{MW\,m^{-2}}\)). Because emission cooling depends steeply on \(T_{\rm wall}\), the heat flux into the cathode body is set by the electrode rather than by the plasma: it changed by less than 4\,\% over all the cathode cases, while the heating of the surface changed by 50\,\%. The anode heat flux, \(38\ \mathrm{MW\,m^{-2}}\), is 82\,\% electronic and increases nearly in proportion to the current, at 4.1--4.4~W per ampere, of which 2.63~W per ampere is the work function.
\item Because the ions cross the cathode sheath with only one to two collisions, a local estimate of their impact energy is not reliable, and only its maximum, for an ion crossing the sheath without a collision, has been given: 5.6~eV, corresponding to at most \(38\ \mathrm{MW\,m^{-2}}\). Owing to the stiffness of emission cooling, this term would change the cathode surface temperature by only about 20~K.
\item Over the ranges of bulk temperature and current density studied, the cathode sheath width stays between 0.55 and \(0.60\ \mu\)m, its larger drop at higher current being held by a denser ion space charge. The anode sheath width follows the Debye length at the surface and decreases from 0.93 to \(0.60\ \mu\)m as the current density increases from \(7\times10^{6}\) to \(2\times10^{7}\ \mathrm{A\,m^{-2}}\).
\item After a change of the current, the cathode layer reaches a new steady state within 0.6--2.6~\(\mu\)s. At the anode, the heat flux follows within about \(20\ \mu\)s, but the ion current needs 40--75~\(\mu\)s.
\end{itemize}

The local, inertialess transport description is marginal within about \(0.4\ \mu\)m of the cathode, where the inertia of the ions exceeds their drag, and the ion energy distribution at the cathode requires a kinetic treatment of the sheath. The present one-dimensional calculations represent the radial losses of the column through a calibrated sink. Since the layers settle within microseconds and are characterized by the bulk state and the current density, the model can supply electrode boundary conditions, such as the voltage drop, the current partition and the heat flux to the electrode, to LTE models of the arc column.
