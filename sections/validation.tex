\section{Validation}
\label{sec:validation}

The model is validated against two published unified models of the near-electrode layers of atmospheric-pressure argon arcs: the near-cathode layer computed by Almeida et al.\ \cite{Almeida2008} and the near-anode layer computed by Semenov et al.\ \cite{semenov2016numerical}. Both works solve the multi-fluid equations for electrons, ions and atoms with separate electron and heavy-particle temperatures, coupled to Poisson's equation, across the whole layer, from the electrode surface through the space-charge sheath and the ionization layer to the bulk plasma. Neither assumes quasi-neutrality or matches separate sheath and plasma solutions, so complete profiles can be compared with those of the present model. The reference profiles were digitized from the published figures.

The conditions of the reference calculations were reproduced as closely as possible. The electrode surface temperature was prescribed at the value used in each reference, rather than obtained from the heat balance~(\ref{eq:bc-heat-balance}), and the radial loss~(\ref{eq:radial-loss}) was switched off, \(\Lambda=0\), since neither reference includes it. The domain length was \(L=5\times10^{-4}\)~m in both cases, and the Dirichlet state imposed at \(x=L\) (Section~\ref{sec:model-bc}) was taken from the reference profiles at that distance, where \(T_e\approx T_h\) in both. The domain was discretized with a uniform base grid of 512 cells and four levels of refinement towards the electrode, each with a refinement ratio of 4, so that the finest cell size is \(3.8\times10^{-9}\)~m, several times smaller than the Debye length near the electrode (Table~\ref{tab:scales}). The equations were integrated in time at a fixed current density until a steady state was reached. In both cases \(x\) denotes the distance from the electrode surface.

\subsection{Near-cathode layer}
\label{sec:val-cathode}

The cathode case is that of figure~5(b) of Almeida et al.\ \cite{Almeida2008}: a tungsten cathode with work function \(4.55\)~eV, thermionic emission~(\ref{eq:bc-emission}) included, surface temperature \(3500\)~K and current density \(10^7\ \mathrm{A\,m^{-2}}\). The two solutions are compared in figure~\ref{fig:val-cathode}. The temperature profiles agree closely across the whole layer (figure~\ref{fig:val-cathode}(b)). The electron temperature stays near \(17.5\)--\(18\)~kK through the sheath and the inner part of the ionization layer and decreases towards the bulk plasma, while the heavy-particle temperature rises from the wall value to a maximum of \(13.9\)~kK at \(x\approx2\times10^{-4}\)~m, compared with \(13.6\)~kK in the reference. From \(x\approx3\times10^{-6}\)~m outwards, both temperatures agree with the reference within \(0.4\)~kK (3\,\%).

The ion fraction of the current, figure~\ref{fig:val-cathode}(c), is reproduced over the whole layer. At the cathode surface the present model gives \(j_i/j=0.218\), compared with \(0.216\) reported by Almeida et al.; \(j_i/j\) stays at this value through the sheath, falls to zero at \(x\approx1.5\times10^{-4}\)~m, and becomes slightly negative further out, where the ion flux is directed away from the cathode. The maximum potential in the layer is \(13.6\)~V, compared with \(13.95\)~V in the reference. In the ionization layer, \(10^{-5}\lesssim x\lesssim3\times10^{-4}\)~m, the charged-particle densities of the present model are 10--15\,\% higher (figure~\ref{fig:val-cathode}(a)).

The largest differences are in the space-charge sheath, \(x\lesssim10^{-6}\)~m. At \(x=10^{-7}\)~m, the electron and ion densities of the present model are higher by a factor of about 2.5, and the field strength is \(2.3\times10^7\ \mathrm{V\,m^{-1}}\), compared with \(1.6\times10^7\ \mathrm{V\,m^{-1}}\). Since the ion current fraction is the same in both models, the higher ion density in the sheath corresponds to a lower ion drift velocity. The sheath is where the solution is most sensitive to the description of ion transport at a high reduced field (Appendix~\ref{app:transport}) and to the ion boundary condition~(\ref{eq:bc-ion}). These differences remain confined to the sheath and its edge: further out, the field is small in both models, and the current fractions and the temperatures agree.

\subsection{Near-anode layer}
\label{sec:val-anode}

The anode case is that of figure~3(b) of Semenov et al.\ \cite{semenov2016numerical}: a non-emitting anode, \(\Gamma_{\rm em}=0\), with surface temperature \(1000\)~K and current density \(1.5\times10^7\ \mathrm{A\,m^{-2}}\) (\(1500\ \mathrm{A\,cm^{-2}}\)). The comparison is shown in figure~\ref{fig:val-anode}. The electron and ion densities agree within 15\,\% across the layer (figure~\ref{fig:val-anode}(a)). Following Semenov et al., the figure also shows the Saha density \(n_{\rm sa}\), the charged-particle density that the local \(T_e\) and \(T_h\) would give in ionization equilibrium with quasi-neutrality at the pressure \(p_0\). Near the anode the actual densities exceed \(n_{\rm sa}\) by nearly two orders of magnitude. The anode region is therefore far from ionization equilibrium: charged particles are supplied by diffusion from the hotter plasma rather than produced locally, and both models capture this departure.

The temperature profiles agree closely (figure~\ref{fig:val-anode}(b)). At the anode surface, the electron temperature is \(5.45\)~kK, compared with \(5.59\)~kK reported by Semenov et al., and the heavy-particle temperatures differ by less than \(0.3\)~kK across the layer. The electric field near the anode, figure~\ref{fig:val-anode}(c), is negative: it is directed towards the anode, so that the anode lies at a lower potential than the adjacent plasma and the electrons are retarded. The field at the surface is \(-1.57\times10^6\ \mathrm{V\,m^{-1}}\), compared with \(-1.42\times10^6\ \mathrm{V\,m^{-1}}\) in the reference. Ions flow into the anode, so \(j_i/j\) is negative there. At the surface, the present model gives \(j_i/j=-0.0068\), compared with \(-0.0065\) in the reference.

The main difference is in the minimum of \(j_i/j\), which is \(-0.018\) at \(x=3.5\times10^{-4}\)~m in the present model and \(-0.021\) at \(x=4.3\times10^{-4}\)~m in the reference. This minimum lies close to the domain boundary \(x=L\). In the reference solution, the plasma becomes uniform only at \(x\approx1\)--\(2\)~mm, where \(T_e=16.8\)~kK, whereas the present calculation imposes the reference state at \(x=L=0.5\)~mm, where \(T_e=T_h\approx14\)~kK and the plasma is still varying. The Dirichlet condition at \(x=L\) therefore constrains the solution in the outer part of the ionization layer, which is the likely cause of the difference near \(x=L\). Closer to the anode, the two solutions agree.

In both cases, the present model reproduces the structure of the near-electrode layer obtained by independent unified models: the temperatures, the current partition between ions and electrons, the sign and magnitude of the near-electrode field, and the departure from ionization equilibrium. Apart from the charged-particle densities in the cathode sheath and the minimum of \(j_i/j\) near the outer boundary of the anode calculation, the temperatures agree within 3--5\,\% and the charged-particle densities within 15\,\%.
