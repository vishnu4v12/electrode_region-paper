\section{Physical model and scale analysis}
\label{sec:model}

\subsection{Governing equations}
\label{sec:model-governing}


In the electrode region, the plasma is in contact with a relatively cold, absorbing electrode surface. This interaction produces strong gradients in the plasma properties, and the plasma departs progressively from LTE as the electrode is approached. We estimate the length scales over which these departures develop by considering the LTE plasma adjacent to the electrode region at temperatures between \(T=12{,}000~\mathrm{K}\) and \(T=22{,}000~\mathrm{K}\), which are typical of the anode and cathode regions, respectively, of a high-intensity atmospheric-pressure arc discharge \cite{Lowke2006,Hsu1983,LiangTrelles}.


In the bulk plasma, the electron density \(n_e\) is governed by ionization and recombination reactions. The characteristic time scale of these reactions can be obtained by linearizing \(\dot n_e=k_{\rm ion}n_en_n-k_{\rm rec}n_e^3\) (with \(n_i=n_e\)) about equilibrium at fixed \(p\) and \(T\) (so that \(\delta n_n=-2\,\delta n_e\)), which gives \(\tau_{rxn}=1/(2k_{\rm rec}n_e^2+2k_{\rm ion}n_e)\). A characteristic length scale for the reactions is then \(\lambda_{rxn}=\sqrt{D_a\tau_{rxn}}\), where \(D_a\) is the ambipolar diffusion coefficient. This length scale implies that a plasma out of chemical equilibrium needs a distance of order \(\lambda_{rxn}\) to relax back to equilibrium. Similarly, since an electron loses only a fraction \(2m_e/M\) of its energy in an elastic collision with a heavy particle, the length scale for the energy relaxation between electrons and heavy species can be estimated as \(\lambda_T=1/[\sqrt{2m_e/M}\,(n_i\overline{Q}_{ei}+n_n\overline{Q}_{en})]\), where \(n_i\) and \(n_n\) are the ion and neutral number densities, and \(\overline{Q}_{ei}\) and \(\overline{Q}_{en}\) are the averaged momentum-transfer cross sections for electron--ion and electron--neutral collisions. \(\overline{Q}_{en}\) is the Maxwellian average of the argon momentum-transfer cross section used in Appendix~\ref{app:transport}. Electron--ion collisions are Coulomb collisions, for which the averaged momentum-transfer cross section is \(\overline{Q}_{ei}=e^4\ln\Lambda/[32\pi\varepsilon_0^2(k_BT_e)^2]\) \cite{Almeida2008}. Here, \(k_B\) is the Boltzmann constant and \(\ln\Lambda\) is the Coulomb logarithm, \(\ln\Lambda=\ln\!\left(1.24\times10^7 T_e^{3/2}n_e^{-1/2}\right)\), with \(T_e\) in K and \(n_e\) in \(\mathrm{m}^{-3}\). Since ions and atoms have nearly equal masses and exchange energy efficiently, the length scale for the equilibration of the heavy-species temperature is \(\lambda_{T_h}=1/[(n_i+n_n)\overline{Q}_{in}]\). Here \(\overline{Q}_{in}=2[7\times10^{-10}-0.6\times10^{-10}\ln(2k_BT_h/e)]^2~\mathrm{m}^2\) is the ion--neutral momentum-transfer cross section \cite{benilov1998ionization}.

At the smallest scale, the Debye length calculated from the LTE number densities, \(\lambda_{De}=\sqrt{\varepsilon_0k_BT_e/(n_ee^2)}\), is of order \(10^{-8}~\mathrm{m}\). It sets the extent over which charge separation and space-charge effects are important. These scales are reported in Table~\ref{tab:scales}, together with electron mean free path \(\lambda_e=1/(n_n\overline{Q}_{en}+n_i\overline{Q}_{ei})\) and ion mean free path \(\lambda_i=1/(n_n\overline{Q}_{in}+n_i\overline{Q}_{ii})\), where \(\overline{Q}_{ii}\) is the Coulomb cross section \(\overline{Q}_{ei}\) evaluated at \(T_h\).

The resulting hierarchy of length scales is \(\lambda_{De}\ll\lambda_i\sim\lambda_e\sim\lambda_{T_h}<\lambda_{\mathrm{rxn}}\sim\lambda_T\). These scale estimates indicate that a continuum description is valid in the quasi-neutral plasma. In the sheath, whose thickness is a few tens of Debye lengths and thus comparable to \(\lambda_i\) and \(\lambda_e\), it is marginal; its validity there is examined a posteriori in Section~\ref{sec:res-momentum}. Furthermore, the gas temperature near the electrodes varies over a fluid boundary-layer thickness of order 1~mm, which is much larger than \(\lambda_{T_h}\). Thus, the heavy species, consisting of ions and neutrals, can reasonably be described using a single temperature. These scales also
indicate that the effect of electrodes is felt over a region of thickness of order \(10^{-4}~\mathrm{m}\) by plasma, in agreement with other modelling results and analyses \cite{Almeida2008,DinulescuPfender1980,benilov1995model,hsu1983analysis}. Consistent with this estimate, Lowke and Tanaka \cite{Lowke2006} showed that excluding a near-electrode region of this thickness is sufficient for the remaining plasma to be modelled as being in LTE. This separation of scales necessitates treating the near-electrode region separately from the bulk plasma.


We therefore model the electrode region as a one-dimensional domain of length \(5\times10^{-4}~\mathrm{m}\), with the electrode surface at one end and the bulk plasma at the other. Externally imposed bulk flows, such as the cathode jet, are not represented. The heavy gas is nevertheless not assumed to be at rest, since it can move during the transient stages of the discharge. The plasma is taken to consist of three species: electrons (e), singly charged argon ions (i), and argon atoms (n). Multiply charged ions are neglected: in the anode region the temperature is too low to produce them, and in the cathode region they are present but have a negligible effect \cite{almeida2000simulation}. Each species is assumed to follow a Maxwellian velocity distribution (see Appendix~\ref{app:transport}).


Solutions are sought for the species number densities, the electron and heavy-particle temperatures, and the electric field. The following governing equations are thus solved in the electrode region.

The number densities of the three species obey
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial n_e}{\partial t}+\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_e=S,
\qquad
\frac{\partial n_i}{\partial t}+\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_i=S,
\qquad
\frac{\partial n_n}{\partial t}+\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_n=-S,
\label{eq:continuity}
\end{equation}


Different authors have evaluated the net ionization rate \(S\) in different ways \cite{Almeida2008,DinulescuPfender1980,Lowke2006,LiangTrelles}. In this work we use the reaction rates as proposed by Almeida et al. \cite{Almeida2008}, where ionization is limited by the excitation of the first excited level, and accounts for the radiative escape of resonance radiation when \(n_e\lesssim10^{21}~\mathrm{m^{-3}}\).




The momentum equation for the plasma as whole is given by \(\rho\,D\mathbf{u}/Dt = \mathbf{F} \), where \(\mathbf{F}\) is the net force on the plasma. For each species \(s\), the corresponding momentum equation is \(m_sn_s\,D_s\mathbf{u}_s/Dt=\mathbf{F}_s\), where \(\mathbf{F}_s\) is the net force on the species. We neglect the inertia of each species relative to the heavy gas, \(D_s\mathbf{u}_s/Dt\simeq D\mathbf{u}/Dt\), so that every species shares the acceleration of the heavy gas and there is no relative acceleration between species. The inertia of species \(s\) is therefore \(Y_s\mathbf{F}\), where \(Y_s=m_sn_s/\rho\) is its mass fraction. The momentum equations then reduce to the local force balances \cite{zhdanov2002transport}
\begin{align}
0 &= -en_e\mathbf{E}-\nabla p_e
 - m_en_e\bar\nu^M_{en}(\mathbf{u}_e-\mathbf{u}_n)
 - m_en_e\bar\nu^M_{ei}(\mathbf{u}_e-\mathbf{u}_i)
 - C_en_ek_B\nabla T_e
 - Y_e\mathbf{F},
\label{eq:mom-e}\\
0 &= +en_i\mathbf{E}-\nabla p_i
 - Mn_i\bar\nu^M_{in}(\mathbf{u}_i-\mathbf{u}_n)
 - Mn_i\bar\nu^M_{ie}(\mathbf{u}_i-\mathbf{u}_e)
 + C_in_ek_B\nabla T_e
 - Y_i\mathbf{F},
\label{eq:mom-i}\\
0 &= -\nabla p_n
 - Mn_n\bar\nu^M_{ni}(\mathbf{u}_n-\mathbf{u}_i)
 - Mn_n\bar\nu^M_{ne}(\mathbf{u}_n-\mathbf{u}_e)
 + C_nn_ek_B\nabla T_e
 - Y_n\mathbf{F} .
\label{eq:mom-n}
\end{align}
The frequencies \(\bar\nu^M_{en}\), \(\bar\nu^M_{ei}\), \(\bar\nu^M_{in}\), \(\bar\nu^M_{ie}\), \(\bar\nu^M_{ni}\), and \(\bar\nu^M_{ne}\) are \emph{averaged momentum-transfer collision frequencies}. \(C_e\) is the electron thermal coefficient, and \(C_i\) and \(C_n\) are its reactions on the ions and atoms. The calculations of these frequencies and coefficients are described in Appendix~\ref{app:transport}. Momentum conservation in each collision pair relates the frequencies, and the thermal forces sum to zero:
\begin{equation}
m_en_e\bar\nu^M_{en}=Mn_n\bar\nu^M_{ne},\qquad
m_en_e\bar\nu^M_{ei}=Mn_i\bar\nu^M_{ie},\qquad
n_i\bar\nu^M_{in}=n_n\bar\nu^M_{ni},\qquad
C_i+C_n=C_e
\label{eq:newton3}
\end{equation}


Adding equations~(\ref{eq:mom-e})--(\ref{eq:mom-n}) and using equation~(\ref{eq:newton3}) results in the total momentum equation.
\begin{equation}
\rho\,D\mathbf{u}/Dt = e(n_i-n_e)\mathbf{E}-\nabla p .
\label{eq:bulk-balance}
\end{equation}
In practice, equations~(\ref{eq:mom-e}), (\ref{eq:mom-i}) and (\ref{eq:bulk-balance}) are solved instead of (\ref{eq:mom-n}), and the neutral velocity \(\mathbf{u}_n\) is obtained \(\mathbf{u}_n\) from the definition of heavy-gas velocity \(\mathbf{u}\) as the mass-weighted average of the ion and neutral velocities,
\begin{equation}
\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_n= (n_i+n_n)\mathbf{u}-n_i\mathbf{u}_i.
\label{eq:flux-n}
\end{equation}


Solving the electron balance~(\ref{eq:mom-e}) for the electron flux, with \(\bar\nu^M_e=\bar\nu^M_{en}+\bar\nu^M_{ei}\) the total electron momentum-transfer frequency and \(\nabla p_e=k_BT_e\nabla n_e+n_ek_B\nabla T_e\), gives
\begin{equation}
\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_e=-\frac{en_e}{m_e\bar\nu^M_e}\mathbf{E}
-\frac{k_BT_e}{m_e\bar\nu^M_e}\nabla n_e
-(1+C_e)\,\frac{n_ek_B}{m_e\bar\nu^M_e}\nabla T_e
+\frac{n_e}{\bar\nu^M_e}\Big(\bar\nu^M_{en}\mathbf{u}_n+\bar\nu^M_{ei}\mathbf{u}_i\Big),
\label{eq:flux-e}
\end{equation}
where the negligible term \(Y_e\mathbf{F}\) has been dropped. The first two terms are the conventional drift and diffusion. Their coefficients, \(e/(m_e\bar\nu^M_e)\) and \(k_BT_e/(m_e\bar\nu^M_e)\), are the magnitudes of the electron mobility and diffusivity; because both are set by the same frequency, they satisfy the Einstein relation identically. The third term combines the temperature-gradient part of the electron pressure force with the thermal force. The last term is the collisional drag exerted on the electrons by the moving heavy species.

The ion flux follows from the ion balance~(\ref{eq:mom-i}) in the same way. With \(\bar\nu^M_i=\bar\nu^M_{in}+\bar\nu^M_{ie}\) the total ion momentum-transfer frequency and \(\nabla p_i=k_BT_h\nabla n_i+n_ik_B\nabla T_h\),
\begin{equation}
\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_i=\frac{en_i}{M\bar\nu^M_i}\mathbf{E}
-\frac{k_BT_h}{M\bar\nu^M_i}\nabla n_i
-\frac{n_ik_B}{M\bar\nu^M_i}\nabla T_h
+C_i\,\frac{n_ek_B}{M\bar\nu^M_i}\nabla T_e
-\frac{n_i}{n_h}\frac{\mathbf{F}}{M\bar\nu^M_i}
+\frac{n_i}{\bar\nu^M_i}\Big(\bar\nu^M_{in}\mathbf{u}_n+\bar\nu^M_{ie}\mathbf{u}_e\Big).
\label{eq:flux-i}
\end{equation}
The first three terms are ion drift, diffusion, and diffusion driven by the heavy-particle temperature gradient, with mobility \(e/(M\bar\nu^M_i)\) and diffusivity \(k_BT_h/(M\bar\nu^M_i)\). The fourth term is the reaction of the ions to the electron thermal force. The fifth term is the ions' share, \(Y_i\mathbf{F}\), of the net force on the plasma. This share accelerates the ions together with the rest of the heavy gas. The last term is the collisional drag exerted on the ions by the atoms and electrons.

The velocities in the drag terms of equations~(\ref{eq:flux-e}) and (\ref{eq:flux-i}) are those of the other species, so the two fluxes are coupled. If the drag, thermal-force, and mixture-acceleration terms are removed (\(C_e=C_i=0\), \(Y_i\mathbf{F}=0\), and \(\mathbf{u}_s=\mathbf{u}=0\) in the drag terms), equations~(\ref{eq:flux-e}) and (\ref{eq:flux-i}) reduce to the classical drift--diffusion fluxes, with mobilities \(e/(m_e\bar\nu^M_e)\) and \(e/(M\bar\nu^M_i)\). Unlike the classical fluxes, however, they retain the full pressure-gradient force of each species in a non-isothermal plasma.

The electric field is obtained from Poisson's equation,
\begin{equation}
\nabla\cdot(\varepsilon_0\mathbf{E})=e(n_i-n_e),
\qquad
\mathbf{E}=-\nabla\phi,
\label{eq:poisson}
\end{equation}
which is solved over the entire computational domain. The transition from the quasi-neutral bulk plasma to the space-charge region is thereby captured without imposing the sheath boundaries a priori.

The electron thermal energy density \(\mathcal{E}=\tfrac32n_ek_BT_e\) obeys
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial\mathcal{E}}{\partial t}+\nabla\cdot\mathbf{h}_e
=-e\,\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_e\cdot\mathbf{E}
-\frac32n_ek_B(T_e-T_h)\,\frac{2m_e}{M}\,\bar\nu^E_{eh}
-\varepsilon_{\rm ion}S
-w_{\rm rad}
-Q_e ,
\label{eq:eenergy}
\end{equation}
with the electron energy flux
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{h}_e=\frac52k_BT_e\,\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_e
-\kappa_e\nabla T_e
+k_BT_e\,n_e\Big[C_i(\mathbf{u}_e-\mathbf{u}_i)+C_n(\mathbf{u}_e-\mathbf{u}_n)\Big].
\label{eq:heatflux-e}
\end{equation}
The terms on the right-hand side of equation~(\ref{eq:eenergy}) are Joule heating by the field, elastic energy transfer to the heavy species, the ionization-energy sink, the net radiative loss, and the radial heat loss of the electrons. In the elastic term, \(\bar\nu^E_{eh}=\bar\nu^E_{en}+\bar\nu^E_{ei}\), where the superscript \(E\) denotes the energy-exchange average of collision frequencies (Appendix~\ref{app:transport}). The ionization sink is the net ionization rate multiplied by \(\varepsilon_{\rm ion}=15.76\)~eV, the first ionization potential of argon. The energy flux~(\ref{eq:heatflux-e}) is the sum of the enthalpy flux at \(\tfrac52k_BT_e\) per electron, Fourier conduction with the zero-current electron thermal conductivity \(\kappa_e\), and a thermal-diffusion heat flux \cite{zhdanov2002transport}. The net radiative loss \(w_{\rm rad}\) is that of Almeida et al.\ \cite{Almeida2008}, taken from Benilov and Naidis \cite{benilov1998ionization}. 

A one-dimensional domain has no lateral boundary, so the Joule heat generated away from the electrode can leave only by radiation and by conduction through the two ends of the domain. In a real arc, the column loses most of its Joule heat radially, by conduction \cite{elenbaas1935ahnlichkeitsgesetze}. Without a lateral sink the one-dimensional column overheats, and the solution depends on the length of the domain. We therefore represent the radial conduction of a column of radius \(R\) by a volumetric loss for each energy channel,
\begin{equation}
Q_e=\Lambda\,(\kappa_e+\kappa_R)\,(T_e-T_{\rm edge}),
\qquad
Q_h=\Lambda\,\kappa_h\,(T_h-T_{\rm edge}),
\qquad
\Lambda=\frac{4}{R^2},
\label{eq:radial-loss}
\end{equation}
where \(T_{\rm edge}\) is the temperature at the edge of the column. Radially, ion pairs also diffuse out of the hot core and recombine near the cold edge, releasing the ionization energy there. The axial counterpart of this transport is carried by the model's own equations, through the ambipolar flux and the ionization sink (Appendix~\ref{app:transport}), but radially it has to be represented by the reactive thermal conductivity \(\kappa_R\). We take \(\kappa_R\) of argon in LTE at atmospheric pressure computed with Mutation++ \cite{scoggins2020mutation}, the same as in figure~\ref{fig:lte-check}, evaluated at \(T_e\). It is charged to the electrons, because the ionization energy is electron energy in this model. The factor \(\Lambda\) follows from a parabolic radial temperature profile, for which \(-r^{-1}\,\partial_r(r\kappa\,\partial_rT)=4\kappa(T-T_{\rm edge})/R^2\) on the axis. Each channel loses heat through its own conductivity, so the gas is not cooled through the electrons, and \(T_e-T_h\) remains set by the elastic exchange. \(Q_e\) enters the electron energy equation~(\ref{eq:eenergy}) and \(Q_h\) the heavy-particle energy equation~(\ref{eq:henergy}). The radius is not prescribed; \(\Lambda\) is instead chosen so that the bulk-plasma state imposed at \(x=L\) (Section~\ref{sec:model-bc}) is a uniform solution of the column, in which Joule heating balances radiation and radial conduction,
\begin{equation}
\frac{J_{\rm app}^2}{\sigma}=w_{\rm rad}+\Lambda\,(\kappa_e+\kappa_R+\kappa_h)\,(T_{\rm bulk}-T_{\rm edge}),
\label{eq:radial-calibration}
\end{equation}
with all quantities evaluated at the bulk state, and \(\sigma\) the electrical conductivity of the plasma. The plasma far from the electrode then relaxes to the bulk state, whatever the length of the domain. Within the near-electrode layer, the axial gradients develop over \(10^{-6}\)--\(10^{-4}\)~m, while \(R\) is of order \(10^{-3}\)~m. There, the radial losses are negligible in comparison.

The common heavy-particle temperature is governed by
\begin{equation}
n_hc_v\left(\frac{\partial T_h}{\partial t}+\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla T_h\right)
=\nabla\cdot(\kappa_h\nabla T_h)
+\frac32n_ek_B(T_e-T_h)\,\frac{2m_e}{M}\,\bar\nu^E_{eh}
+Q_{in}
-Q_h
-n_hk_BT_h\,\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u},
\label{eq:henergy}
\end{equation}
where \(c_v=\tfrac32k_B\) is the translational heat capacity per heavy particle and \(\kappa_h\) is the heavy-particle thermal conductivity (Appendix~\ref{app:transport}). The elastic-exchange term is the same as in the electron energy equation~(\ref{eq:eenergy}), but with the opposite sign. The ionization energy and the radiative loss are charged entirely to the electrons and do not appear here. \(Q_h\) is the radial heat loss of the heavy particles, equation~(\ref{eq:radial-loss}). The ion--neutral frictional heating is
\begin{equation}
Q_{in}=Mn_i\,\bar\nu^M_{in}\,|\mathbf{u}_i-\mathbf{u}_n|^2 ,
\label{eq:Qin}
\end{equation}
which is positive definite and reduces to the Joule dissipation of the ion drift when the neutral gas is at rest. The last term in equation~(\ref{eq:henergy}) is the compression work of the heavy gas.

The heavy-gas velocity is advanced by an inviscid single-fluid momentum equation,
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial(\rho\mathbf{u})}{\partial t}+\nabla\cdot(\rho\mathbf{u}\mathbf{u})
=-\nabla p+e(n_i-n_e)\mathbf{E},
\qquad
p=n_ek_BT_e+n_hk_BT_h .
\label{eq:heavy-momentum}
\end{equation}
This equation governs the motion of the heavy gas as a whole.

The state advanced in time is therefore \(\{n_e,n_i,n_n,T_e,T_h,\mathbf{u},\phi\}\). It is governed by the continuity equations~(\ref{eq:continuity}), Poisson's equation~(\ref{eq:poisson}); the electron and heavy-particle energy equations~(\ref{eq:eenergy}), (\ref{eq:henergy}) and the heavy-gas momentum equation~(\ref{eq:heavy-momentum}).
