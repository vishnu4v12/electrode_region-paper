\section{Physical model and scale analysis}
\label{sec:model}

\subsection{Governing equations}
\label{sec:model-governing}

In the electrode region, the plasma interacts with the relatively cold, absorbing electrode surfaces. This interaction introduces strong gradients in the plasma properties, causing the plasma to progressively depart from LTE. We estimate the characteristic length scales associated with these departures by considerin th LTE plasma adjacent to the electrode regions to be present in temperature range \(T=10{,}000~\mathrm{K}\) to \(T=20{,}000~\mathrm{K}\). which are typical of the anode and cathode regions, respectively, in a high-intensity atmospheric-pressure arc discharge \cite{Lowke2006,Hsu1983,LiangTrelles}. In an ionized gas, the electron mean free path can be approximated as \(\lambda_e = 1/(n_e\overline{Q}_{ee}+n_a\overline{Q}_{ea})\), where \(n_e\) and \(n_a\) are the electron and neutral-atom number densities, respectively, and \(\overline{Q}_{ee}\) and \(\overline{Q}_{ea}\) are the corresponding momentum-transfer collision cross sections for electron--electron and electron--atom collisions. Electron--electron collisions are Coulomb collisions, for which the averaged momentum-transfer cross section, defined as \(\overline{Q}_{ee}=e^4\ln\Lambda/[12\sqrt{2}\,\pi\varepsilon_0^2(k_BT_e)^2]\) \cite{mitchner1973partially}. Here, \(\ln\Lambda\) is the Coulomb logarithm, given by \(\ln\Lambda=\ln\!\left(1.24\times10^7 T_e^{3/2}n_e^{-1/2}\right)\), where \(T_e\) is in K and \(n_e\) is in \(\mathrm{m}^{-3}\).

The average electron--neutral momentum-transfer cross section is of the order \(0.2\times10^{-19}~\mathrm{m}^2\) \cite{lxcat} for the electron temperatures considered. Using the corresponding LTE electron and neutral densities, the resulting electron mean free path is of order \(\lambda_e\sim10^{-6}~\mathrm{m}\). The characteristic length scale for electron--heavy-particle energy relaxation, \(\lambda_T\), is approximated as\( \lambda_e/\sqrt{2m_e/M}\), where \(M\) is the mass of the heavy species (argon in this case) and \(m_e\) is the electron mass. Thus, \(\lambda_T\sim10^{-4}~\mathrm{m}\). To estimate the diffusion length scale, where the effect of absorbing electrode surfaces becomes important, we use the expression \( \lambda_d=(kT_e/e)/E_z\)from Lowke and Tanaka \cite{Lowke2006} which gives \(\lambda_d\sim10^{-4}~\mathrm{m}\) for a typical arc discharge.

At the smallest spatial scale, the Debye length calculated from the LTE number densities is of order \(\lambda_{De}\sim10^{-8}~\mathrm{m}\), which characterizes the spatial extent over which charge separation and space-charge effects become important. The ion mean free path provides another relevant scale and can be estimated as \(\lambda_i=1/(n_a\overline{Q}_{ia})\), where \(\overline{Q}_{ia}\) is the momentum-transfer cross section for ion--atom collisions. For argon, we use the approximate fit \(\overline{Q}_{ia}=2[7\times10^{-10}-0.6\times10^{-10}\ln(2k_BT_h)]\) \cite{benilov1998ionization}, where \(k_B\) is the Boltzmann constant and \(T_h\) is the heavy-particle temperature.Use of this expression gives an ion mean free path of order \(\lambda_i\sim10^{-7}~\mathrm{m}\).

The resulting hierarchy of characteristic length scales in the near-electrode region is therefore \(\lambda_{De}\ll\lambda_i\ll\lambda_e\ll\lambda_d\sim\lambda_T\). These estimates indicate that the near-electrode region over which significant departures from LTE develop extends over a characteristic length scale of order \(10^{-4}~\mathrm{m}\). Other modeling results and analysis shows the same \cite{Almeida2008,DinulescuPfender1980,benilov1995model,hsu1983analysis}. Consistent with this estimate, Lowke and Tanaka \cite{Lowke2006} showed that excluding a near-electrode region of order \(10^{-4}~\mathrm{m}\) is sufficient to model the remaining plasma using an LTE approximation. This separation of scales provides the basis as well a necessity to treat the near-electrode region separately from the bulk plasma.



We therefore model the electrode region as a one-dimensional domain of length 2e-4 m with the electrode surface at one end and the bulk plasma at the other. Externally imposed bulk flows, such as the cathode jet, are not represented. However the bulk fluid is not assumed to be at rest during the transiet stages of the discharge. The region is assumed to consist of three species: electrons (e), singly charged argon ions (i), and argon atoms (n). The presence of multiply charged species is neglected as justified in \cite{almeida2000simulation}. Ions and neutrals exchange momentum and energy rapidly through resonant charge-exchange collisions, whose mean free path \(\lambda_i\) is small compared with the length scale of the temperature variation, and are therefore are treated as a heavy particle mixture with a common temperature, \(T_h=T_i=T_n\). The electrons are described by a separate unique temperature \(T_e\), which is justified in Section~\ref{sec:model-scaling}.

We therfore solve for species number densities, electron and heavy particle temperatures, and the electric field to describe the plasma in the electrode region. The governing equations and the involved assumptions are described below.

\subsubsection{Species continuity}

The number densities of the three species obey
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial n_e}{\partial t}+\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_e=S,
\qquad
\frac{\partial n_i}{\partial t}+\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_i=S,
\qquad
\frac{\partial n_n}{\partial t}+\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_n=-S,
\label{eq:continuity}
\end{equation}
with the net ionization rate
\begin{equation}
S=k_{\rm ion}(T_e)\,n_en_n-k_{\rm rec}(T_e)\,n_e^2n_i .
\label{eq:source}
\end{equation}
Here \(k_{\rm ion}\) and \(k_{\rm rec}\) are the rate coefficients for electron-impact ionization and for its inverse, three-body recombination with an electron as the third body, taken from Hoffert and Lien \cite{hoffert1967quasi}. These are the only reactions retained. Every ionization event creates one electron and one ion and removes one atom, so the source is common to the electrons and ions and opposite for the neutrals.

In what follows, \(p_e=n_ek_BT_e\), \(p_i=n_ik_BT_h\), and \(p_n=n_nk_BT_h\) are the partial pressures and \(p=p_e+p_i+p_n\) is the total pressure. \(n_h=n_i+n_n\) and \(\rho=Mn_h\) are the heavy-particle number and mass densities, with \(M\) the argon mass. \(\mathbf{u}_s\) is the mean velocity of species \(s\) and \(\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_s=n_s\mathbf{u}_s\) its particle flux. \(\mathbf{u}\) is the mass-averaged heavy-particle velocity, defined by \(\rho\mathbf{u}=M(n_i\mathbf{u}_i+n_n\mathbf{u}_n)\), with the electron mass neglected. These frequencies and the remaining transport coefficients are functions of the local state \((n_e,n_i,n_n,T_e,T_h,|\mathbf{E}|)\) and are given in Appendix~\ref{app:transport}.



\subsubsection{Species momentum balances}

The momentum equation of species \(s\) is \(m_sn_s\,D_s\mathbf{u}_s/Dt=\mathbf{F}_s+\mathbf{R}_s\). Here \(\mathbf{F}_s\) are all the forces acting on species \(s\). Neglecting the inertia of the species relative to the heavy gas, \(D_s\mathbf{u}_s/Dt\simeq D\mathbf{u}/Dt\), inplies each species accelerates with the bulk plasma and there is no relative acceleration between species. That bulk acceleration stems from the heavy-gas momentum equation~(\ref{eq:heavy-momentum}), \(\rho\,D\mathbf{u}/Dt=\mathbf{F}\), where \(\mathbf{F}\) is the net force per unit volume on the plasma, defined in equation~(\ref{eq:bulk-balance}) below. Writing \(Y_s=m_sn_s/\rho\) for the mass fraction of species \(s\), so that \(Y_i=n_i/n_h\), \(Y_n=n_n/n_h\), \(Y_e=m_en_e/(Mn_h)\), and \(Y_e+Y_i+Y_n=1\), the momentum equations reduce to the local force balances \cite{zhdanov2002transport}
\begin{align}
0 &= -en_e\mathbf{E}-\nabla p_e
 - m_en_e\bar\nu^M_{en}(\mathbf{u}_e-\mathbf{u}_n)
 - m_en_e\bar\nu^M_{ei}(\mathbf{u}_e-\mathbf{u}_i)
 - C_en_ek_B\nabla T_e
 - Y_e\mathbf{F},
\label{eq:mom-e}\\
0 &= +en_i\mathbf{E}-\nabla p_i
 - Mn_i\bar\nu^M_{in}(\mathbf{u}_i-\mathbf{u}_n)
 - Mn_i\bar\nu^M_{ie}(\mathbf{u}_i-\mathbf{u}_e)
 + C_in_ek_B\nabla T_e
 - Y_i\mathbf{F},
\label{eq:mom-i}\\
0 &= -\nabla p_n
 - Mn_n\bar\nu^M_{ni}(\mathbf{u}_n-\mathbf{u}_i)
 - Mn_n\bar\nu^M_{ne}(\mathbf{u}_n-\mathbf{u}_e)
 + C_nn_ek_B\nabla T_e
 - Y_n\mathbf{F} .
\label{eq:mom-n}
\end{align}
The frequencies \(\bar\nu^M_{en}\), \(\bar\nu^M_{ei}\), \(\bar\nu^M_{in}\), \(\bar\nu^M_{ie}\), \(\bar\nu^M_{ni}\), and \(\bar\nu^M_{ne}\) are \emph{averaged momentum-transfer collision frequencies}. The superscript \(M\) denotes the average over the velocity distribution that reproduces the mobility (equivalently, the electrical conductivity) of the species. It differs from the energy-exchange averages \(\bar\nu^E\), which enter the elastic energy transfer in equations~(\ref{eq:eenergy}) and (\ref{eq:henergy}). The collision frequencies are defined in (Appendix~\ref{app:transport}). \(C_e\) is the electron thermal-diffusion coefficient, and \(C_i\) and \(C_n\) are its reactions on the ions and neutrals. Momentum conservation in each collision pair ties the frequencies together,
\begin{equation}
m_en_e\bar\nu^M_{en}=Mn_n\bar\nu^M_{ne},\qquad
m_en_e\bar\nu^M_{ei}=Mn_i\bar\nu^M_{ie},\qquad
n_i\bar\nu^M_{in}=n_n\bar\nu^M_{ni},
\label{eq:newton3}
\end{equation}
and the thermal-diffusion forces sum to zero, \(C_i+C_n=C_e\). The force that the electron temperature gradient exerts on the electrons is therefore exactly balanced by its reaction on the two heavy species.

Adding equations~(\ref{eq:mom-e})--(\ref{eq:mom-n}) and using equation~(\ref{eq:newton3}) removes every collisional and thermal-diffusion term. The species' own electric and pressure forces add up to the net force on the plasma,
\begin{equation}
\sum_s\mathbf{F}_s=\mathbf{F}\equiv e(n_i-n_e)\mathbf{E}-\nabla p .
\label{eq:bulk-balance}
\end{equation}
Because \(\sum_sY_s=1\), the remaining terms cancel identically, and the sum reduces to \(0=0\). The \(Y_s\mathbf{F}\) terms are what make the species balances consistent with a heavy gas that accelerates under the net force~(\ref{eq:bulk-balance}). Because their sum is an identity, equations~(\ref{eq:mom-e})--(\ref{eq:mom-n}) contain only two independent relations, which determine two relative velocities. The third relation is obtained by the definition of the bulk velocity \(\mathbf{u}\), and neglecting the electron mass.
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{u}= \frac{n_i\mathbf{u}_i+n_n\mathbf{u}_n}{n_i+n_n}
\label{eq:frame}
\end{equation}
Two of equations~(\ref{eq:mom-e})--(\ref{eq:mom-n}) then determine the electron and ion velocities relative to the heavy gas. Equation~(\ref{eq:frame}) determines the neutral velocity once the frame velocity \(\mathbf{u}\) is known from the heavy-gas momentum equation, equation~(\ref{eq:heavy-momentum}).

\subsubsection{Species fluxes}

\emph{Electrons.} With \(\bar\nu^M_e=\bar\nu^M_{en}+\bar\nu^M_{ei}\) the total electron momentum-transfer frequency, solving equation~(\ref{eq:mom-e}) for \(\mathbf{u}_e\) and expanding \(\nabla p_e=k_BT_e\nabla n_e+n_ek_B\nabla T_e\) gives
\begin{equation}
\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_e=-\frac{en_e}{m_e\bar\nu^M_e}\mathbf{E}
-\frac{k_BT_e}{m_e\bar\nu^M_e}\nabla n_e
-(1+C_e)\,\frac{n_ek_B}{m_e\bar\nu^M_e}\nabla T_e
+\frac{n_e}{\bar\nu^M_e}\Big(\bar\nu^M_{en}\mathbf{u}_n+\bar\nu^M_{ei}\mathbf{u}_i\Big).
\label{eq:flux-e}
\end{equation}
The first two terms are ordinary drift and diffusion. Their coefficients, \(e/(m_e\bar\nu^M_e)\) and \(k_BT_e/(m_e\bar\nu^M_e)\), are the magnitudes of the electron mobility and diffusivity, and because both are set by the same frequency they satisfy the Einstein relation identically. The third term combines the temperature-gradient part of the electron pressure force with the additional thermal-diffusion (Soret) force \(C_e\). The last term is the collisional drag exerted on the electrons by the moving heavy species.

\emph{Ions.} The ion balance~(\ref{eq:mom-i}) contains the friction against the neutrals, \(\propto\mathbf{u}_i-\mathbf{u}_n\), and against the electrons, \(\propto\mathbf{u}_i-\mathbf{u}_e\). Through the frame condition~(\ref{eq:frame}), \(\mathbf{u}_n-\mathbf{u}=-(n_i/n_n)(\mathbf{u}_i-\mathbf{u})\), so \(\mathbf{u}_i-\mathbf{u}_n=(n_h/n_n)(\mathbf{u}_i-\mathbf{u})\), while \(\mathbf{u}_i-\mathbf{u}_e=(\mathbf{u}_i-\mathbf{u})-(\mathbf{u}_e-\mathbf{u})\). The total friction on the ions is therefore \(-Mn_i\tilde\nu^M_i(\mathbf{u}_i-\mathbf{u})+Mn_i\bar\nu^M_{ie}(\mathbf{u}_e-\mathbf{u})\), with the effective ion momentum-transfer frequency
\begin{equation}
\tilde\nu^M_i=\frac{n_h}{n_n}\,\bar\nu^M_{in}+\bar\nu^M_{ie} .
\label{eq:nu-i-eff}
\end{equation}
Solving equation~(\ref{eq:mom-i}) for \(\mathbf{u}_i\) then gives the ion flux
\begin{equation}
\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_i=\frac{en_i}{M\tilde\nu^M_i}\mathbf{E}
-\frac{k_BT_h}{M\tilde\nu^M_i}\nabla n_i
-\frac{n_ik_B}{M\tilde\nu^M_i}\nabla T_h
+C_i\,\frac{n_ek_B}{M\tilde\nu^M_i}\nabla T_e
-\frac{n_i}{n_h}\frac{\mathbf{F}}{M\tilde\nu^M_i}
+n_i\Big[\mathbf{u}+\frac{\bar\nu^M_{ie}}{\tilde\nu^M_i}(\mathbf{u}_e-\mathbf{u})\Big].
\label{eq:flux-i}
\end{equation}
Every ion transport term is governed by the single frequency \(\tilde\nu^M_i\). Its ion--neutral part is enhanced by the factor \(n_h/n_n\ge1\) over the bare charge-exchange frequency \(\bar\nu^M_{in}\): an ion drifting relative to the heavy gas is matched, through the frame condition, by a counter-flow of neutrals, which raises the ion--neutral relative velocity. In the weakly ionized limit, \(n_i\ll n_n\), \(\tilde\nu^M_i\to\bar\nu^M_{in}+\bar\nu^M_{ie}\). The enhancement stays finite as \(n_n\to0\), because \(\bar\nu^M_{in}\propto n_n\). The third term is ion diffusion driven by the heavy-particle temperature gradient. The fourth is the reaction of the ions to the electron thermal-diffusion force. The fifth is the ions' share, \(Y_i\mathbf{F}\), of the net force on the plasma. This share accelerates the ions together with the rest of the heavy gas and so does not drive them relative to it. Relative to the ion's own driving force, it is of order the ionization degree \(n_i/n_h\). In the fully ionized limit (\(n_n\to0\), \(Y_i\to1\)) it cancels the ion's own electric and pressure forces, leaving \(en_e\mathbf{E}+\nabla p_e\), the reaction to the force on the electrons. The ion motion relative to the frame is then set entirely by electron--ion friction, as momentum conservation requires. The last term is advection by the heavy gas together with the electron--ion drag.

\emph{Neutrals.} Multiplying the frame condition~(\ref{eq:frame}) by \(n_n\) gives the neutral flux directly,
\begin{equation}
\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_n=n_h\mathbf{u}-\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_i ,
\label{eq:flux-n}
\end{equation}
so no independent neutral transport coefficient is required. The neutral balance~(\ref{eq:mom-n}) is not needed separately, since it follows from the other two balances and the identity obtained by summing all three. In the absence of heavy-gas motion, \(\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_n=-\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_i\): every ion carried towards the electrode is returned as a neutral atom.

With the drag, thermal-diffusion, and mixture-acceleration terms removed (\(C_e=C_i=0\), \(Y_i\mathbf{F}=0\), \(\mathbf{u}_s=\mathbf{u}=0\) in the drag terms, \(n_i\ll n_n\)), equations~(\ref{eq:flux-e}) and (\ref{eq:flux-i}) reduce to the classical drift-diffusion fluxes, with mobilities \(e/(m_e\bar\nu^M_e)\) and \(e/[M(\bar\nu^M_{in}+\bar\nu^M_{ie})]\). Unlike those fluxes, they retain the full pressure-gradient force of each species in a non-isothermal plasma.

\subsubsection{Electrostatic field}

The electric field is obtained from Poisson's equation,
\begin{equation}
\nabla\cdot(\varepsilon_0\mathbf{E})=e(n_i-n_e),
\qquad
\mathbf{E}=-\nabla\phi,
\label{eq:poisson}
\end{equation}
which is solved over the entire computational domain. The transition from the quasi-neutral bulk plasma to the space-charge region is thereby captured without imposing a priori sheath boundaries.

\subsubsection{Electron energy}

The electron thermal energy density \(\mathcal{E}=\tfrac32n_ek_BT_e\) obeys
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial\mathcal{E}}{\partial t}+\nabla\cdot\mathbf{h}_e
=-e\,\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_e\cdot\mathbf{E}
-\frac32n_ek_B(T_e-T_h)\,\frac{2m_e}{M}\,\bar\nu^E_{eh}
-\varepsilon_{\rm ion}S ,
\label{eq:eenergy}
\end{equation}
with the electron energy flux
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{h}_e=\frac52k_BT_e\,\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_e
-\kappa_e\nabla T_e
+k_BT_e\,n_e\Big[C_i(\mathbf{u}_e-\mathbf{u}_i)+C_n(\mathbf{u}_e-\mathbf{u}_n)\Big].
\label{eq:heatflux-e}
\end{equation}
The terms on the right-hand side of equation~(\ref{eq:eenergy}) are Joule heating by the field acting on the total (drift plus diffusion) electron flux, and elastic energy transfer to both heavy species at the averaged energy-exchange frequency \(\bar\nu^E_{eh}=\bar\nu^E_{en}+\bar\nu^E_{ei}\). The superscript \(E\) marks the energy-exchange average over the electron distribution. It differs from the momentum-transfer (mobility) averages \(\bar\nu^M\) of equations~(\ref{eq:mom-e})--(\ref{eq:mom-n}) and is evaluated in Appendix~\ref{app:transport}. The last term is the ionization-energy sink of the net ionization rate, with \(\varepsilon_{\rm ion}=15.76\)~eV the first ionization potential of argon. Three-body recombination returns the energy that ionization removes. The energy flux~(\ref{eq:heatflux-e}) is the sum of the enthalpy flux at \(\tfrac52k_BT_e\) per electron, Fourier conduction with the zero-current electron thermal conductivity \(\kappa_e\), and a thermal-diffusion heat flux. The last of these is the Onsager reciprocal of the thermal-diffusion forces in equations~(\ref{eq:mom-i})--(\ref{eq:mom-n}) and carries the same coefficients \(C_i\) and \(C_n\). Radiation losses are usually small \cite{DinulescuPfender1980,Almeida2008} and are not included.

\subsubsection{Heavy-particle energy}

The common heavy-particle temperature is governed by
\begin{equation}
n_hc_v\left(\frac{\partial T_h}{\partial t}+\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla T_h\right)
=\nabla\cdot(\kappa_h\nabla T_h)
+\frac32n_ek_B(T_e-T_h)\,\frac{2m_e}{M}\,\bar\nu^E_{eh}
+Q_{in}
-n_hk_BT_h\,\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u},
\label{eq:henergy}
\end{equation}
where \(c_v=\tfrac32k_B\) is the translational heat capacity per heavy particle and \(\kappa_h\) is the heavy-particle thermal conductivity (Appendix~\ref{app:transport}). The elastic-exchange term is the exact negative of the electron loss in equation~(\ref{eq:eenergy}), with the same \(\bar\nu^E_{eh}\) and the same local values, so the heavy gas gains exactly the energy the electrons lose. The ionization energy is charged entirely to the electrons and does not appear here. The ion--neutral frictional heating is
\begin{equation}
Q_{in}=Mn_i\,\bar\nu^M_{in}\,|\mathbf{u}_i-\mathbf{u}_n|^2 ,
\label{eq:Qin}
\end{equation}
which is positive definite and reduces to the Joule dissipation of the ion drift when the neutral gas is at rest. It differs from \(\mathbf{E}\cdot\mathbf{j}_i\), which can be negative in a strong sheath field where the field does work against ions diffusing up their own density gradient. The last term in equation~(\ref{eq:henergy}) is the compressive work of the heavy gas.

\subsubsection{Heavy-gas momentum}

The mass-averaged heavy-gas velocity is advanced by an inviscid single-fluid momentum equation,
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial(\rho\mathbf{u})}{\partial t}+\nabla\cdot(\rho\mathbf{u}\mathbf{u})
=-\nabla p+e(n_i-n_e)\mathbf{E},
\qquad
p=n_ek_BT_e+n_hk_BT_h ,
\label{eq:heavy-momentum}
\end{equation}
that is, \(\rho\,D\mathbf{u}/Dt=\mathbf{F}\), with \(\mathbf{F}\) the net force~(\ref{eq:bulk-balance}); the conservative form follows from heavy-particle mass conservation, shown below. This equation governs the motion of the heavy gas as a whole. It also supplies the mixture acceleration whose shares \(Y_s\mathbf{F}\) enter the species balances~(\ref{eq:mom-e})--(\ref{eq:mom-n}), which in turn govern only the motion of each species relative to the heavy gas. Because electron inertia is negligible, the electric and pressure forces on the electrons are transmitted to the heavy gas by friction, so both the electron pressure and the net space-charge force appear on the right-hand side. Together with the neutral continuity equation and the flux~(\ref{eq:flux-n}), equation~(\ref{eq:heavy-momentum}) implies \(\partial n_h/\partial t+\nabla\cdot(n_h\mathbf{u})=0\): heavy-particle mass is conserved exactly, since ionization only converts atoms into ions. The total pressure relaxes acoustically rather than instantaneously. If the inertial terms are dropped, \(\mathbf{F}=0\): the \(Y_s\mathbf{F}\) terms vanish, and the algebraic mechanical-equilibrium constraint \(\nabla p=e(n_i-n_e)\mathbf{E}\) is recovered, which is the limit underlying constant-pressure (Dalton's-law) closures for the neutral density. Viscous stresses are not included.

The state advanced in time is therefore \(\{n_e,n_i,n_n,T_e,T_h,\mathbf{u},\phi\}\). It is governed by the continuity equations~(\ref{eq:continuity})--(\ref{eq:source}) with the fluxes~(\ref{eq:flux-e}), (\ref{eq:flux-i}), and (\ref{eq:flux-n}); Poisson's equation~(\ref{eq:poisson}); the electron and heavy-particle energy equations~(\ref{eq:eenergy})--(\ref{eq:Qin}); and the heavy-gas momentum equation~(\ref{eq:heavy-momentum}).

\subsection{Boundary conditions}
\label{sec:model-bc}

The system is closed by conditions at the two ends of the domain: the electrode surface, \(x=0\), and the edge of the near-electrode layer where the solution is matched to the adjacent LTE bulk plasma, \(x=L\). Below, \(\hat{\mathbf{n}}\) denotes the unit normal at the electrode pointing into the wall.

At the electrode, the surface is taken to be absorbing and non-emitting, and is held at the wall temperature, \(T_h(0)=T_{\rm wall}\), with the potential referenced to zero, \(\phi(0)=0\). Electrons are removed at the one-sided (Maxwellian) thermal-effusion flux,
\begin{equation}
\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_e\cdot\hat{\mathbf{n}}=\tfrac14n_e\bar c_e,
\qquad
\bar c_e=\sqrt{\frac{8k_BT_e}{\pi m_e}},
\label{eq:bc-electron}
\end{equation}
and each absorbed electron removes a mean energy of \(2k_BT_e\), so that \(\mathbf{h}_e\cdot\hat{\mathbf{n}}=2k_BT_e\,\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_e\cdot\hat{\mathbf{n}}\). Ions are collected at their thermal-effusion flux augmented by any drift directed into the wall,
\begin{equation}
\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_i\cdot\hat{\mathbf{n}}=\tfrac14n_i\bar c_i+\max\!\Big(\frac{en_i}{M\tilde\nu^M_i}\mathbf{E}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{n}},\,0\Big),
\qquad
\bar c_i=\sqrt{\frac{8k_BT_h}{\pi M}},
\label{eq:bc-ion}
\end{equation}
which reduces to the thermal flux when the local field does not drive ions into the electrode. This treatment is consistent with the field-aware treatment of the counter-diffusing sheath-edge current used elsewhere \cite{Benilov2016,LiangTrelles}. The wall is impermeable to the heavy gas, \(\mathbf{u}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{n}}=0\), so equation~(\ref{eq:flux-n}) gives
\begin{equation}
\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_n\cdot\hat{\mathbf{n}}=-\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_i\cdot\hat{\mathbf{n}} :
\label{eq:bc-neutral}
\end{equation}
every ion neutralized at the surface re-enters the plasma as an atom. Thermionic and secondary electron emission are not included in equation~(\ref{eq:bc-electron}) at present (Section~\ref{sec:methods-bc}), so the electron supply at a hot thermionic cathode is expected to be under-predicted.

At the outer edge, \(x=L\), the electron and ion densities and both temperatures are set to Dirichlet values taken from the adjacent LTE bulk solution: \(n_e=n_e^{\rm bulk}\), \(n_i=n_i^{\rm bulk}\), \(T_e=T_e^{\rm bulk}\), and \(T_h=T_{\rm bulk}\). The neutral density follows from Dalton's law at the background pressure \(p_0\),
\begin{equation}
n_n=\frac{p_0-n_ek_BT_e}{k_BT_h}-n_i ,
\label{eq:bc-neutral-density}
\end{equation}
and the heavy-gas velocity is given a zero normal gradient. The arc current is imposed through the electric field: the total current density at the boundary, conduction plus displacement, is held equal to the applied current density \(J_{\rm app}\),
\begin{equation}
e\left(\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_i-\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_e\right)\cdot\hat{\mathbf{e}}_x+\varepsilon_0\frac{\partial E_x}{\partial t}=J_{\rm app},
\label{eq:bc-current}
\end{equation}
which is integrated in time as an evolution equation for the boundary field. The initial field enters only as the initial condition of this equation.

\subsection{Quasi-Maxwellian electrons near the electrode}
\label{sec:model-scaling}

Even where the heavy species are strongly out of equilibrium, the electron
population can remain closely Maxwellian provided electron--electron and
electron--ion collisions are frequent enough to redistribute directed
(drift) energy into the isotropic thermal spread faster than electron--heavy
collisions or the imposed field can distort it. Following Dinulescu and
Pfender's analysis of the anode boundary layer \cite{DinulescuPfender1980},
two conditions must hold. First, electron--electron/ion thermalization must
dominate electron--neutral momentum transfer, weighted by the mass ratio
that makes each electron--neutral collision inefficient at removing energy:
\begin{equation}
\sum_h \frac{m_e}{m_h}\,\frac{\bar\nu_{eh}}{\bar\nu_{ee}} \ll 1 .
\label{eq:maxw1}
\end{equation}
Second, the ordered drift energy imparted by the field must remain small
compared with the electron thermal energy, weighted by the same mass ratio:
\begin{equation}
\left(\frac{U_e}{\bar c_e}\right)^2 \ll \frac{m_e}{m_h} .
\label{eq:maxw2}
\end{equation}
For a representative near-anode condition, $T_e\sim1$~eV, $n_e\sim
5\times10^{22}\,\mathrm{m^{-3}}$, neutral density $N\sim6\times10^{23}\,
\mathrm{m^{-3}}$, electron--atom momentum-transfer cross section
$\bar Q_{ea}=2\times10^{-20}\,\mathrm{m^2}$, and mean thermal speed
$\bar c_e = 7\times10^5\,\mathrm{m/s}$: the electron--ion/electron--electron
frequency from the NRL formulary (with $\ln\Lambda\approx3$) is
$\nu_{ee}\sim\nu_{ei}\sim5\times10^{11}\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$, while the
electron--neutral frequency is $\nu_{en} = \bar c_e N \bar Q_{ea} \approx
8\times10^{9}\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$, i.e.\ $\nu_{en}/\nu_{ee}\sim0.016$; with
$m_e/M_{\rm Ar}\approx1.4\times10^{-5}$, condition~(\ref{eq:maxw1}) gives
$\sim2\times10^{-7}\ll1$. For condition~(\ref{eq:maxw2}), the electrical
conductivity of the layer, $\sigma\approx5000$~S/m, corresponds to an
electron momentum-transfer frequency $\bar\nu^M_e = e^2n_e/(m_e\sigma)\approx3\times10^{11}\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$;
with a representative current density $J\sim10^7\,\mathrm{A/m^2}$, the
electron drift speed is $U_e = J/(en_e) \approx1.3\times10^3\,\mathrm{m/s}$,
so $(U_e/\bar c_e)^2\approx3\times10^{-6}$, again well below
$m_e/M_{\rm Ar}\approx1.4\times10^{-5}$. Both conditions are comfortably
satisfied, so the electron population is treated as quasi-Maxwellian
throughout the near-electrode layer, and a single electron temperature
$T_e$ (rather than a fully kinetic electron energy distribution) is
retained in equation~(\ref{eq:eenergy}) there as well as in the bulk.

\subsection{Length scales and the thickness of the near-electrode layer}
\label{sec:model-lengths}

The same parameters fix a hierarchy of length scales that separates the
space-charge sheath from the broader near-electrode non-equilibrium region
that must be resolved around it. The electron Debye length,
\begin{equation}
\lambda_{De} = \sqrt{\frac{\varepsilon_0 T_e}{n_e e}} \approx 3\times10^{-8}
\ \mathrm{m} ,
\label{eq:debye}
\end{equation}
sets the scale of the space-charge sheath itself. The electron mean free
path against the combined electron--neutral and electron--electron/ion
collision frequency used above,
\begin{equation}
\lambda_e = \frac{\bar c_e}{\nu_{en}+\nu_{ee}} \approx 1\times10^{-6}\
\mathrm{m} ,
\label{eq:mfp}
\end{equation}
is $\sim30$ times $\lambda_{De}$, confirming that the sheath is thin
compared with a mean free path and can support the non-collisional
electron kinetics discussed in Section~\ref{sec:model-collisionality}. The
electron--heavy energy-relaxation length, over which an electron transfers
its excess thermal energy to the heavy gas through the inefficient
mass-ratio-weighted elastic channel in equation~(\ref{eq:eenergy}), is
larger still,
\begin{equation}
\lambda_T = \frac{\lambda_e}{\sqrt{2m_e/M}} \approx 2\times10^{-4}\
\mathrm{m} .
\label{eq:thermal-length}
\end{equation}
Finally, the diffusion length that controls how far the electron/ion density
profile itself can depart from its local equilibrium value under a given
field, used directly by Lowke and Tanaka's mesh-sizing rule
\cite{Lowke2006}, is $\Lambda = (kT_e/e)/E$; with $T_e\approx1$~V and a
field $E = J/\sigma \approx (10^7\,\mathrm{A/m^2})/(5000\,\mathrm{S/m}) =
2000$~V/m consistent with the parameters above,
\begin{equation}
\Lambda \approx 5\times10^{-4}\ \mathrm{m} .
\label{eq:diffusion-length}
\end{equation}
The four scales satisfy $\lambda_{De} \ll \lambda_e \ll \Lambda \sim
\lambda_T$, the same ordering (Debye length $\ll$ mean free path $\ll$
boundary-layer thickness) reported directly in Table~I of
\cite{DinulescuPfender1980} for the anode boundary layer of a comparable
arc, $\lambda_{De}\sim(2$--$3)\times10^{-8}$~m,
$\lambda_e\sim(1$--$2)\times10^{-6}$~m, boundary-layer thickness
$L\sim0.2$--$0.6$~mm. On this basis we take a near-electrode layer of
thickness $\delta \approx 0.2$~mm --- comparable to both $\Lambda$ and
$\lambda_T$, and consistent with the range of $L$ found in
\cite{DinulescuPfender1980} --- as the region over which the unified,
non-equilibrium model of Section~\ref{sec:methods} must be applied; outside
it, the plasma is close enough to collision-dominated equilibrium for the
LTE bulk treatment of Section  to hold. Note that this
$\sim$0.2~mm layer is not itself the space-charge sheath: it is the outer,
thermal/diffusion-dominated non-equilibrium region (corresponding to layers
2--3 of the four-sublayer structure found by \cite{Almeida2008}) inside
which a much thinner space-charge sheath, of order $\lambda_{De}$ to a few
times $\lambda_{De}$, is nested.

\subsection{Is the sheath collisionless?}
\label{sec:model-collisionality}

A collisionless sheath is the standard simplifying assumption used to close
the NLTE-sheath and two-temperature ionization-layer-sheath approaches of
Section~\ref{sec:intro-electrode}, and it is tempting to expect it to hold
automatically at atmospheric pressure, where collision rates are high.
Almeida, Benilov and Naidis show this expectation is wrong: computing the
number of ion collisions actually incurred while crossing the sheath gives
values from order unity to $10^4$, not $\ll1$, even for atmospheric-pressure
argon \cite{Almeida2008}. The reason is that the sheath thickness itself
scales with the \emph{local} Debye length, $\lambda_{De}\propto
n_e^{-1/2}$, which is set by the arc's own high electron density and
shrinks faster, as the arc becomes hotter and more strongly ionized, than
the neutral mean free paths shrink with pressure (the Ramsauer minimum in
the argon electron--neutral cross section keeps $\lambda_e$ from falling as
quickly as $\lambda_{De}$). A collisionless sheath at high pressure is
therefore not evidence of a crude or incomplete model; it is close to the
generic outcome for a dense atmospheric-pressure arc, and the question that
actually needs answering species by species and quantity by quantity is
\emph{how} collisionless, and for what.

We make this quantitative with four Knudsen numbers, each the ratio of the
relevant mean free path to the resolved sheath width $s$: $\mathrm{Kn}_e =
\lambda_e/s$ (do electrons collide crossing the sheath at all);
$\mathrm{Kn}_\varepsilon = \lambda_T/s$ (electron--heavy energy exchange:
does a local electron energy balance exist inside the sheath);
$\mathrm{Kn}_{ee} = \lambda_{ee}/s$ (electron--electron thermalization:
does a well-defined $T_e$, i.e.\ a locally Maxwellian electron energy
distribution, exist inside the sheath at all -- a distinct and much more
restrictive question than $\mathrm{Kn}_\varepsilon$, since $\lambda_{ee}$
and $\lambda_T$ differ by roughly $10^4$); and $\mathrm{Kn}_i =
\lambda_i/s$ for the ion charge-exchange mean free path. For the converged,
resolved-sheath operating point of the \textsc{PlasmeX} test case discussed
in Section~\ref{sec:methods} ($s = 1.65\times10^{-7}$~m $= 5.6\,
\lambda_{De}$, $T_e\approx0.98$~eV at the wall), these evaluate to
$\mathrm{Kn}_e\approx63$, $\mathrm{Kn}_\varepsilon\approx1.2\times10^4$,
$\mathrm{Kn}_{ee}\approx5.3$, and $\mathrm{Kn}_i\approx0.8$--$1.7$: the
sheath is collisionless for electron momentum and electron--heavy energy
exchange by a wide margin, only marginally collisionless for electron
thermalization, and carries of order one ion collision, consistent with the
lower end of the $10^2$--$10^4$ range quoted by \cite{Almeida2008} for a
somewhat different case. Despite $\mathrm{Kn}_{ee}\sim5$ being only
marginal, an electron population entering a purely retarding, elastic
potential barrier is mapped onto itself by Liouville's theorem: reflection
by an energy-conserving barrier preserves a Maxwellian at unchanged $T_e$
while depleting the density by the Boltzmann factor, giving a testable,
parameter-free reference prediction, $T_e(x) = T_{e,\rm edge} = {\rm
const}$ across the sheath, against which the local closure used in
equation~(\ref{eq:eenergy}) can be checked directly (Section~\ref{sec:methods}).
No such collisionless reference state exists for the ions, which do not see
a retarding barrier and for which $\mathrm{Kn}_i = O(1)$ rules out both the
collisionless limit and full collisional equilibrium; ion behaviour in the
sheath is therefore treated as an explicit target for the resolved
drift-diffusion solution rather than assumed a priori in either limit.
