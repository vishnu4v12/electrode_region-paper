\section{Physical model and scale analysis}
\label{sec:model}

\subsection{Spatial scale analysis}
\label{sec:scales involved}

In the electrode regions the bulk plasma is perturbed by the presence of relatively cold and absorbing nature of electrode surfaces resulting in steep gradients in plasma properties. The bulk plasma therefore progressively deviates from the LTE conditions as the electrode is approached. We estimate the length scales over which these departures develop in a high-intensity atmospheric pressure arc considering LTE plasma adjacent to the electrodes at temperatures from \(23\,000\)~K, typical of the cathode region, down to \(11\,000\)~K, typical of the anode region \cite{Hsu1983,haddad1984temperature}.

In the bulk plasma, the electron number density \(n_e\) is governed by ionization and recombination. Let \(n_n\) be the number density of argon atoms, \(n_i=n_e\) that of the ions, and \(k_{\rm ion}\) and \(k_{\rm rec}\) the rate coefficients of electron-impact ionization and of three-body recombination. The electron density then evolves as \(\dot n_e=k_{\rm ion}n_en_n-k_{\rm rec}n_e^3\) (equation~(\ref{eq:S})). Linearizing this rate about equilibrium at fixed pressure \(p\) and temperature \(T\), where Dalton's law gives \(\delta n_n=-2\,\delta n_e\), yields the reaction time \(\tau_{\rm rxn}=1/(2k_{\rm rec}n_e^2+2k_{\rm ion}n_e)\). During this time, the charged particles diffuse ambipolarly over the reaction length \(\lambda_{\rm rxn}=\sqrt{D_a\tau_{\rm rxn}}\), where \(D_a\) is the ambipolar diffusion coefficient, equation~(\ref{eq:Da}). A plasma displaced from ionization equilibrium therefore travels a distance of order \(\lambda_{\rm rxn}\) before reaching ionization equilibrium.

The electron and heavy-particle (ions and neutrals) temperatures in a plasma equilibrate through elastic collisions. In an elastic collision with a heavy particle, an electron loses only a fraction \(2m_e/M\) of its energy, where \(m_e\) and \(M\) are the electron and argon masses. Its energy therefore relaxes only after about \(M/(2m_e)\) collisions, during which it random-walks over a distance \(\lambda_T=\lambda_{eh}/\sqrt{2m_e/M}\), which is the length scale of energy relaxation between the electrons and the heavy particles. Here \(\lambda_{eh}=1/(n_n\overline{Q}_{en}+n_i\overline{Q}_{ei})\) is the electron mean free path for heavy-particle collisions, and \(\overline{Q}_{en}\) and \(\overline{Q}_{ei}\) are the \rev{average} momentum-transfer cross sections \rev{of} electron--atom and electron--ion collisions over the electron energy distribution (Appendix~\ref{app:cross}). Ions and atoms, by contrast, have nearly equal masses and exchange energy efficiently, so their temperatures equalize over \(\lambda_{T_h}=1/[(n_i+n_n)\overline{Q}_{in}]\), where \(\overline{Q}_{in}\) is the \rev{average} ion--atom momentum-transfer cross section \rev{over} the ion energy distribution, dominated by resonant charge exchange (equation~(\ref{eq:Qia})).

The smallest scale is the electron Debye length \(\lambda_{De}=\sqrt{\varepsilon_0k_BT_e/(n_ee^2)}\), which is of order \(10^{-8}\)~m and governs the extent of charge separation. Here \(\varepsilon_0\) is the vacuum permittivity, \(k_B\) the Boltzmann constant and \(e\) the elementary charge. These scales are calculated for the temperatures representative of the cathode and anode regions and are listed in table~\ref{tab:scales}, together with the electron mean free path \(\lambda_e=1/(n_n\overline{Q}_{en}+n_i\overline{Q}_{ei}+n_e\overline{Q}_{ee})\) and the ion mean free path \(\lambda_i=1/(n_n\overline{Q}_{in}+n_i\overline{Q}_{ii})\), where \(\overline{Q}_{ee}=\overline{Q}_{ei}\) and \(\overline{Q}_{ii}\) is the \rev{average} Coulomb cross section \(\overline{Q}_{ei}\) evaluated at \(T_h\).

The resulting hierarchy of length scales is \(\lambda_{De}\ll\lambda_e\lesssim\lambda_i\lesssim\lambda_{T_h}\ll\lambda_{\rm rxn}\approx\lambda_T\). The largest scales, \(\lambda_{\rm rxn}\) and \(\lambda_T\), indicate that the electrode affects the plasma over a region of thickness of order \(10^{-4}\)~m. Other models and analyses \cite{Almeida2008,DinulescuPfender1980,benilov1995model,hsu1983analysis,tashiro2010numerical,semenov2016numerical} arrive at similar estimates. Consistent with them, Lowke and Tanaka \cite{Lowke2006} found that departures from LTE are confined to within about 0.1~mm of the electrodes of a 200~A atmospheric-pressure argon arc, and suggested that the rest of the plasma can be modeled as being in LTE. Because \(\lambda_e\) and \(\lambda_i\) are small compared with this region, a continuum description with collision dominated transport is reasonable over the arc end of the near-electrode region, and because \(\lambda_{T_h}\) is small as well a single temperature \(T_h\) can be used to describe the heavy species. The validity of both assumptions close to the electrodes is examined a posteriori in Section~\ref{sec:res-momentum}.

The temperature and current density at the plasma end are taken as typical values from experiments and LTE models \cite{Lowke2006,Hsu1983,LiangTrelles,lago2004numerical,freton2000comparison,korsipati2026fast,haddad1984temperature}. For these conditions, LTE assumption from a length of \(5\times10^{-4}~\mathrm{m}\) from the electrodes is a reasonable choice based on scales estimates  and previous studies \cite{Almeida2008,DinulescuPfender1980,benilov1995model,hsu1983analysis,tashiro2010numerical,semenov2016numerical}.

\subsection{Governing equations}
\label{sec:model-governing}
\rev{This section describes the governing equations of the plasma in the electrode region.} Externally imposed bulk flows, such as the cathode jet, are not modeled in the electrode region. The heavy gas is nevertheless not assumed to be at rest, since it can move during transients. The plasma is taken to consist of three species, namely electrons (e), singly charged argon ions (i), and argon atoms (n). Multiply charged ions are neglected, because the temperature in the anode region is too low for thier production, and in the cathode region even though they are present, they are shown to have a negligible effect \cite{almeida2000simulation}. 
The number densities of the three species obey
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial n_e}{\partial t}+\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_e=S,
\qquad
\frac{\partial n_i}{\partial t}+\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_i=S,
\qquad
\frac{\partial n_n}{\partial t}+\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_n=-S,
\label{eq:continuity}
\end{equation}
where \(\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_s=n_s\mathbf{u}_s\) is the flux of species \(s\) and \(S\) is the net ionization rate. The latter accounts for electron-impact ionization and its inverse, three-body recombination with an electron as the third particle,
\begin{equation}
S=k_{\rm ion}\,n_en_n-k_{\rm rec}\,n_e^2n_i .
\label{eq:S}
\end{equation}
The rate coefficients \(k_{\rm ion}\) and \(k_{\rm rec}\) have been evaluated in different ways by different authors \cite{Almeida2008,DinulescuPfender1980,Lowke2006,LiangTrelles}. In this work we use those of Almeida et al.\ \cite{Almeida2008}, taken from Benilov and Naidis \cite{benilov1998ionization}. In their model, ionization proceeds mainly stepwise, through the first excited level. The escape of resonance radiation, which reduces the ionization rate, is accounted for when \(n_e\lesssim10^{21}~\mathrm{m^{-3}}\), and \(k_{\rm rec}\) follows from \(k_{\rm ion}\) by detailed balance with Saha equilibrium at \(T_e\).

The momentum equation for the plasma as a whole is \(\rho\,D\mathbf{u}/Dt=\mathbf{F}\), where \(\mathbf{F}\) is the net force on the plasma. For each species \(s\), the corresponding equation is \(m_sn_s\,D_s\mathbf{u}_s/Dt=\mathbf{F}_s\), where \(\mathbf{F}_s\) is the net force on that species. We neglect the inertia of each species relative to the heavy gas, \(D_s\mathbf{u}_s/Dt\simeq D\mathbf{u}/Dt\), so that every species shares the acceleration of the heavy gas and there is no relative acceleration between species. The inertia of species \(s\) is then \(Y_s\mathbf{F}\), where \(Y_s=m_sn_s/\rho\) is its mass fraction, and the species momentum equations reduce to the local force balances \cite{zhdanov2002transport}
\begin{align}
0 &= -en_e\mathbf{E}-\nabla p_e
 - m_en_e\bar\nu^M_{en}(\mathbf{u}_e-\mathbf{u}_n)
 - m_en_e\bar\nu^M_{ei}(\mathbf{u}_e-\mathbf{u}_i)
 - C_en_ek_B\nabla T_e
 - Y_e\mathbf{F},
\label{eq:mom-e}\\
0 &= +en_i\mathbf{E}-\nabla p_i
 - Mn_i\bar\nu^M_{in}(\mathbf{u}_i-\mathbf{u}_n)
 - Mn_i\bar\nu^M_{ie}(\mathbf{u}_i-\mathbf{u}_e)
 + C_in_ek_B\nabla T_e
 - Y_i\mathbf{F},
\label{eq:mom-i}\\
0 &= -\nabla p_n
 - Mn_n\bar\nu^M_{ni}(\mathbf{u}_n-\mathbf{u}_i)
 - Mn_n\bar\nu^M_{ne}(\mathbf{u}_n-\mathbf{u}_e)
 + C_nn_ek_B\nabla T_e
 - Y_n\mathbf{F} .
\label{eq:mom-n}
\end{align}
The frequencies \(\bar\nu^M_{en}\), \(\bar\nu^M_{ei}\), \(\bar\nu^M_{in}\), \(\bar\nu^M_{ie}\), \(\bar\nu^M_{ni}\), and \(\bar\nu^M_{ne}\) are \emph{averaged momentum-transfer collision frequencies} within the collision pairs, the superscript \(m\) has been used to distinguish them from the \emph{averaged energy-transfer collision frequencies} \(\bar\nu^E_{ab}\), \(C_e\) is the electron thermal-force coefficient, and \(C_i\) and \(C_n\) are its reactions on the ions and atoms. Their calculation is described in Appendix~\ref{app:transport}. Momentum conservation in each collision pair relates the frequencies, and the thermal forces sum to zero,
\begin{equation}
m_en_e\bar\nu^M_{en}=Mn_n\bar\nu^M_{ne},\qquad
m_en_e\bar\nu^M_{ei}=Mn_i\bar\nu^M_{ie},\qquad
n_i\bar\nu^M_{in}=n_n\bar\nu^M_{ni},\qquad
C_i+C_n=C_e .
\label{eq:newton3}
\end{equation}
Adding equations~(\ref{eq:mom-e})--(\ref{eq:mom-n}) and using equation~(\ref{eq:newton3}) gives the total momentum equation,
\begin{equation}
\rho\,D\mathbf{u}/Dt = e(n_i-n_e)\mathbf{E}-\nabla p .
\label{eq:bulk-balance}
\end{equation}
In practice, equations~(\ref{eq:mom-e}), (\ref{eq:mom-i}) and (\ref{eq:bulk-balance}) are solved instead of (\ref{eq:mom-n}), and the neutral flux is obtained from the definition of the heavy-gas velocity \(\mathbf{u}\) as the mass-weighted average of the ion and neutral velocities,
\begin{equation}
\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_n= (n_i+n_n)\mathbf{u}-n_i\mathbf{u}_i.
\label{eq:flux-n}
\end{equation}

Solving the electron balance~(\ref{eq:mom-e}) for the electron flux, with \(\bar\nu^M_e=\bar\nu^M_{en}+\bar\nu^M_{ei}\) the total electron momentum-transfer frequency and \(\nabla p_e=k_BT_e\nabla n_e+n_ek_B\nabla T_e\), gives
\begin{equation}
\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_e=-\frac{en_e}{m_e\bar\nu^M_e}\mathbf{E}
-\frac{k_BT_e}{m_e\bar\nu^M_e}\nabla n_e
-(1+C_e)\,\frac{n_ek_B}{m_e\bar\nu^M_e}\nabla T_e
+\frac{n_e}{\bar\nu^M_e}\Big(\bar\nu^M_{en}\mathbf{u}_n+\bar\nu^M_{ei}\mathbf{u}_i\Big),
\label{eq:flux-e}
\end{equation}
where the negligible term \(Y_e\mathbf{F}\) has been dropped. The first two terms are the drift and diffusion fluxes. Their coefficients, \(e/(m_e\bar\nu^M_e)\) and \(k_BT_e/(m_e\bar\nu^M_e)\), are the magnitudes of the electron mobility and diffusivity. Because both are set by the same frequency, they satisfy the Einstein relation identically. The third term combines the temperature-gradient part of the electron pressure force with the thermal force. The last term is the collisional drag exerted on the electrons by the moving heavy species.

The ion flux follows from the ion balance~(\ref{eq:mom-i}) in a similar way,
\begin{equation}
\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_i=\frac{en_i}{M\bar\nu^M_i}\mathbf{E}
-\frac{k_BT_h}{M\bar\nu^M_i}\nabla n_i
-\frac{n_ik_B}{M\bar\nu^M_i}\nabla T_h
+C_i\,\frac{n_ek_B}{M\bar\nu^M_i}\nabla T_e
-\frac{n_i}{n_h}\frac{\mathbf{F}}{M\bar\nu^M_i}
+\frac{n_i}{\bar\nu^M_i}\Big(\bar\nu^M_{in}\mathbf{u}_n+\bar\nu^M_{ie}\mathbf{u}_e\Big).
\label{eq:flux-i}
\end{equation}
The first three terms are ion drift, diffusion, and diffusion driven by the heavy-particle temperature gradient, with mobility \(e/(M\bar\nu^M_i)\) and diffusivity \(k_BT_h/(M\bar\nu^M_i)\). The fourth term is the reaction of the ions to the electron thermal force. The fifth term is the ions' share, \(Y_i\mathbf{F}\), of the net force on the plasma, which accelerates them together with the rest of the heavy gas. The last term is the collisional drag exerted on the ions by the atoms and electrons. The velocities in the drag terms of equations~(\ref{eq:flux-e}) and (\ref{eq:flux-i}) are those of the other species, so the two fluxes are coupled. If the drag, thermal-force and mixture-acceleration terms are removed (\(C_e=C_i=0\), \(Y_i\mathbf{F}=0\), and \(\mathbf{u}_s=\mathbf{u}=0\) in the drag terms), equations~(\ref{eq:flux-e}) and (\ref{eq:flux-i}) reduce to the conventional drift diffusion equations. Unlike the classical fluxes, however, they retain the full pressure-gradient force of each species in a non-isothermal plasma.

The electric field is obtained from Poisson's equation,
\begin{equation}
\nabla\cdot(\varepsilon_0\mathbf{E})=e(n_i-n_e),
\qquad
\mathbf{E}=-\nabla\phi,
\label{eq:poisson}
\end{equation}
which is solved over the entire computational domain, so that the transition from the quasi-neutral bulk plasma to the space-charge region is captured without imposing the sheath boundaries a priori.

The electron thermal energy density \(\mathcal{E}=\tfrac32n_ek_BT_e\) obeys
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial\mathcal{E}}{\partial t}+\nabla\cdot\mathbf{h}_e
=-e\,\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_e\cdot\mathbf{E}
-\frac32n_ek_B(T_e-T_h)\,\frac{2m_e}{M}\,\bar\nu^E_{eh}
-\varepsilon_{\rm ion}S
-w_{\rm rad}
-Q_e ,
\label{eq:eenergy}
\end{equation}
with the electron energy flux
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{h}_e=\frac52k_BT_e\,\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_e
-\kappa_e\nabla T_e
+k_BT_e\,n_e\Big[C_i(\mathbf{u}_e-\mathbf{u}_i)+C_n(\mathbf{u}_e-\mathbf{u}_n)\Big].
\label{eq:heatflux-e}
\end{equation}
The terms on the right-hand side of equation~(\ref{eq:eenergy}) are Joule heating by the field, elastic energy transfer to the heavy species, the ionization-energy sink, the net radiative loss, and the radial heat loss of the electrons. In the elastic term, \(\bar\nu^E_{eh}=\bar\nu^E_{en}+\bar\nu^E_{ei}\), where the superscript \(E\) denotes the \emph{averaged energy-transfer collision frequencies} (Appendix~\ref{app:transport}). The ionization sink is the net ionization rate multiplied by \(\varepsilon_{\rm ion}=15.76\)~eV, the first ionization potential of argon. The energy flux~(\ref{eq:heatflux-e}) is the sum of the enthalpy flux at \(\tfrac52k_BT_e\) per electron, Fourier conduction with the zero-current electron thermal conductivity \(\kappa_e\), and a thermal-diffusion heat flux \cite{zhdanov2002transport}. The net radiative loss \(w_{\rm rad}\) is that of Almeida et al.\ \cite{Almeida2008}, taken from Benilov and Naidis \cite{benilov1998ionization}.

A one-dimensional domain has no lateral boundary, so the Joule heat generated away from the electrode can leave only by radiation and by conduction through the two ends of the domain. In a real arc, by contrast, the bulk plasma can lose heat radially, by conduction \cite{elenbaas1935ahnlichkeitsgesetze}. We therefore represent the radial conduction of a column of radius \(R\) by a volumetric loss for each energy channel \cite{elenbaas1935ahnlichkeitsgesetze},
\begin{equation}
Q_e=\Lambda\,(\kappa_e+\kappa_R)\,(T_e-T_{\rm edge}),
\qquad
Q_h=\Lambda\,\kappa_h\,(T_h-T_{\rm edge}),
\qquad
\Lambda=\frac{4}{R^2},
\label{eq:radial-loss}
\end{equation}
where \(T_{\rm edge}\) is the temperature at the edge of the column. Ion pairs also diffuse radially out of the hot core and recombine near the cold edge, releasing their ionization energy there. This heat loss is represented by the reactive thermal conductivity \(\kappa_R\) (Appendix~\ref{app:lte-check}), which is assigned to the electron channel \cite{Freton2012}. \rev{The radius \(R\) is a parameter of the model. We take it as the radius of a uniform column that carries the arc current \(I\) at the applied current density, \(I=\pi R^2J_{\rm app}\), so that \(R=\sqrt{I/(\pi J_{\rm app})}\).} Since the loss scales as \(\kappa T/R^2\), it is most important where the plasma is hot and the attachment is small, as in the cathode spot. \rev{The radius therefore differs between the cathode and the anode, and it changes with the current density (Section~\ref{sec:results}).} Closer to the electrodes, the axial gradients develop over \(10^{-6}\)--\(10^{-4}\)~m, whereas \(R\) is of order \(10^{-3}\)~m, so the radial losses are comparatively negligible there.

The common heavy-particle temperature is governed by
\begin{equation}
n_hc_v\left(\frac{\partial T_h}{\partial t}+\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla T_h\right)
=\nabla\cdot(\kappa_h\nabla T_h)
+\frac32n_ek_B(T_e-T_h)\,\frac{2m_e}{M}\,\bar\nu^E_{eh}
+Q_{in}
-Q_h
-n_hk_BT_h\,\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u},
\label{eq:henergy}
\end{equation}
where \(c_v=\tfrac32k_B\) is the translational heat capacity per heavy particle and \(\kappa_h\) is the heavy-particle thermal conductivity (Appendix~\ref{app:transport}). The elastic-exchange term is the same as in the electron energy equation~(\ref{eq:eenergy}), but with the opposite sign. The ionization energy and the radiative loss are assigned entirely to the electrons and do not appear here. The term \(Q_h\) is the radial heat loss of the heavy particles, equation~(\ref{eq:radial-loss}), and the ion--neutral frictional heating is
\begin{equation}
Q_{in}=Mn_i\,\bar\nu^M_{in}\,|\mathbf{u}_i-\mathbf{u}_n|^2 ,
\label{eq:Qin}
\end{equation}
which is positive definite and reduces to the Joule dissipation of the ion drift when the neutral gas is at rest. The last term in equation~(\ref{eq:henergy}) is the compression work of the heavy gas.

The velocity of the heavy gas as a whole is governed by an inviscid single-fluid momentum equation,
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial(\rho\mathbf{u})}{\partial t}+\nabla\cdot(\rho\mathbf{u}\mathbf{u})
=-\nabla p+e(n_i-n_e)\mathbf{E},
\qquad
p=n_ek_BT_e+n_hk_BT_h .
\label{eq:heavy-momentum}
\end{equation}

We therefore solve for following qunatities, \(\{n_e, n_i, n_n, \mathcal{E}, T_h, \rho u, \phi\}\). They are governed by the continuity equations~(\ref{eq:continuity}) with the fluxes~(\ref{eq:flux-e}), (\ref{eq:flux-i}) and (\ref{eq:flux-n}), Poisson's equation~(\ref{eq:poisson}), the electron and heavy-particle energy equations~(\ref{eq:eenergy}) and (\ref{eq:henergy}), and the heavy-gas momentum equation~(\ref{eq:heavy-momentum}).
