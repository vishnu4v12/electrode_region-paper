\section{Transport coefficients}
\label{app:transport}


This appendix describes how the transport and energy-exchange coefficients that close the model are calculated.


\subsection{Electrons}
Following Mitchner and Kruger \cite{mitchner1973partially}, the electron current density \(\mathbf{J}_e=-e\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_e\) and the electron heat flux \(\mathbf{q}_e\) are written as
\begin{align}
\mathbf{J}_e &= \sigma\,(\mathbf{E}+\nabla p_e/(en_e))+\varphi\,\nabla T_e,
\label{eq:mk-J}\\
\mathbf{q}_e &= -\lambda\nabla T_e+\psi\,\mathbf{J}_e.
\label{eq:mk-q}
\end{align}
Here \(\sigma\) is the electrical conductivity, \(\varphi\) is the thermal-diffusion coefficient, \(\lambda\) is the ordinary (zero-current) thermal conductivity, and \(\psi\) is the energy transported by the current per unit charge. Comparing equations~(\ref{eq:mk-J}) and (\ref{eq:mk-q}) with the electron momentum balance~(\ref{eq:mom-e}) and the energy flux~(\ref{eq:heatflux-e}) gives
\begin{equation}
\bar\nu^M_e=\frac{n_ee^2}{m_e\sigma},\qquad
C_e=\frac{e}{k_B}\frac{\varphi}{\sigma},\qquad
\kappa_e=\lambda,\qquad
-\frac{e\psi}{k_BT_e}=\frac52+C_e .
\label{eq:mk-map}
\end{equation}
Mitchner and Kruger \cite{mitchner1973partially} give \(\sigma\), \(\varphi\), and \(\lambda\) in the weakly and fully ionized limits. Substituting them into equation~(\ref{eq:mk-map}) gives the collision frequencies and thermal-diffusion coefficients in each limit.

\emph{Weakly ionized limit.} Electron--neutral collisions dominate, and the electrons behave as a Lorentz gas whose distribution function has a Maxwellian isotropic part \(f_M(T_e)\). Let \(C\) be the electron speed, \(\varepsilon=\tfrac12m_eC^2\) the electron energy, \(x=\varepsilon/(k_BT_e)\) the reduced energy, and \(\nu_{en}=n_nCQ^{(1)}(\varepsilon)\) the energy-dependent momentum-transfer frequency, where \(Q^{(1)}\) is the momentum-transfer cross section. A two-term solution of the electron Boltzmann equation then gives
\begin{equation}
\bar\nu^M_{en}=\frac{\langle C^2\rangle_0}{\langle C^2/\nu_{en}\rangle_0},\qquad
C_{en}=\langle x\rangle_\nu-\frac52,\qquad
\lambda_{en}=\frac{n_ek_B^2T_e}{m_e\bar\nu^M_{en}}\left[\langle x^2\rangle_\nu-\langle x\rangle_\nu^2\right],
\label{eq:nu-M}
\end{equation}
where \(\bar\nu^M_{en}\) is the momentum-transfer average of \(\nu_{en}\), \(\langle\cdot\rangle_0\) is the Maxwellian average, and \(\langle\cdot\rangle_\nu\) is the transport average with the weight \(C^4f_M/\nu_{en}\):
\begin{equation}
\langle g\rangle_0=4\pi\int_0^\infty g\,C^2f_M\,dC,\qquad
\langle g\rangle_\nu=\frac{\langle g\,C^2/\nu_{en}\rangle_0}{\langle C^2/\nu_{en}\rangle_0} .
\label{eq:lorentz-avg}
\end{equation}
The elastic energy exchange in equations~(\ref{eq:eenergy}) and (\ref{eq:henergy}) uses instead the energy-exchange average
\begin{equation}
\bar\nu^E_{en}=\frac{\langle C^2\nu_{en}\rangle_0}{\langle C^2\rangle_0} .
\label{eq:nu-E}
\end{equation}
All averages are computed from the argon momentum-transfer cross section \(Q^{(1)}\) of the LXCat database \cite{lxcat}.

\emph{Fully ionized limit.} For singly charged ions, the Spitzer--H\"arm solution gives \cite{mitchner1973partially}
\begin{equation}
\bar\nu^M_{ei}=\frac{\bar\nu^E_{ei}}{1.975},\qquad
C_{ei}=\frac{1.389}{1.975}=0.703,\qquad
\lambda_{ei}=3.203\,\frac{n_ek_B^2T_e}{m_e\bar\nu^E_{ei}},
\label{eq:spitzer}
\end{equation}
where \(\bar\nu^E_{ei}\) is the energy-exchange average~(\ref{eq:nu-E}) of the Coulomb collision frequency \cite{mitchner1973partially},
\begin{equation}
\bar\nu^E_{ei}=n_i\,\frac{4\sqrt{2\pi}}{3}\left(\frac{m_e}{k_BT_e}\right)^{3/2}
\left(\frac{e^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0m_e}\right)^2\ln\Lambda ,
\label{eq:nu-ei}
\end{equation}
and \(\ln\Lambda\) is the Coulomb logarithm,
\begin{equation}
\ln\Lambda=\max\left(2,\ \ln\frac{\lambda_{De}}{b_{90}}\right),\qquad
\lambda_{De}=\sqrt{\frac{\varepsilon_0k_BT_e}{n_ee^2}},\qquad
b_{90}=\frac{e^2}{12\pi\varepsilon_0k_BT_e} .
\label{eq:coulomb-log}
\end{equation}
Here \(\lambda_{De}\) is the electron Debye length and \(b_{90}\) is the \(90^\circ\)-deflection impact parameter. The floor at 2 keeps \(\ln\Lambda\), and hence \(\bar\nu^E_{ei}\), positive when the plasma is not weakly coupled.

At intermediate ionization, the two limits are combined. The momentum-transfer frequencies are added, the thermal conductivities are combined in series (their thermal resistances add), and the thermal-diffusion coefficient is the mean of the two limits weighted by the energy-exchange frequencies:
\begin{equation}
\bar\nu^M_e=\bar\nu^M_{en}+\bar\nu^M_{ei},\qquad
\frac{1}{\kappa_e}=\frac{1}{\lambda_{en}}+\frac{1}{\lambda_{ei}},\qquad
C_e=\frac{C_{en}\bar\nu^E_{en}+C_{ei}\bar\nu^E_{ei}}{\bar\nu^E_{en}+\bar\nu^E_{ei}} .
\label{eq:bridge}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Ions}

The ion--neutral transport is governed by resonant charge exchange. The momentum-transfer cross section is taken from the fit \cite{benilov1998ionization}
\begin{equation}
\overline{Q}_{in}=2\left[7\times10^{-10}-0.6\times10^{-10}\ln\left(\frac{2k_BT_h}{e}\right)\right]^2\ \mathrm{m^2},
\label{eq:Qia}
\end{equation}
with \(2k_BT_h/e\) in eV. The corresponding low-field mobility is obtained from the Mason--Schamp equation \cite{mason1958mobility},
\begin{equation}
\mu_i^0=\frac{1.104\times10^{5}}{\overline{Q}_{in}\sqrt{T_h}\,n_n}\ \ \mathrm{m^2\,V^{-1}\,s^{-1}}
\qquad(\overline{Q}_{in}\ \mathrm{in\ m^2},\ T_h\ \mathrm{in\ K},\ n_n\ \mathrm{in\ m^{-3}}).
\label{eq:mu_i0}
\end{equation}
To account for the effect of strong electric fields on the ion mobility, a closed-form two-temperature Wannier formulation \cite{mancinelli2018modelling,viehland1978gaseous} is used, in which the drift speed \(v_d\) and the effective ion temperature \(T_{\rm eff}\) are determined self-consistently:
\begin{equation}
v_d^2=\frac{\sqrt{1+4\mathcal{A}\mathcal{B}}-1}{2\mathcal{B}},\qquad
\mathcal{A}=\left(\mu_i^0|\mathbf{E}|\right)^2,\qquad
\mathcal{B}=\frac{M}{3k_BT_h},
\label{eq:wannier}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
T_{\rm eff}=T_h+\frac{Mv_d^2}{3k_B},\qquad
\mu_{in}=\mu_i^0\sqrt{\frac{T_h}{T_{\rm eff}}} .
\label{eq:mu-in}
\end{equation}
The ion--neutral momentum-transfer frequency is then obtained from the mobility as
\begin{equation}
\bar\nu^M_{in}=\frac{e}{M\mu_{in}} .
\label{eq:nu-in}
\end{equation}

Figure~\ref{fig:mobility} compares the reduced mobility \(K_0=\mu n_n/N_0\) (with \(N_0=2.687\times10^{25}\ \mathrm{m^{-3}}\)) of the single-temperature mobility \(\mu_i^0\), equation~(\ref{eq:mu_i0}), and the two-temperature mobility \(\mu_{in}\), equation~(\ref{eq:mu-in}), with the Monte Carlo results of Nikitovi\'c and Raspopovi\'c \cite{nikitovic2026cross} for Ar\(^+\) in Ar at 300~K. These Monte Carlo results agree with the measured drift-tube mobilities \cite{nikitovic2026cross}. At \(T_h\) fixed, \(K_0\) depends only on \(E/N\). At low field, both formulations give \(K_0=1.54\ \mathrm{cm^2\,V^{-1}\,s^{-1}}\), 2\,\% below the Monte Carlo value. Above about 30~Td, the single-temperature mobility stays constant and overestimates \(K_0\) by a factor of 1.6 at 300~Td and 4 at 3000~Td. The two-temperature mobility reproduces the decrease of \(K_0\) with \(E/N\), and lies 15\,\% below the Monte Carlo value at 300~Td and 26\,\% below at 3000~Td. Most of this remaining difference comes from evaluating \(\overline{Q}_{in}\) at \(T_h\) instead of \(T_{\rm eff}\). Evaluating it at \(T_{\rm eff}\) brings the two-temperature mobility within 7\,\% of the Monte Carlo values from 1 to 7000~Td, but then \(T_{\rm eff}\) has to be found iteratively instead of in closed form.

The ion--electron frequency follows from momentum conservation, equation~(\ref{eq:newton3}),
\begin{equation}
\bar\nu^M_{ie}=\frac{m_en_e}{Mn_i}\,\bar\nu^M_{ei},
\label{eq:nu-ie}
\end{equation}
with \(\bar\nu^M_{ei}\) given by equation~(\ref{eq:spitzer}). The ions take up the electron--ion part of the electron thermal force, so their thermal coefficient is the \(C_{ei}\) term of equation~(\ref{eq:bridge}),
\begin{equation}
C_i=\frac{C_{ei}\bar\nu^E_{ei}}{\bar\nu^E_{en}+\bar\nu^E_{ei}} .
\label{eq:Ci}
\end{equation}
The ion contribution \(\kappa_i\) to the heavy-particle thermal conductivity is calculated as in Almeida et al.\ \cite{Almeida2008}.


\subsection{Heavy species}

The atom frequencies follow from momentum conservation, equation~(\ref{eq:newton3}),
\begin{equation}
\bar\nu^M_{ni}=\frac{n_i}{n_n}\,\bar\nu^M_{in},\qquad
\bar\nu^M_{ne}=\frac{m_en_e}{Mn_n}\,\bar\nu^M_{en}.
\label{eq:nu-n}
\end{equation}
The atoms take up the electron--neutral part of the electron thermal force, so their thermal coefficient is the \(C_{en}\) term of equation~(\ref{eq:bridge}),
\begin{equation}
C_n=\frac{C_{en}\bar\nu^E_{en}}{\bar\nu^E_{en}+\bar\nu^E_{ei}} ,
\label{eq:Cn}
\end{equation}
and equations~(\ref{eq:Ci}) and (\ref{eq:Cn}) satisfy \(C_i+C_n=C_e\), as required by equation~(\ref{eq:newton3}). The heavy-particle thermal conductivity \(\kappa_h=\kappa_n+\kappa_i\), the sum of the atom and ion contributions, is calculated as in Almeida et al.\ \cite{Almeida2008}.

The electrical conductivity \(\sigma=n_ee^2/(m_e\bar\nu^M_e)\) and the thermal conductivity obtained with these coefficients are compared with the transport properties of argon in local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE) in figure~\ref{fig:lte-check}. The coefficients are evaluated with \(T_e=T_h=T\) and the Saha composition at atmospheric pressure, and compared with the data tabulated by Boulos et al.\ \cite{boulos2023handbook}. Along the axis, the model has no reactive thermal conductivity: the energy carried by the diffusion of ion pairs and released on recombination appears through the ambipolar flux and the ionization source (\(\kappa_R\) enters only the radial loss~(\ref{eq:radial-loss})). For the comparison, the reactive conductivity \(\kappa_R\) computed with Mutation++ \cite{scoggins2020mutation} is therefore added to \(\kappa_e+\kappa_h\).

The electrical conductivity agrees well with the LTE data over most of the temperature range, with only minor deviations in the low-temperature regime where electron--neutral collisions dominate. At intermediate temperatures, however, the additive rule for the collision frequencies overestimates the conductivity. This discrepancy arises because, in argon, the slow electrons associated with the Ramsauer minimum contribute substantially to the current in the electron--neutral limit, while these same electrons are strongly scattered by ions. Simply adding the averaged electron--neutral and electron--ion collision frequencies therefore fails to capture the correlation between the two collision processes. The thermal conductivity also shows good agreement with the reference data in the high-temperature regime, with a modest overprediction at intermediate temperatures.
