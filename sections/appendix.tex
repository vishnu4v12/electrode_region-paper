\section{Appendix A. Transport Coefficients}
\label{app:transport}

This appendix gives the transport and exchange coefficients that close the governing equations of Section~\ref{sec:model-governing}: the electron momentum-transfer and energy-exchange collision frequencies, thermal-diffusion coefficients, and thermal conductivity; the ion momentum-transfer frequencies; and the heavy-particle heat capacity and thermal conductivity. Every coefficient is a closed-form or tabulated function of the local state.

\subsection{Electron transport: general framework}

The electron current density \(\mathbf{J}_e=-e\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_e\) and heat flux \(\mathbf{q}_e\) obey a linear-response (Onsager) relation with two driving forces. These are the generalized field \(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{E}}=\mathbf{E}+\nabla p_e/(en_e)\) and the temperature gradient:
\begin{align}
\mathbf{J}_e &= \sigma\,\boldsymbol{\mathcal{E}}+\varphi\,\nabla T_e,
\label{eq:onsager-J}\\
\mathbf{q}_e &= -\frac52\frac{k_BT_e}{e}\mathbf{J}_e-\lambda'\nabla T_e-T_e\varphi\,\boldsymbol{\mathcal{E}}
=-\lambda\nabla T_e+\psi\,\mathbf{J}_e,
\label{eq:onsager-q}
\end{align}
with
\begin{equation}
\lambda=\lambda'-T_e\frac{\varphi^2}{\sigma},\qquad
\psi=-\frac52\frac{k_BT_e}{e}-T_e\frac{\varphi}{\sigma}.
\label{eq:onsager-lambda}
\end{equation}
Here \(\sigma\) is the electrical conductivity, \(\varphi\) the thermal-diffusion coefficient, \(\lambda'\) the thermal conductivity at fixed \(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{E}}\), and \(\lambda\) the ordinary (zero-current) thermal conductivity. Comparing equations~(\ref{eq:onsager-J})--(\ref{eq:onsager-lambda}) with the electron momentum balance~(\ref{eq:mom-e}) and the energy flux~(\ref{eq:heatflux-e}) identifies
\begin{equation}
\bar\nu^M_e=\frac{n_ee^2}{m_e\sigma},\qquad
C_e=\frac{e}{k_B}\frac{\varphi}{\sigma},\qquad
\kappa_e=\lambda,\qquad
\frac52+C_e=-\frac{e\psi}{k_BT_e}.
\label{eq:onsager-map}
\end{equation}
The conductivity entering the energy equation is the zero-current value \(\lambda\), not \(\lambda'\). A temperature gradient alone drives a current \(\varphi\nabla T_e\), and holding \(\mathbf{J}_e=0\) requires a field \(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{E}}=-(\varphi/\sigma)\nabla T_e\). The heat flux carried by this field reduces the apparent conduction from \(\lambda'\) to \(\lambda\). Because equation~(\ref{eq:eenergy}) accounts separately for Joule heating and the enthalpy flux, its conductivity must be the zero-current one. The coefficients \(\sigma\), \(\varphi\), and \(\lambda\) are evaluated in the partially and fully ionized limits and then bridged.

\subsection{Partially ionized limit: electron--neutral transport}

When electron--neutral collisions dominate, the electrons form a Lorentz gas. The isotropic part of the distribution is a Maxwellian \(f_M(T_e)\), and only the first-order anisotropic correction responds to the field and the temperature gradient. With \(C\) the electron speed, \(x=m_eC^2/(2k_BT_e)\), and \(\nu_{eH}(C)=n_nCQ^{(1)}(\varepsilon)\) the energy-dependent electron--neutral momentum-transfer frequency built from the momentum-transfer cross section \(Q^{(1)}(\varepsilon)\), a two-term solution of the electron Boltzmann equation gives the anisotropic correction
\begin{equation}
f^1=\frac{e}{m_e\nu_{eH}}\frac{\partial f_M}{\partial C}\boldsymbol{\mathcal{E}}
-\frac{C}{\nu_{eH}}f_M\left[\nabla\ln p_e+\left(x-\tfrac52\right)\nabla\ln T_e\right].
\label{eq:lorentz-f1}
\end{equation}
Two different kinds of velocity average appear below, and they are given different notation. The first is the plain average over the Maxwellian, normalized to unity,
\begin{equation}
\langle g\rangle_0=\int g\,f_M\,d^3C=4\pi\int_0^\infty g\,C^2f_M\,dC .
\label{eq:maxw-avg}
\end{equation}
The second is the \emph{transport average}. Every current and conduction heat-flux moment of equation~(\ref{eq:lorentz-f1}) carries, besides the Maxwellian, an extra factor \(C^2/\nu_{eH}\), so the transport coefficients are expressed through averages under the weight \(C^4f_M/\nu_{eH}\):
\begin{equation}
\langle g\rangle_\nu=\frac{\langle g\,C^2/\nu_{eH}\rangle_0}{\langle C^2/\nu_{eH}\rangle_0}
=\frac{\int_0^\infty g\,C^4f_M\nu_{eH}^{-1}\,dC}{\int_0^\infty C^4f_M\nu_{eH}^{-1}\,dC} .
\label{eq:lorentz-avg}
\end{equation}
In energy, this weight is \(\varepsilon\,e^{-\varepsilon/k_BT_e}/Q^{(1)}(\varepsilon)\). The transport average is used only for the dimensionless moments \(\langle x\rangle_\nu\) and \(\langle x^2\rangle_\nu\) in equation~(\ref{eq:lorentz-coeffs}) below. It is \emph{not} the average that defines the collision frequencies.

The collision frequencies in the model are Maxwellian averages \(\langle\cdot\rangle_0\) of \(\nu_{eH}(C)\), and two different ones are needed. Both use the same weight, \(C^2f_M\), that is, the Maxwellian weighted by the electron kinetic energy. They differ in whether \(\nu_{eH}\) or \(1/\nu_{eH}\) is averaged. The \emph{momentum-transfer (mobility) average}, denoted by the superscript \(M\), is the harmonic mean of \(\nu_{eH}\) under this weight. It reproduces the mobility and is the frequency used in the force balances~(\ref{eq:mom-e})--(\ref{eq:mom-n}):
\begin{equation}
\bar\nu^M_{en}=\frac{\langle C^2\rangle_0}{\langle C^2/\nu_{eH}\rangle_0}
=n_n\,\frac{3k_BT_e/m_e}{4\pi\int_0^\infty C^3f_M\,[Q^{(1)}]^{-1}\,dC}
=n_n\sqrt{\frac{2}{m_e}}\;\frac{3\sqrt\pi}{4}\;
\frac{(k_BT_e)^{5/2}}{\int_0^\infty\varepsilon\,e^{-\varepsilon/k_BT_e}\,[Q^{(1)}(\varepsilon)]^{-1}\,d\varepsilon} .
\label{eq:nu-M}
\end{equation}
The second form follows from \(\nu_{eH}=n_nCQ^{(1)}\) and \(\langle C^2\rangle_0=3k_BT_e/m_e\). The third follows from the change of variable \(\varepsilon=\tfrac12m_eC^2\), with \(f_M=(m_e/2\pi k_BT_e)^{3/2}e^{-\varepsilon/k_BT_e}\). For a constant cross section, it reduces to \(\bar\nu^M_{en}=(9\pi/32)\,\tfrac43n_n\bar c_eQ^{(1)}\approx0.88\times\tfrac43n_n\bar c_eQ^{(1)}\). Equation~(\ref{eq:nu-M}) is identical to defining \(\bar\nu^M_{en}\) through the Lorentz conductivity of \cite{mitchner1973partially} (their equation VIII~2.14a), \(\sigma_{en}=n_ee^2/(m_e\bar\nu^M_{en})\). This is how it is evaluated in practice.
The \emph{energy-exchange average}, denoted by the superscript \(E\), is the arithmetic mean of \(\nu_{eH}\) under the same weight:
\begin{equation}
\bar\nu^E_{en}=\frac{\langle C^2\nu_{eH}\rangle_0}{\langle C^2\rangle_0}
=\frac43\,n_n\bar c_e\,\overline{Q}^{(1,1)}_{en}(T_e),
\qquad
\bar c_e=\sqrt{\frac{8k_BT_e}{\pi m_e}} .
\label{eq:nu-ea}
\end{equation}
Because a harmonic mean never exceeds the arithmetic mean over the same weight, \(\bar\nu^M_{en}\le\bar\nu^E_{en}\), with equality only for an energy-independent \(\nu_{eH}\). The denominator of equation~(\ref{eq:nu-M}) is also the normalizing integral of the transport average~(\ref{eq:lorentz-avg}). With \(\langle C^2\rangle_0=3k_BT_e/m_e\), the Lorentz-gas coefficients are
\begin{equation}
\sigma_{en}=\frac{n_ee^2}{m_e\bar\nu^M_{en}},\qquad
\left(\frac{\varphi}{\sigma}\right)_{en}=\frac{k_B}{e}\left(\langle x\rangle_\nu-\frac52\right),\qquad
\lambda_{en}=\frac{n_ek_B^2T_e}{m_e\bar\nu^M_{en}}\left[\langle x^2\rangle_\nu-\langle x\rangle_\nu^2\right].
\label{eq:lorentz-coeffs}
\end{equation}
The electron--neutral thermal-diffusion coefficient \((e/k_B)(\varphi/\sigma)_{en}=\langle x\rangle_\nu-\tfrac52\) vanishes for an energy-independent collision frequency. Its sign and magnitude therefore reflect the energy dependence of \(Q^{(1)}\), which for argon is dominated by the Ramsauer minimum.

The energy-exchange average~(\ref{eq:nu-ea}) is the one that gives the elastic energy-loss rate of a Maxwellian electron population, \(\tfrac{2m_e}{M}\langle\varepsilon\,\nu_{eH}\rangle_0(1-T_h/T_e)=\tfrac32k_B(T_e-T_h)\tfrac{2m_e}{M}\bar\nu^E_{en}\). For argon between 0.5 and 2~eV, \(\bar\nu^E_{en}\) exceeds \(\bar\nu^M_{en}\) by a factor of 4--4.5, because energy exchange is dominated by fast electrons above the Ramsauer minimum, while the mobility is dominated by slow electrons trapped inside it. Using \(\bar\nu^E_{en}\) in the force balances would therefore overestimate the electron--neutral friction by this factor, and using \(\bar\nu^M_{en}\) in the energy equations would underestimate the elastic loss by the same factor. Equations~(\ref{eq:lorentz-avg})--(\ref{eq:nu-ea}) are evaluated on the argon momentum-transfer cross section \cite{lxcat} and tabulated as functions of \(T_e\), in reduced form per unit neutral density.

\subsection{Fully ionized limit and the Coulomb logarithm}

When electron--ion collisions dominate, the Spitzer--H\"arm solution for a singly charged ion gives \cite{mitchner1973partially}
\begin{equation}
\sigma_{ei}=1.975\,\frac{n_ee^2}{m_e\bar\nu^E_{ei}},\qquad
\lambda_{ei}=3.203\,\frac{n_ek_B^2T_e}{m_e\bar\nu^E_{ei}},\qquad
\varphi_{ei}=1.389\,\frac{n_eek_B}{m_e\bar\nu^E_{ei}},\qquad
\psi_{ei}=-3.203\,\frac{k_BT_e}{e}.
\label{eq:spitzer}
\end{equation}
The collision frequency cancels in \((e/k_B)(\varphi/\sigma)_{ei}=1.389/1.975=0.7033\), and the consistency condition of equation~(\ref{eq:onsager-map}), \(-e\psi/(k_BT_e)=3.203=\tfrac52+0.703\), is satisfied. Here \(\bar\nu^E_{ei}\) is the energy-exchange (\(C^2\)-weighted) average of the electron--ion momentum-transfer frequency,
\begin{equation}
\bar\nu^E_{ei}=n_i\,\frac{4\sqrt{2\pi}}{3}\left(\frac{m_e}{k_BT_e}\right)^{3/2}
\left(\frac{e^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0m_e}\right)^2\ln\Lambda
=2.906\times10^{-12}\,\frac{n_i[\mathrm{m^{-3}}]\,\ln\Lambda}{T_e[\mathrm{eV}]^{3/2}}\ \mathrm{s^{-1}},
\label{eq:nu-ei}
\end{equation}
with the Coulomb logarithm
\begin{equation}
\ln\Lambda=\max\left(2,\ \ln\frac{\lambda_{De}}{b_{90}}\right),\qquad
\lambda_{De}=\sqrt{\frac{\varepsilon_0T_e[\mathrm{eV}]}{n_ee}},\qquad
b_{90}=\frac{e}{12\pi\varepsilon_0T_e[\mathrm{eV}]} .
\label{eq:coulomb-log}
\end{equation}
Here \(b_{90}\) is the classical \(90^\circ\)-deflection impact parameter. The logarithm is floored at 2 to keep \(\bar\nu^E_{ei}\) finite when the plasma is not weakly coupled. The rate~(\ref{eq:nu-ei}) is set by the ion density, which is the collision partner, whereas the Debye length uses the electron density. The two coincide only under quasi-neutrality, which does not hold inside the sheath.

\subsection{Bridging the two limits}

At intermediate ionization, the electron--neutral and electron--ion contributions are combined by additive (resistance-type) mixture rules. These rules are exact in both limits and are weighted by the ratio of the energy-exchange averages of the two collision frequencies,
\begin{equation}
P=\frac{\bar\nu^E_{en}}{\bar\nu^E_{ei}} .
\label{eq:P}
\end{equation}

\emph{Momentum-transfer frequency.} The electron--ion momentum-transfer average is \(\bar\nu^M_{ei}=0.5\,\bar\nu^E_{ei}\), where the factor \(0.5\approx1/1.975\) is the strongly ionized conductivity weight of equation~(\ref{eq:spitzer}). Adding the electron--neutral and electron--ion resistivities is the same as adding the two momentum-transfer frequencies,
\begin{equation}
\bar\nu^M_e=\bar\nu^M_{en}+\bar\nu^M_{ei},\qquad
\sigma=\frac{n_ee^2}{m_e\bar\nu^M_e},
\label{eq:coulomb}
\end{equation}
with \(\bar\nu^M_{en}\) from equation~(\ref{eq:nu-M}). This is the total frequency in the electron flux~(\ref{eq:flux-e}). Relative to the electron--neutral value alone, the electron--ion (Coulomb) contribution reduces the conductivity by the factor \(\bar\nu^M_{en}/\bar\nu^M_e\).

\emph{Thermal-diffusion coefficients.} The two values of \(\varphi/\sigma\) are combined as a weighted mean in \(1/P\):
\begin{equation}
\left(\frac{\varphi}{\sigma}\right)_e=\frac{(\varphi/\sigma)_{ei}/P+(\varphi/\sigma)_{en}}{1+1/P},
\qquad
\left(\frac{\varphi}{\sigma}\right)_{ei}=\frac{1.389}{1.975}\frac{k_B}{e},
\label{eq:phi-bridge}
\end{equation}
so that
\begin{equation}
C_e=\frac{e}{k_B}\left(\frac{\varphi}{\sigma}\right)_e,\qquad
C_i=\frac{e}{k_B}\,\frac{(\varphi/\sigma)_{ei}/P}{1+1/P},\qquad
C_n=C_e-C_i .
\label{eq:Ce-Ai}
\end{equation}
Only electron--ion collisions couple momentum directly between electrons and ions. The ion reaction \(C_i\) is therefore the electron--ion (Coulomb) part of the thermal-diffusion force alone. The electron--neutral part acts on the atoms, giving \(C_n=C_e-C_i\), and the sum rule \(C_i+C_n=C_e\) used in Section~\ref{sec:model-governing} holds identically.

\emph{Thermal conductivity.} The zero-current conductivities combine as parallel thermal resistances,
\begin{equation}
\frac{1}{\lambda_e}=\frac{1}{\lambda_{en}}+\frac{1}{\lambda_{ei}},\qquad
\kappa_e=\lambda_e\equiv K\,\frac{n_ek_B^2T_e}{m_e\bar\nu^M_e} ,
\label{eq:kappa_e}
\end{equation}
with \(\bar\nu^M_e\) the total momentum-transfer frequency of equation~(\ref{eq:coulomb}). Both \(\lambda_{en}\) and \(\lambda_{ei}\) scale linearly with \(n_e\), so the dimensionless factor \(K\) is independent of the electron density. It tends to \(\langle x^2\rangle_\nu-\langle x\rangle_\nu^2\) in the electron--neutral limit and to \(3.203\,\bar\nu^M_{ei}/\bar\nu^E_{ei}=3.203/2\approx1.60\) in the fully ionized limit.

\emph{Elastic exchange frequency.} The total elastic energy-exchange frequency in equations~(\ref{eq:eenergy}) and (\ref{eq:henergy}) is the sum of the two energy-exchange averages, not of the momentum-transfer averages \(\bar\nu^M\):
\begin{equation}
\bar\nu^E_{eh}=\bar\nu^E_{en}+\bar\nu^E_{ei}.
\label{eq:nu_ex}
\end{equation}
Both terms are, by construction, the elastic energy-loss rate of a near-Maxwellian electron population. The energy lost by the electrons in equation~(\ref{eq:eenergy}) is therefore exactly the energy gained by the heavy gas in equation~(\ref{eq:henergy}).

\subsection{Ion collision frequencies}

The ion--neutral transport is governed by resonant charge exchange. The momentum-transfer cross section is taken from the fit \cite{benilov1998ionization}
\begin{equation}
\overline{Q}_{in}=2\left[7\times10^{-10}-0.6\times10^{-10}\ln\left(\frac{2k_BT_h}{e}\right)\right]^2\ \mathrm{m^2},
\label{eq:Qia}
\end{equation}
with \(2k_BT_h/e\) in eV. The corresponding low-field mobility is
\begin{equation}
\mu_i^0=\frac{1.104\times10^{5}}{\overline{Q}_{in}\sqrt{T_h}\,n_n}\ \ \mathrm{m^2\,V^{-1}\,s^{-1}}
\qquad(\overline{Q}_{in}\ \mathrm{in\ m^2},\ T_h\ \mathrm{in\ K},\ n_n\ \mathrm{in\ m^{-3}}).
\label{eq:mu_i0}
\end{equation}
The high reduced fields near the electrode heat the ions. This is accounted for by a closed-form two-temperature Wannier formulation \cite{mason1958mobility}, in which the drift speed \(v_d\) and the effective ion temperature \(T_{\rm eff}\) are determined self-consistently:
\begin{equation}
v_d^2=\frac{\sqrt{1+4\mathcal{A}\mathcal{B}}-1}{2\mathcal{B}},\qquad
\mathcal{A}=\left(\mu_i^0|\mathbf{E}|\right)^2,\qquad
\mathcal{B}=\frac{M}{3k_BT_h},
\label{eq:wannier}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
T_{\rm eff}=T_h+\frac{Mv_d^2}{3k_B},\qquad
\mu_{in}=\mu_i^0\sqrt{\frac{T_h}{T_{\rm eff}}} .
\label{eq:mu-in}
\end{equation}
Equation~(\ref{eq:wannier}) is the positive root of the quadratic in \(v_d^2\) obtained by substituting \(\mu_{in}(T_{\rm eff})\) into \(v_d=\mu_{in}|\mathbf{E}|\). In the low-field limit, \(v_d\to\mu_i^0|\mathbf{E}|\), \(T_{\rm eff}\to T_h\), and \(\mu_{in}\to\mu_i^0\). The charge-exchange data are available in this mobility form. They enter the model only through the ion--neutral momentum-transfer frequency
\begin{equation}
\bar\nu^M_{in}=\frac{e}{M\mu_{in}} .
\label{eq:nu-in}
\end{equation}

The ion--electron frequency follows from momentum conservation, equation~(\ref{eq:newton3}),
\begin{equation}
\bar\nu^M_{ie}=\frac{m_en_e}{Mn_i}\,\bar\nu^M_{ei},
\label{eq:nu-ie}
\end{equation}
with \(\bar\nu^M_{ei}=0.5\,\bar\nu^E_{ei}\) as in equation~(\ref{eq:coulomb}). Equations~(\ref{eq:nu-in}) and (\ref{eq:nu-ie}) give the total ion momentum-transfer frequency \(\bar\nu^M_i=\bar\nu^M_{in}+\bar\nu^M_{ie}\), which sets the ion mobility and diffusivity in equation~(\ref{eq:flux-i}). In the weakly ionized bulk, \(\bar\nu^M_{ie}\ll\bar\nu^M_{in}\), so \(\bar\nu^M_i\to\bar\nu^M_{in}\). As \(n_n\to0\) (near-total ionization), \(\bar\nu^M_{in}\propto n_n\) vanishes and \(\bar\nu^M_i\) is bounded below by the Coulomb-collision value \(\bar\nu^M_{ie}\), which keeps the ion mobility finite. The ion--neutral frictional heating~(\ref{eq:Qin}) uses the charge-exchange frequency \(\bar\nu^M_{in}\) of equation~(\ref{eq:nu-in}) alone, not \(\bar\nu^M_i\), which also contains the electron-friction contribution.

\subsection{Heavy-particle heat capacity and thermal conductivity}

The heat capacity per heavy particle in equation~(\ref{eq:henergy}) contains only the translational degrees of freedom, \(c_v=\tfrac32k_B\). The gas is compressible, and the work of expansion appears explicitly as \(-n_hk_BT_h\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}\). The electron energy is carried separately, and the ionization energy enters as a source in the electron energy equation. The heavy-particle thermal conductivity is the sum of an atom and an ion channel, each given by a first-approximation Chapman--Enskog expression:
\begin{equation}
\kappa_h=\kappa_n+\kappa_i,
\label{eq:kappa-h}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\kappa_n=\frac{75k_B}{64}\sqrt{\frac{\pi k_BT_h}{M}}\,\frac{n_n}{n_n\overline{Q}_{nn}+n_i\overline{Q}_{in}},
\qquad
\kappa_i=\frac{75k_B}{64}\sqrt{\frac{\pi k_BT_h}{M}}\,\frac{n_i}{n_n\overline{Q}_{in}+n_i\overline{Q}_{ii}},
\label{eq:kappa-ai}
\end{equation}
with the viscosity-type \((2,2)\) cross sections
\begin{equation}
\overline{Q}^{(2,2)}_{nn}=1.12\times10^{-18}\,T_h^{-0.2},\qquad
\overline{Q}^{(2,2)}_{in}=3.6\times10^{-18}\,T_h^{-0.3},\qquad
\overline{Q}^{(2,2)}_{ii}=\frac{e^4\ln\Lambda}{36\pi\varepsilon_0^2(k_BT_h)^2},
\label{eq:Q22}
\end{equation}
in \(\mathrm{m^2}\) with \(T_h\) in K. Here \(\ln\Lambda\) is the Coulomb logarithm of equation~(\ref{eq:coulomb-log}). The expression is regular in both limits. As \(n_n\to0\), \(\kappa_i\) tends to the pure ion--ion Coulomb value, and as \(n_i\to0\), \(\kappa_n\) tends to the neutral--neutral value.

\subsection{Reaction rate coefficients}

The ionization and three-body recombination rate coefficients \(k_{\rm ion}(T_e)\) and \(k_{\rm rec}(T_e)\) in equation~(\ref{eq:source}) are effective rate coefficients from Hoffert and Lien \cite{hoffert1967quasi}. They are tabulated in \(T_e\) and interpolated log-linearly. The energy charged to the electrons per net ionization event is \(\varepsilon_{\rm ion}=15.76\)~eV.
