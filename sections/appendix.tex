\section{Transport coefficients}
\label{app:transport}

This appendix gives the collision cross sections, describes how the transport and energy-exchange coefficients that close the model of Section~\ref{sec:model-governing} are calculated from them, and compares the resulting conductivities with the transport properties of argon in LTE.


\subsection{Collision cross sections}
\label{app:cross}
The electron--atom collisions are described by the energy-dependent momentum-transfer cross section \(Q^{(1)}(\varepsilon)\) of argon from the LXCat database \cite{lxcat}, where \(\varepsilon\) is the electron energy. The \rev{average} cross sections, denoted by an overbar, are averages over the Maxwellian energy distribution of the colliding particles. Following Almeida et al.\ \cite{Almeida2008}, the \rev{average} electron--atom and electron--ion \rev{momentum-transfer} cross sections \rev{over} the electron energy distribution are
\begin{equation}
\overline{Q}_{en}=\frac12\int_0^\infty x^2e^{-x}\,Q^{(1)}(x\,k_BT_e)\,dx,\qquad
\overline{Q}_{ei}=\frac{e^4\ln\Lambda}{32\pi\varepsilon_0^2(k_BT_e)^2},
\label{eq:Qbar-e}
\end{equation}
where \(x=\varepsilon/(k_BT_e)\) is the reduced electron energy. The second expression is the same average of the Coulomb momentum-transfer cross section. They are related to the energy-exchange frequencies by \(\bar\nu^E_{en}=\tfrac43n_n\bar C_e\overline{Q}_{en}\) and \(\bar\nu^E_{ei}=\tfrac43n_i\bar C_e\overline{Q}_{ei}\), equations~(\ref{eq:nu-E}) and (\ref{eq:nu-ei}), with \(\bar C_e=\sqrt{8k_BT_e/(\pi m_e)}\) the mean electron speed. Here \(\ln\Lambda\) is the Coulomb logarithm,
\begin{equation}
\ln\Lambda=\ln\frac{\lambda_{De}}{b_{90}},\qquad
\lambda_{De}=\sqrt{\frac{\varepsilon_0k_BT_e}{n_ee^2}},\qquad
b_{90}=\frac{e^2}{12\pi\varepsilon_0k_BT_e} ,
\label{eq:coulomb-log}
\end{equation}
where \(\lambda_{De}\) is the electron Debye length and \(b_{90}\) is the \(90^\circ\)-deflection impact parameter. 

The ion--atom collisions are governed by resonant charge exchange. Their \rev{average} momentum-transfer cross section \rev{over} the energy distribution at the heavy-particle temperature is taken from the fit \cite{benilov1998ionization}
\begin{equation}
\overline{Q}_{in}=2\left[7\times10^{-10}-0.6\times10^{-10}\ln\left(\frac{2k_BT_h}{e}\right)\right]^2\ \mathrm{m^2},
\label{eq:Qia}
\end{equation}
with \(2k_BT_h/e\) in eV. The \rev{average} electron--electron cross section \(\overline{Q}_{ee}\) is \(\overline{Q}_{ei}\), and the \rev{average} ion--ion cross section \(\overline{Q}_{ii}\) is \(\overline{Q}_{ei}\) evaluated at \(T_h\).

The scales of table~\ref{tab:scales} are evaluated with these cross sections in LTE at atmospheric pressure, \(T_e=T_h=T\), with the composition given by the Saha equation, quasi-neutrality and Dalton's law. The reaction length \(\lambda_{\rm rxn}\) uses the ambipolar diffusion coefficient
\begin{equation}
D_a=\left(1+\frac{T_e}{T_h}\right)D_{in},\qquad
D_{in}=\frac{3\pi}{32}\,\frac{\bar C_{in}}{(n_i+n_n)\,\overline{Q}_{in}},\qquad
\bar C_{in}=\sqrt{\frac{8k_BT_h}{\pi\,m_{in}}},
\label{eq:Da}
\end{equation}
where \(D_{in}\) is the ion--atom diffusion coefficient \cite{Almeida2008} and \(m_{in}=M/2\) is the ion--atom reduced mass, and the reaction time \(\tau_{\rm rxn}\) uses the rate coefficients of Almeida et al.\ \cite{Almeida2008}, from Benilov and Naidis \cite{benilov1998ionization}.


\subsection{Electrons}
\label{app:electrons}
Following Mitchner and Kruger \cite{mitchner1973partially}, the electron current density \(\mathbf{J}_e=-e\boldsymbol{\Gamma}_e\) and the electron heat flux \(\mathbf{q}_e\) are written as
\begin{align}
\mathbf{J}_e &= \sigma\,(\mathbf{E}+\nabla p_e/(en_e))+\varphi\,\nabla T_e,
\label{eq:mk-J}\\
\mathbf{q}_e &= -\lambda\nabla T_e+\psi\,\mathbf{J}_e.
\label{eq:mk-q}
\end{align}
Here \(\sigma\) is the electrical conductivity, \(\varphi\) is the thermal-diffusion coefficient, \(\lambda\) is the ordinary (zero-current) thermal conductivity, and \(\psi\) is the energy transported by the current per unit charge. Comparing equations~(\ref{eq:mk-J}) and (\ref{eq:mk-q}) with the electron momentum balance~(\ref{eq:mom-e}) and the energy flux~(\ref{eq:heatflux-e}) gives
\begin{equation}
\bar\nu^M_e=\frac{n_ee^2}{m_e\sigma},\qquad
C_e=\frac{e}{k_B}\frac{\varphi}{\sigma},\qquad
\kappa_e=\lambda,\qquad
-\frac{e\psi}{k_BT_e}=\frac52+C_e .
\label{eq:mk-map}
\end{equation}
Mitchner and Kruger \cite{mitchner1973partially} give \(\sigma\), \(\varphi\), and \(\lambda\) in the weakly and fully ionized limits. Substituting them into equation~(\ref{eq:mk-map}) gives the collision frequencies and thermal-diffusion coefficients in each limit.

\emph{Weakly ionized limit.} Electron--neutral collisions dominate, and the electrons behave as a Lorentz gas whose distribution function has a Maxwellian isotropic part \(f_M(T_e)\). Let \(C\) be the electron speed, \(\varepsilon=\tfrac12m_eC^2\) the electron energy, \(x=\varepsilon/(k_BT_e)\) the reduced energy, and \(\nu_{en}=n_nCQ^{(1)}(\varepsilon)\) the energy-dependent momentum-transfer frequency, where \(Q^{(1)}\) is the electron--atom momentum-transfer cross section (Appendix~\ref{app:cross}). A two-term solution of the electron Boltzmann equation then gives
\begin{equation}
\bar\nu^M_{en}=\frac{\langle C^2\rangle_0}{\langle C^2/\nu_{en}\rangle_0},\qquad
C_{en}=\langle x\rangle_\nu-\frac52,\qquad
\lambda_{en}=\frac{n_ek_B^2T_e}{m_e\bar\nu^M_{en}}\left[\langle x^2\rangle_\nu-\langle x\rangle_\nu^2\right],
\label{eq:nu-M}
\end{equation}
where \(\bar\nu^M_{en}\) is the momentum-transfer average of \(\nu_{en}\), \(\langle\cdot\rangle_0\) is the Maxwellian average, and \(\langle\cdot\rangle_\nu\) is the transport average with the weight \(C^4f_M/\nu_{en}\),
\begin{equation}
\langle g\rangle_0=4\pi\int_0^\infty g\,C^2f_M\,dC,\qquad
\langle g\rangle_\nu=\frac{\langle g\,C^2/\nu_{en}\rangle_0}{\langle C^2/\nu_{en}\rangle_0} .
\label{eq:lorentz-avg}
\end{equation}
The elastic energy exchange in equations~(\ref{eq:eenergy}) and (\ref{eq:henergy}) uses instead the energy-exchange average
\begin{equation}
\bar\nu^E_{en}=\frac{\langle C^2\nu_{en}\rangle_0}{\langle C^2\rangle_0} .
\label{eq:nu-E}
\end{equation}

\emph{Fully ionized limit.} For singly charged ions, the Spitzer--H\"arm solution gives \cite{mitchner1973partially}
\begin{equation}
\bar\nu^M_{ei}=\frac{\bar\nu^E_{ei}}{1.975},\qquad
C_{ei}=\frac{1.389}{1.975}=0.703,\qquad
\lambda_{ei}=3.203\,\frac{n_ek_B^2T_e}{m_e\bar\nu^E_{ei}},
\label{eq:spitzer}
\end{equation}
where \(\bar\nu^E_{ei}\) is the energy-exchange average~(\ref{eq:nu-E}) of the Coulomb collision frequency \cite{mitchner1973partially},
\begin{equation}
\bar\nu^E_{ei}=n_i\,\frac{4\sqrt{2\pi}}{3}\left(\frac{m_e}{k_BT_e}\right)^{3/2}
\left(\frac{e^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0m_e}\right)^2\ln\Lambda ,
\label{eq:nu-ei}
\end{equation}
and \(\ln\Lambda\) is the Coulomb logarithm, equation~(\ref{eq:coulomb-log}).

At intermediate ionization, the two limits are combined. The momentum-transfer frequencies are added, the thermal conductivities are combined in series (their thermal resistances add), and the thermal-diffusion coefficient is the mean of the two limits weighted by the energy-exchange frequencies,
\begin{equation}
\bar\nu^M_e=\bar\nu^M_{en}+\bar\nu^M_{ei},\qquad
\frac{1}{\kappa_e}=\frac{1}{\lambda_{en}}+\frac{1}{\lambda_{ei}},\qquad
C_e=\frac{C_{en}\bar\nu^E_{en}+C_{ei}\bar\nu^E_{ei}}{\bar\nu^E_{en}+\bar\nu^E_{ei}} .
\label{eq:bridge}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Ions}

The ion--neutral transport is governed by resonant charge exchange. The low-field mobility is obtained from the ion--atom cross section~(\ref{eq:Qia}) with the Mason--Schamp equation \cite{mason1958mobility},
\begin{equation}
\mu_i^0=\frac{1.104\times10^{5}}{\overline{Q}_{in}\sqrt{T_h}\,n_n}\ \ \mathrm{m^2\,V^{-1}\,s^{-1}}
\qquad(\overline{Q}_{in}\ \mathrm{in\ m^2},\ T_h\ \mathrm{in\ K},\ n_n\ \mathrm{in\ m^{-3}}).
\label{eq:mu_i0}
\end{equation}
In strong fields the ions are heated by their drift through the atoms. This is accounted for by the two-temperature theory of Wannier \cite{mancinelli2018modelling,viehland1978gaseous}, in which the drift of the ions relative to the atoms, \(v_d=|\mathbf{u}_i-\mathbf{u}_n|\), raises their random energy to an effective temperature \(T_{\rm eff}\), which sets the ion--atom mobility,
\begin{equation}
T_{\rm eff}=T_h+\frac{Mv_d^2}{3k_B},\qquad
\mu_{in}=\mu_i^0\sqrt{\frac{T_h}{T_{\rm eff}}} .
\label{eq:mu-in}
\end{equation}
In the ion momentum balance~(\ref{eq:mom-i}), the friction with the atoms, \(Mn_i\bar\nu^M_{in}(\mathbf{u}_i-\mathbf{u}_n)\), balances the remaining forces on the ions, a force \(eF_{in}\) per ion with
\begin{equation*}
F_{in}=\frac{1}{en_i}\left|en_i\mathbf{E}-\nabla p_i-Mn_i\bar\nu^M_{ie}(\mathbf{u}_i-\mathbf{u}_e)+C_in_ek_B\nabla T_e-Y_i\mathbf{F}\right| .
\end{equation*}
Hence \(v_d=\mu_{in}F_{in}\), and with equation~(\ref{eq:mu-in}) the drift speed follows in closed form,
\begin{equation}
v_d^2=\frac{\sqrt{1+4\mathcal{A}\mathcal{B}}-1}{2\mathcal{B}},\qquad
\mathcal{A}=\left(\mu_i^0F_{in}\right)^2,\qquad
\mathcal{B}=\frac{M}{3k_BT_h}.
\label{eq:wannier}
\end{equation}
For ions drifting in a uniform field through a gas at rest, \(F_{in}=|\mathbf{E}|\), and equations~(\ref{eq:mu-in}) and (\ref{eq:wannier}) take the form in which the closure is usually written, with \(v_d=\mu_{in}|\mathbf{E}|\) \cite{mancinelli2018modelling}. The ion--neutral momentum-transfer frequency is then obtained from the mobility as
\begin{equation}
\bar\nu^M_{in}=\frac{e}{M\mu_{in}} .
\label{eq:nu-in}
\end{equation}

Figure~\ref{fig:mobility} compares the reduced mobility \(K_0=\mu n_n/N_0\) (with \(N_0=2.687\times10^{25}\ \mathrm{m^{-3}}\)) of the single-temperature mobility \(\mu_i^0\), equation~(\ref{eq:mu_i0}), and the two-temperature mobility \(\mu_{in}\), equations~(\ref{eq:mu-in}) and (\ref{eq:wannier}) with \(F_{in}=|\mathbf{E}|\), with the Monte Carlo results of Nikitovi\'c and Raspopovi\'c \cite{nikitovic2026cross} for Ar\(^+\) in Ar at 300~K. The two-temperature mobility reproduces the decrease of \(K_0\) with \(E/N\) and lies 15\,\% below the Monte Carlo value at 300~Td and 26\,\% below at 3000~Td.

The ion--electron frequency follows from momentum conservation, equation~(\ref{eq:newton3}),
\begin{equation}
\bar\nu^M_{ie}=\frac{m_en_e}{Mn_i}\,\bar\nu^M_{ei},
\label{eq:nu-ie}
\end{equation}
with \(\bar\nu^M_{ei}\) given by equation~(\ref{eq:spitzer}). The ions take up the electron--ion part of the electron thermal force, so their thermal coefficient is the \(C_{ei}\) term of equation~(\ref{eq:bridge}),
\begin{equation}
C_i=\frac{C_{ei}\bar\nu^E_{ei}}{\bar\nu^E_{en}+\bar\nu^E_{ei}} .
\label{eq:Ci}
\end{equation}
The ion contribution \(\kappa_i\) to the heavy-particle thermal conductivity is calculated as in Almeida et al.\ \cite{Almeida2008}.


\subsection{Heavy species}

The atom frequencies follow from momentum conservation, equation~(\ref{eq:newton3}),
\begin{equation}
\bar\nu^M_{ni}=\frac{n_i}{n_n}\,\bar\nu^M_{in},\qquad
\bar\nu^M_{ne}=\frac{m_en_e}{Mn_n}\,\bar\nu^M_{en}.
\label{eq:nu-n}
\end{equation}
The atoms take up the electron--neutral part of the electron thermal force, so their thermal coefficient is the \(C_{en}\) term of equation~(\ref{eq:bridge}),
\begin{equation}
C_n=\frac{C_{en}\bar\nu^E_{en}}{\bar\nu^E_{en}+\bar\nu^E_{ei}} ,
\label{eq:Cn}
\end{equation}
and equations~(\ref{eq:Ci}) and (\ref{eq:Cn}) satisfy \(C_i+C_n=C_e\), as required by equation~(\ref{eq:newton3}). The heavy-particle thermal conductivity \(\kappa_h=\kappa_n+\kappa_i\), the sum of the atom and ion contributions, is calculated as in Almeida et al.\ \cite{Almeida2008}.

\subsection{Comparison with LTE transport properties}
\label{app:lte-check}

The electrical conductivity \(\sigma=n_ee^2/(m_e\bar\nu^M_e)\) and the thermal conductivity obtained with these coefficients are compared with the transport properties of argon in local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE) in figure~\ref{fig:lte-check}. The coefficients are evaluated with \(T_e=T_h=T\) and the Saha composition at atmospheric pressure, and compared with the data tabulated by Boulos et al.\ \cite{boulos2023handbook}. The reactive conductivity \(\kappa_R\) in our model resides in the ionization term and therefore for the comparison, the reactive conductivity computed with Mutation++ \cite{scoggins2020mutation} is added to \(\kappa_e+\kappa_h\).

The electrical conductivity agrees well with the LTE data over most of the temperature range, with only minor deviations at low temperatures, where electron--neutral collisions dominate. At intermediate temperatures, however, the additive rule for the collision frequencies overestimates the conductivity. In argon, the slow electrons near the Ramsauer minimum carry a substantial part of the current in the electron--neutral limit, but these same electrons are strongly scattered by the ions, and adding the averaged electron--neutral and electron--ion collision frequencies does not capture this correlation between the two collision processes. The thermal conductivity also agrees well with the reference data at high temperatures, and is moderately overpredicted at intermediate temperatures.
